The True Renunciation: Understanding Bhagavad Gita 2.71

This verse teaches the essence of true renunciation and detachment from desires, guiding individuals toward inner peace and stability.

May 14, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Sankhya Yoga #Renunciation #Karma Yoga #Non-attachment
2.71

Chapter 2: Sankhya Yoga

Verse 71

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

विहाय कमाण्यसर्वाणि मनोऽन्यस्यति सन्निष्कामः। असक्तोऽन्यस्यति सन्निष्कामः॥ 71॥

Transliteration (IAST)

vihāya karmāṇy asarvāṇi mano 'nyasya ti sanniṣkāmaḥ, asakto 'nyasya ti sanniṣkāmaḥ.

Translation

One who is unattached to the external world and has renounced all actions, yet remains engaged in activities, is truly renounced.

Explanation

Context

This verse appears in Chapter 2 of the Bhagavad Gita, known as Sankhya Yoga, where Lord Krishna imparts wisdom to Arjuna, who is confused and reluctant to engage in battle. The teachings in this chapter focus on understanding the nature of the self, the process of yoga, and the importance of performing one’s duty without attachment to the results. Verse 2.71 emphasizes the state of true renunciation, distinguishing mere withdrawal from action from the deeper understanding of non-attachment.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • विहाय (vihāya): renouncing or giving up.
  • कमाण्यसर्वाणि (karmāṇy asarvāṇi): all actions.
  • मनः (manaḥ): mind.
  • अन्यस्यति (anyasya ti): is attached to another (external).
  • सन्निष्कामः (sanniṣkāmaḥ): one who is without desire or attachment.
  • असक्तः (asakto): unattached or free from attachment.

These terms together convey the essence of renouncing desires and actions while remaining engaged in the world, highlighting a nuanced understanding of detachment.

Deeper Understanding

In this verse, Krishna teaches Arjuna that true renunciation does not mean abandoning one’s responsibilities or duties. Rather, it involves performing one’s actions without attachment to the fruits they yield. This is a central theme in the Gita, where Krishna advocates for the practice of karma yoga, or selfless action.

The concept presented here is foundational to understanding dharma (righteous duty) and karma (action). By acting according to one’s duty without selfish desires, one can achieve a state of inner peace and stability, which is essential for spiritual growth. This idea resonates with the broader philosophical teachings of the Gita, emphasizing that one can attain liberation (moksha) while still engaging in the world, as long as one maintains a mindset of detachment.

Practical Application

  1. Work with Detachment: In professional settings, focus on performing your tasks to the best of your ability without becoming overly attached to the outcomes. For example, if you are working on a project, dedicate yourself fully but do not let your self-worth depend on the project’s success or failure.
  2. Cultivate Mindfulness: Engage in daily activities mindfully, such as eating or walking, appreciating the experience without letting your mind wander to past regrets or future anxieties. This practice helps in developing a sense of presence and reduces attachment to fleeting thoughts.
  3. Letting Go of Outcomes: In personal relationships, approach interactions with love and kindness without clinging to expectations. For instance, support a friend in need without expecting anything in return; this fosters genuine connections and reduces emotional turmoil.

Reflection

  • What actions in your life are you overly attached to? How can you shift your perspective?
  • Reflect on a time when you acted without attachment. How did it feel?
  • Consider the balance between fulfilling your duties and letting go of the need for recognition or reward.

Key Concepts

  • Renunciation (Vihaya): The act of letting go of attachment to actions.
  • Non-attachment (Asakti): Engaging in the world without being emotionally tied to outcomes.
  • Karma Yoga: The path of selfless action as a means to achieve spiritual liberation.
  • Dharma: Fulfilling one’s duty while maintaining a sense of detachment from results.
