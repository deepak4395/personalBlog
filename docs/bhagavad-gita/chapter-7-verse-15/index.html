Obstacles to Divine Surrender: The Deluded and Demonic

This verse identifies four types of misguided individuals—the foolish, the lowest among men, those whose knowledge is stolen by illusion, and those with demonic nature—who fail to surrender to the Divine.

November 6, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Jnana Vijnana Yoga
7.15

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 15

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

न मां दुष्कृतिनो मूढाः प्रपद्यन्ते नराधमाः। माययापहृतज्ञाना आसुरं भावमाश्रिताः॥ १५॥

Transliteration (IAST)

na māṁ duṣkṛtino mūḍhāḥ prapadyante narādhamāḥ | māyayāpahṛtajñānā āsuram bhāvam āśritāḥ || 15 ||

Translation

The foolish, the lowest among men, those whose knowledge is stolen by illusion, and those who partake of the demonic nature, do not surrender unto Me.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, known as Jnana Vijnana Yoga, focuses on Lord Krishna revealing His supreme nature as the ultimate reality, the source and sustainer of all existence. After describing the profound difficulty of transcending His divine illusion (Maya) in verse 7.14, Krishna now elaborates on who precisely fails to overcome this Maya and, consequently, does not surrender to Him. This verse acts as a counterpoint to the later descriptions of the four types of pious devotees who do worship Him (7.16), painting a clear picture of the spiritual barriers that prevent genuine devotion and surrender. It sets the stage for understanding the qualities that lead away from the divine path.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • न (na): Not
  • माम् (mām): To Me (referring to Lord Krishna, the Supreme Being)
  • दुष्कृतिनः (duṣkṛtinaḥ): Evil-doers; those who perform unrighteous or sinful actions. This implies actions that lead one away from truth and spiritual growth.
  • मूढाः (mūḍhāḥ): Foolish; deluded, ignorant, lacking spiritual discernment.
  • प्रपद्यन्ते (prapadyante): Surrender; take refuge in, approach.
  • नराधमाः (narādhamāḥ): The lowest among men. This is a strong term (nara = man, adhama = lowest) implying those who, despite being born as humans with the capacity for spiritual inquiry, squander this opportunity.
  • माययापहृतज्ञानाः (māyayāpahṛtajñānāḥ): Whose knowledge has been stolen or carried away by Maya (divine illusion). Their true understanding of self and reality is obscured by the material world’s allure.
  • आसुरं भावम् (āsuram bhāvam): Demonic nature or disposition. ‘Asura’ refers to qualities associated with materialistic, ego-driven, and often cruel tendencies.
  • आश्रिताः (āśritāḥ): Having taken refuge in; partaking of, resorting to.

Deeper Understanding

In this pivotal verse, Krishna identifies four categories of individuals who, due to their inherent qualities and actions, fail to recognize or surrender to the Divine. This is not a condemnation but an explanation of the spiritual blindness that prevents true prapatti (surrender).

  1. Duṣkṛtinaḥ (Evil-doers): These are not merely people who commit ‘bad’ acts, but those whose actions are fundamentally misaligned with dharma and spiritual evolution. Their deeds are driven by selfishness, greed, and attachment, further entangling them in the cycle of cause and effect, making it difficult to turn towards a higher purpose.

  2. Mūḍhāḥ (The Foolish): This refers to those who are spiritually ignorant. Despite potentially having worldly knowledge or intelligence, they lack the wisdom to discern between the temporary and the eternal, the real and the unreal. They remain deluded by the superficial aspects of existence, unable to grasp the deeper truths Krishna is revealing.

  3. Narādhamāḥ (Lowest Among Men): This is a powerful descriptor. Being born human is considered a rare and precious opportunity for spiritual advancement, as humans possess the unique capacity for self-reflection and discrimination. Those who, despite this gift, choose to live a life devoid of spiritual purpose, indulging solely in base desires and harming others, are deemed ‘lowest’ because they squander their highest potential.

  4. Māyayāpahṛtajñānāḥ (Those whose knowledge is stolen by Maya): This is a crucial category. Maya, Krishna’s divine illusionary energy, creates the apparent separation and duality of the material world. For these individuals, their inherent spiritual knowledge (ātma-jñāna) is covered by Maya, making them identify completely with their material body, mind, and worldly possessions. They cannot see beyond the veil of illusion to perceive the ultimate reality or their true spiritual identity.

  5. Āsuram Bhāvam Āśritāḥ (Those who partake of the demonic nature): This refers to individuals who cultivate qualities antithetical to spiritual growth. As described in detail in Chapter 16, demonic traits include arrogance, pride, anger, cruelty, insatiable desires, hypocrisy, and a denial of God or higher principles. Such individuals are inherently hostile to spiritual teachings and surrender, believing themselves to be the sole enjoyers and controllers.

This verse underscores that surrender to the Divine is not a passive act but requires a conscious overcoming of these internal obstacles—misguided actions, spiritual ignorance, squandered human potential, the illusion of Maya, and negative character traits. It highlights that the path to Krishna requires inner purification and a shift in perspective from the material to the spiritual.

Practical Application

  1. Self-Examination of Actions: Regularly reflect on your motivations for actions. Are they driven by self-interest, anger, or greed (duṣkṛtinaḥ), or by a sense of duty, compassion, and a desire for growth? Strive to align your actions with ethical principles (dharma) and selfless service, recognizing that every action has consequences that shape your spiritual path.
  2. Cultivate Spiritual Discernment (Viveka): Actively seek knowledge and wisdom that transcend superficial information. Question your assumptions about reality and happiness. Are you pursuing fleeting pleasures or lasting peace? Engage in practices like meditation, study of scriptures, and contemplation to pierce through the veil of illusion (māyayāpahṛtajñānāḥ) and gain a deeper understanding of your true self and the world.
  3. Address Negative Tendencies: Be honest about any ‘demonic’ traits (āsuram bhāvam) you might harbor, such as arrogance, envy, or a tendency to criticize without compassion. Work consciously to replace these with virtues like humility, empathy, kindness, and gratitude. This inner transformation is essential for opening oneself to divine grace and surrender.

Reflection

  • In what ways do I see elements of ‘foolishness’ or ‘demonic nature’ manifesting in my own thoughts, words, or actions?
  • How does Maya obscure my understanding of what is truly important in life?
  • What specific steps can I take today to move away from these described states and cultivate a more surrendered and spiritually aligned perspective?

Key Concepts

  • Obstacles to Surrender (Prapatti): Identifies specific barriers like ignorance, unrighteous actions, and demonic tendencies that prevent individuals from approaching the Divine.
  • The Power of Maya: Emphasizes how divine illusion can obscure true knowledge and create a false sense of reality, hindering spiritual progress.
  • Human Potential and Degradation: Contrasts the profound opportunity of human birth for spiritual growth with the state of ‘lowest among men’ who squander this potential.
  • Demonic Nature (Asura Bhava): Describes a mindset characterized by ego, materialism, hostility towards spiritual values, and a denial of higher truths.
