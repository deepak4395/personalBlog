The Unbreakable Bond: Seeing God Everywhere

This verse reveals the profound state of a yogi who perceives the Divine in all beings and all beings within the Divine, establishing an eternal connection.

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 30

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

यो मां पश्यति सर्वत्र सर्वं च मयि पश्यति। तस्याहं न प्रणश्यामि स च मे न प्रणश्यति।।

Transliteration (IAST)

yo māṁ paśyati sarvatra sarvaṁ ca mayi paśyati | tasyāhaṁ na praṇaśyāmi sa ca me na praṇaśyati ||

Translation

He who sees Me everywhere and sees everything in Me, for him I am never lost, nor is he ever lost to Me.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled “Atma Samyama Yoga” or “Dhyana Yoga,” delves deeply into the science of meditation and self-control. Krishna meticulously guides Arjuna through the practical steps of stilling the mind, achieving detachment, and cultivating equanimity. The preceding verses have laid the groundwork by explaining how a yogi perceives the Self (Atman) in all beings and all beings in the Self, thereby attaining a state of universal vision and impartiality. Verse 30 takes this profound understanding a significant step further. While verse 29 spoke of seeing the impersonal Self (Brahman) pervading all, verse 30 introduces a crucial element: the identification of this universal Self with Krishna, the Supreme Being. This transition bridges the impersonal (Nirguna) and personal (Saguna) aspects of the Divine, offering a complete spiritual vision where the ultimate reality is not just an abstract principle but a conscious, loving presence.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • यः (yaḥ): He who
  • माम् (mām): Me (referring to Krishna, the Supreme Self, the ultimate reality)
  • पश्यति (paśyati): sees, perceives, realizes
  • सर्वत्र (sarvatra): everywhere, in all places, in all beings
  • सर्वम् (sarvam): everything, all
  • च (ca): and
  • मयि (mayi): in Me
  • तस्य (tasya): for him, to him
  • अहम् (aham): I (Krishna)
  • न (na): never, not
  • प्रणश्यामि (praṇaśyāmi): perish, am lost, disappear
  • सः (saḥ): he (the yogi, the devotee)
  • मे (me): to Me
  • प्रणश्यति (praṇaśyati): perishes, is lost, disappears

Deeper Understanding

This shloka describes the ultimate state of realization achieved by a perfected yogi or devotee. It speaks of a profound, intuitive vision that transcends mere intellectual understanding. The first part, “He who sees Me everywhere,” signifies the realization of the omnipresence of the Divine. The yogi perceives the Supreme Being as the fundamental essence, the animating principle, and the underlying reality of every atom, every creature, every phenomenon in the cosmos. There is nothing separate from God; every form, every name, every experience is a manifestation of the one Supreme Consciousness.

The second part, “and sees everything in Me,” completes this holistic vision. It means that the entire universe, with all its myriad forms, energies, and beings, rests within the Divine. Just as waves arise from and subside into the ocean, all creation emerges from, is sustained by, and ultimately dissolves into the Supreme Being. This is a non-dual (Advaitic) understanding, where the distinction between the observer and the observed, the creator and the created, ultimately dissolves into a singular, all-encompassing reality.

Crucially, the “Me” here refers to Krishna as the ultimate, non-dual Brahman, the Paramatma (Supersoul), and Bhagavan (the personal God). This verse beautifully integrates the philosophical depth of Advaita Vedanta with the devotional fervor of Bhakti Yoga. It’s not just an impersonal realization but a deeply personal, relational one.

The profound consequence of this realization is expressed in the latter half of the verse: “for him I am never lost, nor is he ever lost to Me.” This signifies an eternal, unbreakable bond, a perpetual state of union (Yoga). The yogi, having merged their consciousness with the Divine, never loses sight of the Supreme Truth, and the Supreme Truth, in turn, never loses sight of the yogi. This is the essence of liberation (moksha), freedom from the cycle of birth and death, and a continuous experience of divine communion. It means the yogi lives in a state of constant awareness of the Divine, and the Divine reciprocates with unceasing grace and presence. This state transcends all duality, fear, and suffering, establishing the practitioner in an eternal abode of peace and bliss.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivating Universal Love and Compassion: By practicing seeing the divine spark in every individual, regardless of their background, beliefs, or actions, we can foster profound empathy and reduce judgment. When we recognize the same divine essence in a stranger, a friend, or even an adversary, our capacity for love and understanding expands, transforming our relationships and promoting harmony in society. For instance, instead of reacting with anger to a difficult person, try to acknowledge the underlying consciousness that connects you both.
  2. Mindfulness in Daily Activities: This teaching encourages us to find the sacred in the seemingly mundane. Whether washing dishes, working on a project, or walking in nature, we can consciously perceive the divine presence pervading every object, every action, and every moment. This transforms routine tasks into spiritual practices, making every aspect of life an opportunity for divine communion. For example, when eating, be mindful of the life force in the food and its connection to the universal energy.
  3. Overcoming Fear and Isolation: The realization that we are always held within the Supreme Being and are never truly separate can be incredibly comforting. In moments of loneliness, anxiety, or existential dread, remembering that “everything is in Me” and “I am never lost to Me” can provide a deep sense of security and belonging. This understanding dissolves the illusion of separation and fosters an inner peace that is not dependent on external circumstances.

Key Concepts

  • Omnipresence of the Divine: The realization that God is everywhere and in everything.
  • Unity of Existence (Advaita): The understanding that all of creation is interconnected and ultimately one with the Supreme Being.
  • Eternal Union (Yoga): The unbreakable, perpetual bond between the realized soul and the Divine.
  • Liberation (Moksha): The ultimate freedom from suffering and the cycle of rebirth, achieved through this profound realization.
