The Ultimate Remembrance: Attaining Krishna's State at Life's End

Krishna reveals that remembering Him at the moment of death ensures one attains His divine state, emphasizing the culmination of a life of devotion.

November 26, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Aksara Brahma Yoga
8.5

Chapter 8: Aksara Brahma Yoga

Verse 5

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

अन्तकाले च मामेव स्मरन्मुक्त्वा कलेवरम्। यः प्रयाति स मद्भावं याति नास्त्यत्र संशयः॥

Transliteration (IAST)

Anta-kāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram | Yaḥ prayāti sa mad-bhāvaṁ yāti nāsty atra saṁśayaḥ ||

Translation

And whoever, at the time of death, leaving the body, remembers Me alone, he attains My state; there is no doubt about this.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 8 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled “Aksara Brahma Yoga” (The Yoga of the Imperishable Brahman), delves into profound questions about the nature of reality, the divine, and the path to liberation. Arjuna, prompted by Krishna’s earlier statements, asks seven crucial questions at the beginning of this chapter (verses 1-2). These queries range from the definition of Brahman and Karma to the understanding of the divine principle within the body and, most importantly for this verse, how one can know the Supreme Lord at the time of death. Verse 5 is a direct and powerful answer to Arjuna’s question: “कथं च ज्ञेयोऽसि नियतात्मभिः” (8.2) – “how are You to be known by the self-controlled at the time of death?” Krishna here lays bare the ultimate secret to transcending the cycle of birth and death, emphasizing the paramount importance of one’s consciousness at the final moment of existence.

Word-by-Word Meaning

Let’s break down the profound simplicity of this verse:

  • अन्तकाले (anta-kāle): From anta (end) and kāla (time), meaning ‘at the time of death’ or ‘at the final moment’. This highlights the critical juncture of transition.
  • च (ca): And, also. A connective.
  • माम् एव (mām eva): ‘Me alone’. Mām refers to Krishna, the Supreme Being, the ultimate reality. Eva emphasizes exclusivity – ‘Me only’.
  • स्मरन् (smaran): Remembering, contemplating, meditating upon. This isn’t a passive thought but an active, deep engagement of the mind.
  • मुक्त्वा (muktvā): Leaving, abandoning, giving up. It signifies the act of the soul detaching from its physical casing.
  • कलेवरम् (kalevaram): The body, the physical form. The temporary vessel.
  • यः (yaḥ): Whoever, he who.
  • प्रयाति (prayāti): Departs, goes forth, proceeds. Refers to the soul’s journey after leaving the body.
  • सः (saḥ): He, that person.
  • मद्भावम् (mad-bhāvam): ‘My state’, ‘My nature’, ‘My being’. Mad (My) + bhāvam (state, nature, existence). This implies attaining the very essence, the divine consciousness, of the Supreme Lord.
  • याति (yāti): Attains, reaches, goes to.
  • न अस्ति अत्र संशयः (na asty atra saṁśayaḥ): ‘There is no doubt here’. Na (not) + asti (is) + atra (here) + saṁśayaḥ (doubt). This emphatic statement underscores the certainty of Krishna’s promise.

Deeper Understanding

This verse encapsulates a fundamental teaching of the Bhagavad Gita and indeed, Sanatana Dharma: the state of one’s consciousness at the moment of death determines their next destination. Krishna is not suggesting a last-minute trick to salvation, but rather emphasizing that the final thought is the culmination and reflection of one’s entire life’s dominant consciousness, desires, and spiritual practices.

What Krishna is teaching Arjuna is the profound truth that our inner world, our thoughts, and our intentions are the architects of our destiny. The smaran (remembrance) of Him at the final moment is not a sudden act but the natural outcome of a life lived in constant remembrance and devotion. If one has consistently integrated Krishna (or the divine principle) into their daily life, through contemplation, selfless action (karma yoga), knowledge (jnana yoga), or devotion (bhakti yoga), then the mind, by its very nature, will naturally gravitate towards that divine thought at the precipice of transition.

The mad-bhāvam (My state) signifies liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death (samsara). It implies attaining union with the Supreme, an end to suffering, and an experience of ultimate bliss and consciousness. This is not merely going to a heavenly realm but merging with the divine essence itself. This verse reinforces the idea that what we focus on, what we cultivate in our inner landscape throughout our lives, becomes our reality at the most crucial juncture.

Practical Application

This ancient wisdom holds immense relevance for modern life, even for those not explicitly following a spiritual path. It teaches us about the power of sustained focus and intention:

  1. Cultivating Present Moment Awareness: The verse encourages us to live mindfully. If our ‘final thought’ is a culmination of our life’s dominant thoughts, then practicing mindfulness and consciously choosing positive, uplifting, and purposeful thoughts daily builds a strong foundation. This could be through daily meditation, journaling, or simply taking moments to pause and connect with one’s higher purpose throughout the day.
  2. Integrating Purpose into Daily Life: For spiritual seekers, this means making the remembrance of the divine a part of daily routine – through chanting, prayer, selfless service, or seeing the divine in all beings. For others, it means aligning one’s actions with deeply held values and a sense of meaning. If our work, relationships, and leisure are all infused with a sense of higher purpose, then our consciousness naturally elevates.
  3. Preparing for Life’s Transitions: Death is the ultimate transition, but life is full of smaller ones – job changes, relationship shifts, health challenges. By consciously approaching these moments with equanimity, detachment, and a focus on our core values, we train our minds to handle the ultimate transition with grace. This practice helps us build the mental resilience and spiritual depth needed to face any ending with a sense of peace and purpose.

Reflection

  • What occupies the majority of your thoughts and energy on a daily basis?
  • How can you consciously infuse more spiritual awareness or a sense of higher purpose into your everyday activities?
  • Are you living a life that naturally cultivates the state of mind you wish to embody at all times, and especially at life’s end?

Key Concepts

  • Consciousness at Death (Anta-kāla-smṛti): The critical role of one’s final thoughts and awareness in determining the soul’s next destination or liberation.
  • Divine Remembrance (Smaranam): The practice of constant contemplation and remembrance of the Supreme Being, not as a fleeting thought but as a deeply ingrained state of mind.
  • Attainment of Divine State (Mad-bhāvam): The ultimate goal of spiritual practice, leading to union with the Supreme, liberation from rebirth, and experiencing divine consciousness.
  • The Power of Intention: The understanding that our sustained mental focus and intentions throughout life shape our ultimate destiny and the nature of our being.
