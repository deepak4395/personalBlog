The Vision of Oneness: Seeing the Self in All

Through the practice of Yoga, a harmonized soul perceives the Universal Self dwelling within all beings, and all beings residing within the Self, thus experiencing profound equanimity everywhere.

6.29

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 29

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

सर्वभूतस्थमात्मानं सर्वभूतानि चात्मनि। ईक्षते योगयुक्तात्मा सर्वत्र समदर्शनः।।

Transliteration (IAST)

Sarvabhūtasthamātmānaṁ sarvabhūtāni cātmani. Īkṣate yogayuktātmā sarvatra samadarśanaḥ.

Translation

The self, harmonized by Yoga, sees the Self abiding in all beings, and all beings in the Self; he sees the same everywhere.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled “Dhyana Yoga” or “Atma Samyama Yoga” (The Yoga of Self-Control), is dedicated to the profound practice of meditation and the cultivation of inner stillness. Lord Krishna has been meticulously guiding Arjuna through the intricacies of a yogi’s lifestyle, the disciplined control of the mind and senses, and the establishment of a steady meditative posture. He has described the mental state required for deep contemplation, where the mind is withdrawn from external distractions and focused singularly on the Self. This particular verse, 6.29, marks a pivotal point, describing the ultimate vision and realization that dawns upon a yogi who has successfully mastered these practices. It is the fruit of sustained effort, a profound shift in perception from duality to non-duality, where the individual consciousness merges with the universal.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • सर्वभूतस्थमात्मानं (Sarvabhūtasthamātmānam): The Self (Ātman) abiding (stham) in all (sarva) beings (bhūta). This refers to the immanent aspect of the Divine, the universal consciousness present in every living entity.
  • सर्वभूतानि (Sarvabhūtāni): All beings.
  • च (ca): And.
  • आत्मनि (ātmani): In the Self. This refers to the transcendent aspect, where all of creation is contained within the Universal Self.
  • ईक्षते (Īkṣate): Sees; perceives. This is not mere intellectual understanding but a direct, intuitive, and experiential vision.
  • योगयुक्तात्मा (Yogayuktātmā): One whose self (ātmā) is united (yukta) by Yoga. This signifies a mind that is harmonized, disciplined, and integrated through the practice of yoga.
  • सर्वत्र (Sarvatra): Everywhere.
  • समदर्शनः (samadarśanaḥ): One who sees equality; possessing an equal vision.

Deeper Understanding

This shloka encapsulates the very essence of Advaita Vedanta and the ultimate goal of yogic practice: the realization of oneness. Krishna teaches Arjuna that a yogi, whose mind has been purified and stabilized through meditation (yogayuktātmā), develops a unique and profound vision. They perceive the same single, universal Self (Ātman) dwelling within every single being, from the smallest insect to the most complex human. Simultaneously, they see all diverse beings and the entire cosmos as existing within that very same Universal Self. This is a profound shift from a fragmented, dualistic view of reality to an integrated, non-dualistic one.

This vision transcends all superficial distinctions of race, gender, species, social status, or even form. The yogi sees through the veil of maya (illusion) and recognizes the singular, unchanging consciousness that animates all existence. This state is called samadarśanaḥ – equal vision. It’s not about ignoring differences, but about seeing the underlying unity despite the apparent diversity. This realization naturally leads to boundless compassion, unconditional love, and an unwavering sense of peace, as the concept of ‘other’ dissolves. The yogi understands that harming another is, in essence, harming oneself, as there is no true separation.

This verse is deeply connected to the themes of Brahman (the ultimate reality) and Atman (the individual soul). It asserts the identity of Atman and Brahman, emphasizing that the individual Self is not separate from the Universal Self. It’s the culmination of the path of knowledge (Jnana Yoga) and devotion (Bhakti Yoga) when integrated with the discipline of action (Karma Yoga) and meditation (Dhyana Yoga). It moves beyond theoretical knowledge to direct experience.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivating Empathy and Compassion: In our daily lives, we often judge others based on their external appearance, actions, or beliefs. This verse encourages us to look beyond these superficialities and recognize the shared consciousness, the divine spark, within every individual. When faced with conflict or disagreement, consciously reminding ourselves, “The same Self that is within me is also within them,” can foster empathy and lead to more compassionate and understanding responses. This practice can transform relationships and reduce prejudice.
  2. Overcoming Self-Centeredness: The samadarśana vision naturally diminishes egoistic tendencies. When we truly perceive our interconnectedness, our actions become less about personal gain and more about the collective good. Before making a decision, one can ask: “How does this impact the greater whole, in which I am an inseparable part?” This perspective encourages selfless service and responsible citizenship, fostering a sense of universal responsibility.
  3. Mindful Interaction with Nature: Extending this vision beyond human beings to all forms of life and even the environment can lead to profound ecological awareness. If we see the same Self in animals, plants, rivers, and mountains, our attitude towards nature shifts from exploitation to reverence and stewardship. This helps in making conscious choices for sustainable living and protecting our planet.

Reflection

  • How can I consciously practice seeing the underlying unity in people and situations that challenge my perception of ‘otherness’?
  • What specific actions can I take today to embody samadarśana in my interactions?
  • How does the concept of my ‘Self’ being in all, and all being in my ‘Self,’ change my understanding of responsibility and connection?

Key Concepts

  • Oneness of Self (Advaita): The realization that the individual soul (Atman) is identical with the Universal Soul (Brahman), pervading all existence.
  • Equanimity (Samadarśana): The state of seeing all beings and phenomena with an equal eye, recognizing the same divine essence in everything.
  • Yoga’s Ultimate Vision: The highest spiritual perception attained through the disciplined practice of Yoga, leading to non-dualistic experience.
  • Universal Presence of Atman: The understanding that the Self is immanent in all beings and transcendent, encompassing all of creation within itself.
