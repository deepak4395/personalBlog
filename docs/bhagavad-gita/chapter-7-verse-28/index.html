Beyond Duality: The Path of Pure Devotion

This verse reveals that only those purified by virtuous deeds, free from the delusion of dualities, can worship the Divine with unwavering resolve.

November 19, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Jnana Vijnana Yoga
7.28

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 28

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

येषां त्वन्तगतं पापं जनानां पुण्यकर्मणाम् । ते द्वन्द्वमोहनिर्मुक्ता भजन्ते मां दृढव्रताः ॥ २८ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

yeṣāṁ tvantagataṁ pāpaṁ janānāṁ puṇyakarmaṇām | te dvandvamohanirmuktā bhajante māṁ dṛḍhavratāḥ || 28 ||

Translation

But those men of virtuous deeds, whose sins have come to an end, they, freed from the delusion of dualities, worship Me with firm resolve.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 7, “Jnana Vijnana Yoga,” delves into the nature of the Supreme Being, Lord Krishna, as the ultimate reality, both manifest and unmanifest. Krishna has been explaining how His divine maya (illusory energy) deludes most people, preventing them from recognizing Him. In the preceding verse (7.27), Krishna states that all beings are born into delusion, bewildered by the pairs of opposites (desire and aversion). This verse (7.28) then serves as a crucial clarification, identifying who can transcend this pervasive delusion and truly approach the Divine. It outlines the essential prerequisites for genuine, unwavering devotion, emphasizing purification and freedom from the captivating influence of dualities.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • येषां (yeṣāṁ): Of those
  • तु (tu): But, indeed
  • अन्तगतं (antagataṁ): Come to an end, completely destroyed
  • पापं (pāpaṁ): Sins, unrighteous actions, negative karmic reactions
  • जनानां (janānāṁ): Of men, of people
  • पुण्यकर्मणाम् (puṇyakarmaṇām): Of virtuous deeds, whose actions are righteous and meritorious
  • ते (te): They
  • द्वन्द्वमोहनिर्मुक्ता (dvandvamohanirmuktā): Freed from the delusion of dualities (dvandva - pairs of opposites; moha - delusion; nirmuktā - freed)
  • भजन्ते (bhajante): Worship, adore, serve with devotion
  • मां (māṁ): Me (Lord Krishna)
  • दृढव्रताः (dṛḍhavratāḥ): With firm resolve, steadfast vows, unwavering commitment

Deeper Understanding

In this profound verse, Krishna reveals the path to true devotion, which is not merely an emotional sentiment but a state of purified consciousness. He outlines a clear progression:

  1. Cessation of Sin (अन्तगतं पापं): The first and fundamental condition is the eradication of pāpa, or unrighteous actions and their karmic consequences. This doesn’t necessarily mean never having committed a wrong deed, but rather having reached a point where the accumulated negative karma has been exhausted or neutralized through sincere repentance, virtuous living, and divine grace. It implies a significant cleansing of the heart and mind, where the inclination towards unrighteousness diminishes.

  2. Virtuous Deeds (पुण्यकर्मणाम्): This purification is achieved through puṇyakarmaṇām, consistent performance of meritorious, righteous actions. These are actions performed without selfish attachment to results, driven by a sense of duty, compassion, and a desire to uphold dharma. Such actions purify the inner being, clear the mind of impurities, and create a fertile ground for spiritual understanding and devotion.

  3. Freedom from the Delusion of Dualities (द्वन्द्वमोहनिर्मुक्ता): Once the mind is purified by virtuous actions and freed from the burden of sin, individuals become liberated from dvandvamoha – the delusion caused by the pairs of opposites. This refers to the constant mental agitation arising from attachment to pleasure and aversion to pain, desire for gain and fear of loss, attraction to honor and repulsion from dishonor, etc. This delusion binds us to the material world and prevents us from seeing the underlying unity of existence. Freedom from this delusion brings equanimity, inner peace, and a clear perception of reality.

  4. Worship with Firm Resolve (भजन्ते मां दृढव्रताः): It is only these purified souls, who have transcended the fluctuating nature of dualities, who can truly worship Krishna with dṛḍhavratāḥ – unwavering commitment and steadfast vows. Their devotion is not superficial or temporary; it is deep-rooted, consistent, and unshakeable, because their minds are no longer swayed by external circumstances or internal desires. Their faith is firm, their resolve unyielding, and their focus entirely on the Divine. This links to the concept of sthita-prajña (one of steady wisdom) from Chapter 2, where equanimity is a hallmark of the enlightened.

This verse beautifully connects Karma Yoga (righteous action) with Jnana Yoga (understanding and transcending dualities) as a precursor to pure Bhakti Yoga (devotion). It emphasizes that spiritual progress is a journey of purification and transformation, leading to a state where genuine, unwavering devotion becomes possible.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivate Virtuous Habits: Consciously choose to engage in actions that align with ethical principles (truthfulness, non-violence, compassion, honesty). Even small acts of kindness, selfless service, or integrity contribute to the purification of the mind and heart. Regularly reflect on your actions and intentions.
  2. Practice Equanimity: Observe your reactions to life’s ups and downs. When faced with praise or criticism, success or failure, pleasure or pain, try to maintain a balanced perspective. Acknowledge the experience without getting overly attached or averse. For instance, if you receive a promotion, enjoy it, but don’t let it inflate your ego; if you face a setback, learn from it, but don’t let it define your self-worth.
  3. Identify and Challenge Dualities: Become aware of your strong likes and dislikes. What situations or people trigger strong positive or negative emotions in you? By understanding these attachments and aversions (which are the roots of dvandvamoha), you can gradually work towards detaching from their hold and cultivating a more neutral, accepting stance.

Reflection

  • What ‘sins’ or unrighteous patterns of thought/action do I need to address in my life?
  • How consistently do I engage in virtuous deeds, and what motivates them?
  • Am I often swayed by the pairs of opposites (e.g., chasing pleasure, avoiding pain)? How can I cultivate more inner stability?
  • What does ‘firm resolve’ mean for my own spiritual practice, and how can I strengthen it?

Key Concepts

  • Purification through Virtuous Action (Puṇyakarmaṇām): The prerequisite for spiritual advancement and true devotion is the cessation of negative karma and the consistent performance of righteous, selfless deeds.
  • Freedom from Dualities (Dvandvamoha-nirmuktā): Transcending the delusion caused by the pairs of opposites (pleasure/pain, gain/loss) leads to inner peace, equanimity, and clarity of perception.
  • Steadfast Devotion (Dṛḍhavratāḥ): Only a purified mind, free from the agitation of dualities, can offer unwavering, single-pointed devotion to the Divine.
  • Prerequisite for True Bhakti: This verse establishes that true devotion is not a casual act but arises from a profound inner transformation and purification.
