The Path to Liberation: Faith, Humility, and Freedom from Karma

This verse promises liberation from karmic bondage for those who follow Krishna's teachings with unwavering faith and a heart free from envy.

June 14, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Karma Yoga
3.31

Chapter 3: Karma Yoga

Verse 31

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

ये मे मतमिदं नित्यमनुतिष्ठन्ति मानवाः। श्रद्धावन्तोऽनसूयन्तो मुच्यन्ते तेऽपि कर्मभिः॥

Transliteration (IAST)

ye me matam idaṃ nityam anutiṣṭhanti mānavāḥ | śraddhāvanto 'nasūyanto mucyante te 'pi karmabhiḥ ||

Translation

Those human beings who constantly practice this teaching of Mine with faith and without envy, they too are liberated from the bondage of karma.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled ‘Karma Yoga,’ is dedicated to elucidating the path of action without attachment. Arjuna, initially confused about whether knowledge or action is superior, is being guided by Krishna to understand that right action, performed with a detached attitude and dedicated to the Divine, is a powerful means to spiritual liberation. Krishna has just explained in previous verses how one should perform actions, surrendering them to Him, freeing oneself from desire, attachment, and grief (Verse 30). This verse, 31, builds upon that foundation, offering a powerful promise to those who embrace and consistently apply His teachings. It sets the stage for the contrast in the following verse, which warns of the consequences of rejecting these divine instructions.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • ये (ye): those who
  • मे (me): My
  • मतम् (matam): teaching, doctrine, opinion, philosophy
  • इदम् (idam): this
  • नित्यम् (nityam): constantly, always, perpetually
  • अनुतिष्ठन्ति (anutiṣṭhanti): practice, follow, abide by, implement
  • मानवाः (mānavāḥ): human beings
  • श्रद्धावन्तः (śraddhāvantaḥ): endowed with faith, full of conviction, having reverence
  • अनसूयन्तः (anasūyantaḥ): without envy, without malice, without fault-finding, without critical attitude
  • मुच्यन्ते (mucyante): are liberated, are freed
  • ते (te): they
  • अपि (api): also, even
  • कर्मभिः (karmabhiḥ): from actions, from the bonds of karma

Deeper Understanding

In this pivotal verse, Krishna outlines the precise attitude required to benefit from His teachings and attain liberation. It’s not enough to merely hear the wisdom; one must practice it, and do so with a specific inner disposition.

What is Krishna teaching Arjuna? Krishna is emphasizing that His instructions on Karma Yoga – performing one’s duties without attachment to results, surrendering them to the Divine, and acting for the welfare of the world – are not theoretical concepts but practical guidelines for living. He is assuring Arjuna, and by extension all humanity, that following these principles leads to the highest spiritual goal.

Key Concepts Presented:

  1. Constant Practice (nityam anutiṣṭhanti): Spiritual growth is not a one-time event or a sporadic effort. It requires continuous application of principles in daily life. Just as a plant needs constant nurturing to grow, spiritual wisdom must be consistently cultivated and integrated into one’s actions, thoughts, and words. This implies discipline and perseverance.
  2. Faith (śraddhāvantaḥ): Shraddha is a profound concept in Indian philosophy. It’s not blind belief but a deep conviction, trust, and sincere inclination towards the truth of the teachings and the integrity of the teacher. It’s the inner receptivity that allows wisdom to penetrate and transform. Without shraddha, even the most profound teachings remain external and ineffective. It’s the conviction that this path is indeed beneficial and leads to the promised results.
  3. Without Envy/Criticism (anasūyantaḥ): This is a crucial and often overlooked condition. Asūyā (envy or malice) refers to a critical, fault-finding attitude, often stemming from ego, jealousy, or intellectual arrogance. When one approaches teachings or a teacher with asūyā, one is unable to genuinely receive the wisdom. The mind is closed, preoccupied with finding flaws rather than absorbing truth. Krishna states that freedom from this critical, envious spirit is essential for the teachings to take root and bear fruit. It implies humility, an open mind, and a willingness to learn without prejudice.

Relation to Gita Themes: This verse directly relates to Karma Yoga by defining the mental framework for effective action. It connects to dharma (righteous conduct) by emphasizing the importance of following divine law with the right attitude. Ultimately, it points towards moksha (liberation) by promising freedom from karma-bandha (the bondage of actions). Actions performed with shraddha and anasūyā, as an offering to the Divine, cease to generate binding karmic reactions, thus paving the way for spiritual freedom.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivate an Open Mind and Trust: When encountering new wisdom, especially spiritual, approach it with an attitude of receptive faith rather than immediate skepticism or cynicism. Give the teachings a fair chance to demonstrate their value through your own practice. For example, if you decide to try mindfulness meditation, approach it with the faith that consistent practice will yield benefits, rather than dismissing it after a few attempts.
  2. Practice Non-Envy and Humility: Observe your reactions when others succeed or when you encounter ideas that challenge your preconceived notions. Instead of criticizing or feeling envious, try to understand and learn. If a spiritual teacher or practice seems to be helping others, instead of dismissing it with a critical mind, consider what you might gain from an open exploration. This helps in fostering a positive internal environment for learning and growth.
  3. Consistent Application in Daily Life: Identify one or two principles from the Gita (e.g., performing duty without attachment to results, seeing the divine in all beings) and consciously try to apply them throughout your day. Whether it’s doing household chores, performing professional duties, or interacting with family, make a conscious effort to act with detachment and dedication. This consistent practice, even in small ways, builds spiritual muscle and transforms mundane activities into sacred acts.

Reflection

  • How does my current approach to challenges and new ideas reflect faith or skepticism?
  • Where do I find myself expressing envy or a critical attitude, and how can I consciously shift this perspective towards openness and understanding?
  • Am I consistently applying spiritual wisdom in my daily life, or only when it feels convenient or necessary?

Key Concepts

  • Karma Yoga (Action in Wisdom): The path of performing duties without attachment to results, as an offering to the Divine.
  • Śraddhā (Faith/Conviction): A deep inner trust, conviction, and reverence that enables one to truly receive and implement spiritual teachings.
  • Anasūyā (Non-envy/Humility): Freedom from a critical, fault-finding, or envious attitude, fostering an open mind receptive to wisdom.
  • Karma-bandha (Bondage of Karma): The cycle of action and reaction that binds the soul to repeated existences; from which this verse promises liberation.
