Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 1

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

श्रीभगवानुवाच मय्यासक्तमनाः पार्थ योगं युञ्जन्मदाश्रयः। असंशयं समग्रं मां यथा ज्ञास्यसि तच्छृणु॥

Transliteration (IAST)

śrībhagavānuvāca mayyāsaktamanāḥ pārtha yogaṁ yuñjanmadāśrayaḥ। asaṁśayaṁ samagraṁ māṁ yathā jñāsyasi tacchṛṇu॥

Translation

The Supreme Lord said: O son of Pritha, hear now how you can know Me completely, without any doubt, by practicing yoga with your mind attached to Me and taking refuge in Me.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita concluded with a powerful declaration by Krishna, stating that among all yogis, the one who constantly remembers Him with faith and worships Him is considered the most superior. Chapter 7, titled “Jnana Vijnana Yoga” (The Yoga of Knowledge and Realized Knowledge), begins with this pivotal verse. Krishna, the Supreme Lord, directly addresses Arjuna, inviting him to listen intently as He promises to reveal the full and complete knowledge of His divine nature. This verse marks a significant transition, moving from the more introspective and meditative forms of yoga described in previous chapters to a path where devotion (bhakti) and complete reliance on the Divine are presented as essential for attaining a comprehensive and doubt-free understanding of the Absolute Truth. It sets the stage for Krishna to unveil His two primary energies – the material and the spiritual – and how He pervades and sustains the entire cosmos.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • śrībhagavānuvāca (श्रीभगवानुवाच): The Supreme Lord said. This opening emphasizes the divine origin and authoritative nature of the profound teachings that are about to follow.
  • mayyāsaktamanāḥ (मय्यासक्तमनाः): With mind attached to Me. This is a crucial compound word: mayi (in Me), āsakta (attached), manāḥ (mind). It signifies a state of continuous, loving, and focused mental engagement with the Divine, where one’s thoughts are consistently directed towards Krishna.
  • pārtha (पार्थ): O son of Pritha (Arjuna). This affectionate address highlights the intimate and confidential relationship between Krishna and Arjuna, indicating that a profound teaching is being shared.
  • yogaṁ yuñjan (योगं युञ्जन्): Practicing yoga, engaging in spiritual union. Yogaṁ yuñjan means to be engaged in the practice of yoga, whether it be meditation, selfless action, or devotional service, all aimed at connecting with the divine.
  • madāśrayaḥ (मदाश्रयः): Taking refuge in Me. This term is composed of mat (My) and āśrayaḥ (refuge, shelter, support). It implies performing all spiritual disciplines and daily duties with the understanding that Krishna is the ultimate source of support, protection, and the ultimate goal of one’s existence.
  • asaṁśayaṁ (असंशयं): Without doubt. Krishna assures that the knowledge gained through this prescribed method will be clear, firm, and entirely free from any uncertainty or skepticism.
  • samagraṁ (समग्रं): Completely, in totality, integrally. This is a key word, indicating that the knowledge imparted will not be fragmented or partial, but a full, experiential, and holistic realization of the Divine in all His aspects and energies.
  • māṁ (मां): Me. Referring specifically to Bhagavan Krishna, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, in His complete manifestation.
  • yathā jñāsyasi (यथा ज्ञास्यसि): How you shall know. Yathā (how), jñāsyasi (you will know). Krishna promises to reveal the precise method and process for attaining this complete knowledge.
  • tacchṛṇu (तच्छृणु): Hear that. An imperative command, urging Arjuna to give his full attention and receptivity to the upcoming discourse.

Deeper Understanding

This opening verse of Chapter 7 is a profound invitation to a higher dimension of spiritual realization. Krishna is not merely offering philosophical concepts; He is presenting a direct and assured path to know Him—the Supreme Being—in His entirety. The bedrock of this path lies in two essential prerequisites: mayyāsaktamanāḥ (a mind deeply attached to Him) and madāśrayaḥ (a spirit of complete surrender and taking refuge in Him).

This emphasis distinguishes the path Krishna is about to describe from purely intellectual pursuits or austere ascetic practices. While jnana (knowledge) and vijnana (realized knowledge) are central to the chapter, Krishna clearly states that true, complete knowledge of the Absolute (samagraṁ māṁ) is attainable only when the heart is fully engaged through devotion and surrender. It implies that mere intellectual understanding of spiritual truths is insufficient; one must experientially realize them by aligning one’s consciousness, emotions, and will with the Divine.

The phrase yogaṁ yuñjan suggests engaging in any form of spiritual practice, but now with the added, transformative dimension of madāśrayaḥ. This means that whether one practices karma yoga (action in devotion), jnana yoga (the path of knowledge), or dhyana yoga (meditative absorption), when these are performed with devotion and a sense of reliance on Krishna, they become potent pathways to supreme knowledge. This elevates spiritual practice from mere self-effort to a divinely guided journey, infused with grace.

Knowing Krishna samagraṁ means understanding Him in His totality – not just as the impersonal Brahman or the indwelling Paramatma, but as Bhagavan, the Supreme Personality of Godhead. It encompasses recognizing His transcendental qualities, His energies (both material and spiritual), His divine pastimes, and His supreme position as the origin, sustainer, and dissolver of all existence. This comprehensive knowledge is what removes all saṁśayaṁ (doubts), leading to an unwavering, firm conviction. This verse, therefore, lays the crucial foundation for understanding Krishna’s nature as both immanent (pervading everything) and transcendent (beyond everything), setting the stage for His subsequent revelations about His material and spiritual energies and His omnipresence in the cosmos.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivating Divine Attachment in Daily Life: Integrate mayyāsaktamanāḥ by performing all daily activities with an awareness of a higher purpose or divine connection. For instance, when working, a professional can dedicate their efforts to contributing positively to society, seeing their work as an offering to a higher principle. A student can approach their studies with sincerity, viewing the acquisition of knowledge as a means to understand the world and its divine source better. This transforms mundane tasks into spiritual practices.
  2. Practicing Surrender and Trust (madāśrayaḥ): In moments of challenge, uncertainty, or decision-making, consciously take refuge in the Divine. This doesn’t imply inaction, but rather performing one’s duties to the best of one’s ability while surrendering the outcome to a higher power. For example, when faced with a difficult personal or professional situation, one can do their part diligently and then release attachment to a specific result, trusting that the divine plan will unfold for the highest good, thereby finding peace in surrender.
  3. Holistic Spiritual Practice: This verse encourages a balanced and integrated approach to spiritual growth. Whether one engages in meditation, selfless service, devotional chanting, or scriptural study, the key is to perform these practices with a focused mind (attachment) and a profound sense of taking refuge in the Divine. The goal is not just intellectual understanding, but a complete, experiential knowledge (samagraṁ jñāna) that transforms one’s entire being.

Reflection

  • In what areas of my life do I genuinely take refuge in a higher power, and where do I still rely solely on my limited self-effort and desire for control?
  • Am I seeking fragmented knowledge or a complete, doubt-free understanding of spiritual reality? What steps can I take to move towards samagraṁ jñāna?

Key Concepts

  • Bhakti Yoga (Devotional Path): The emphasis on mayyāsaktamanāḥ (mind attached to Me) and madāśrayaḥ (taking refuge in Me) clearly establishes the path of devotion as the primary means to attain supreme and complete divine knowledge.
  • Saranaagati (Surrender/Refuge): The concept of madāśrayaḥ is central, highlighting the profound importance of surrendering to and relying on the Divine as the ultimate shelter, guide, and source of all support.
  • Samagra Jnana (Complete Knowledge): Krishna promises to reveal how one can know Him samagraṁ (completely, in totality), indicating a holistic, experiential, and doubt-free understanding of the Absolute Truth in all its manifestations.
  • Mind Attachment (Mayyāsaktamanāḥ): This term underscores the active and crucial role of the mind in spiritual realization, emphasizing that focused, loving, and continuous engagement with the Divine is a prerequisite for profound and transformative insight.
