Bhagavad Gita 7.6: The Divine Origin of All Existence

Krishna declares Himself the ultimate source and dissolution of the entire universe, with all beings originating from His two divine natures.

7.6

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 6

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

एतद्योनीनि भूतानि सर्वाणीत्युपधारय । अहं कृत्स्नस्य जगतः प्रभवः प्रलयस्तथा ॥६॥

Transliteration (IAST)

etadyonīni bhūtāni sarvāṇītyupadhāraya | ahaṁ kṛtsnasyajagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayastathā ||6||

Translation

Understand that all beings have their origin in these two natures (Para and Apara Prakriti). I am the origin and the dissolution of the entire universe.

Explanation

Context

Verse 6 of the Seventh Chapter, ‘Jnana Vijnana Yoga’ (The Yoga of Knowledge and Realization), is a pivotal declaration by Lord Krishna. In the preceding verses (7.4 and 7.5), Krishna meticulously introduced His two primary natures, or Prakritis. Verse 7.4 detailed the eight components of His Apara Prakriti (inferior nature): earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intellect, and ego – essentially the material constituents of the universe. Verse 7.5 then revealed His Para Prakriti (superior nature): the life-principle, the conscious energy that sustains the entire cosmos and animates all living beings.

With this verse, Krishna synthesizes these two concepts, stating that all beings arise from this dual nature. More profoundly, He then transcends even these two Prakritis, declaring Himself as the ultimate source (prabhava) and dissolution (pralaya) of the entire universe. This firmly establishes His supreme position, not merely as a creator, but as the fundamental reality from which everything emerges and into which everything ultimately returns. This revelation is crucial for Arjuna, and for all seekers, to grasp the true nature of the Divine and the interconnectedness of all existence.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • एतद्योनीनि (etadyonīni): Having these as their origin or source. ‘Etad’ refers to ‘these two’ (Para and Apara Prakriti), and ‘yonīni’ means ‘having their womb’ or ‘source’.
  • भूतानि (bhūtāni): Beings, entities, creations, living organisms.
  • सर्वाणि (sarvāṇi): All.
  • इति उपधारय (iti upadhāraya): Thus understand, know this for certain, be convinced of this. ‘Iti’ means ‘thus’ or ‘in this way’, and ‘upadhāraya’ is an imperative verb meaning ‘understand’ or ‘know’.
  • अहं (ahaṁ): I (referring to Lord Krishna).
  • कृत्स्नस्य (kṛtsnasya): Of the entire, of the whole, complete.
  • जगतः (jagataḥ): Of the universe, of the world, of existence.
  • प्रभवः (prabhavaḥ): The origin, the source, the creation.
  • प्रलयः (pralayaḥ): The dissolution, the end, the destruction, the reabsorption.
  • तथा (tathā): Also, similarly, as well.

Deeper Understanding

This verse offers a profound teaching on the nature of reality and the Divine. Firstly, it clarifies the intricate mechanism of creation: all beings are a combination of Krishna’s superior (conscious, life-giving) and inferior (material, inert) natures. A human being, for example, is not merely a collection of physical elements and mental processes (Apara Prakriti) but is animated and sustained by the conscious life-force (Para Prakriti). This dualism, however, is not a dichotomy but a unified expression of the Divine.

Secondly, and more significantly, Krishna asserts His ultimate supremacy. By stating, “Ahaṁ kṛtsnasyajagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayastathā” (I am the origin and the dissolution of the entire universe), He positions Himself as the substratum from which even the Para and Apara Prakritis emerge. He is not just the sum of His parts; He is the transcendent reality that encompasses and underlies all existence. This elevates Krishna from merely a ‘creator god’ to the absolute, all-pervading Brahman, the one ultimate reality that is both immanent (within everything) and transcendent (beyond everything).

This teaching is central to Jnana Vijnana Yoga because it moves beyond theoretical knowledge (jnana) to direct realization (vijnana). To truly grasp that the entire cosmos, with all its diverse forms, arises from and dissolves into the Divine, and that Krishna is that Divine, is to attain a profound spiritual insight. It connects to the overarching themes of the Gita by emphasizing the unity of existence, the sacredness inherent in all life, and the ultimate goal of understanding and aligning oneself with this supreme reality. This realization can inspire both selfless action (Karma Yoga) and unwavering devotion (Bhakti Yoga), as one recognizes the Divine in every aspect of life.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivating Reverence and Empathy: Understanding that all beings, from the smallest insect to the largest galaxy, have their origin in the Divine can foster a deep sense of reverence for life and the natural world. This perspective encourages empathy, as we recognize the same divine spark (Para Prakriti) within ourselves and in every other living being. Practically, this can translate into more compassionate interactions, ethical consumption, and active participation in environmental stewardship, viewing nature not as a resource to exploit but as a manifestation of the sacred.

  2. Transcending Duality and Embracing Change: The verse states Krishna is both the origin (prabhava) and the dissolution (pralaya). This teaches us to embrace the natural cycles of creation, maintenance, and destruction in our own lives and in the world. Rather than fearing change, loss, or endings, we can view them as integral parts of a larger divine process. This perspective can bring equanimity in times of personal challenge, job loss, or bereavement, recognizing that dissolution is merely a prelude to new forms of creation.

  3. Mindfulness of the Divine Presence: By regularly contemplating that the entire universe, including our own physical body and consciousness, originates from and is sustained by the Divine, we can cultivate a continuous awareness of God’s presence. This isn’t just an abstract concept; it’s a living reality. In daily activities, whether working, eating, or resting, one can pause to acknowledge the divine energy animating every breath and thought, transforming mundane tasks into acts of mindfulness and spiritual connection.

  • In what ways can I better accept the cycles of creation and dissolution, both externally and in my personal life?
  • How can the understanding that Krishna is the ultimate source of everything deepen my sense of purpose and connection?

Key Concepts

  • Divine Origin of All Beings: All living entities are a combination of Krishna’s superior (conscious) and inferior (material) natures.
  • Krishna as Ultimate Cause: Krishna is the absolute source, sustainer, and dissolver of the entire universe, transcending even His own Prakritis.
  • Unity of Existence: The verse emphasizes a monistic reality where everything emanates from and returns to the one Divine source.
  • Cyclical Nature of Reality: Creation and dissolution are eternal, intertwined processes, both expressions of the Divine will.
