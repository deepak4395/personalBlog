The Witness: Observing Actions of the Senses and Body (Bhagavad Gita 5.9)

This verse enumerates daily actions, highlighting how the enlightened individual perceives them as functions of the senses and body, not the true Self.

Chapter 5: Karma Sanyasa Yoga

Verse 9

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

पश्यञ्छृण्वन्स्पृशञ्जिघ्रन्नश्नन्गच्छन्स्वपन्श्वसन् । प्रलपन्विसृजन्गृह्णन्नुन्मिषन्निमिषन्नपि ॥ ५-९ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

paśyañ śṛṇvan spṛśañ jighrann aśnan gacchan svapan śvasan | pralapan visṛjan gṛhṇann unmiṣan nimiṣann api || 5-9 ||

Translation

While seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing; speaking, releasing, grasping, opening and closing the eyes—

Explanation

Context

Chapter 5 of the Bhagavad Gita, ‘Karma Sanyasa Yoga,’ is dedicated to resolving the apparent contradiction between Sankhya Yoga (the path of knowledge and renunciation of action) and Karma Yoga (the path of selfless action). Lord Krishna clarifies that true renunciation is not the cessation of all activity, but rather the renunciation of the sense of doer-ship. This chapter aims to show how both paths ultimately lead to the same goal: liberation from suffering and union with the Divine.

Verse 9 is a direct continuation of Verse 8. In Verse 8, Krishna states that a ‘yukta’ (one united with the Self) or ‘tattvavit’ (knower of truth) should think, “I do nothing at all.” Verse 9 then provides a comprehensive list of actions that such an enlightened individual observes happening, rather than feels themselves to be doing. It’s an enumeration of the vast spectrum of physiological, sensory, and motor functions that are performed by the body and senses, but not by the true Self.

Word-by-Word Meaning

Let’s break down the continuous stream of participles that describe various actions:

  • पाश्यन् (paśyañ): seeing
  • शृण्वन् (śṛṇvan): hearing
  • स्पृशन् (spṛśañ): touching
  • जिघ्रन् (jighrann): smelling
  • अश्नन् (aśnan): eating
  • गच्छन् (gacchan): walking
  • स्वपन् (svapan): sleeping
  • श्वसन् (śvasan): breathing
  • प्रलपन् (pralapan): speaking, talking
  • विसृजन् (visṛjan): releasing, excreting (e.g., waste products)
  • गृह्णन् (gṛhṇann): grasping, taking, receiving
  • उन्मिषन् (unmiṣan): opening the eyes
  • निमिषन् (nimiṣann): closing the eyes
  • अपि (api): even, also (emphasizing that even these involuntary or subtle actions are included).

Together, these words paint a picture of continuous, multifaceted activity, ranging from conscious sensory input to vital autonomic functions and voluntary motor actions.

Deeper Understanding

This verse is a profound teaching on the nature of the Self and its relationship to the material world. Krishna is teaching Arjuna (and us) about:

  • The Witness Consciousness (Sakshi Bhava): The core message is that the true Self (Atman) is a pure, unchanging consciousness, a silent witness to the activities of the body, mind, and senses. It does not actively ‘do’ any of these actions. Instead, it observes them as phenomena occurring within its field of awareness.

  • Non-Doership (Akartritva): This is a central tenet of both Sankhya and Karma Yoga. The actions listed are performed by the Prakriti (material nature)—specifically, by the body, senses, and mind, which are products of the three gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas). The Atman, or Purusha, is transcendent and therefore not the doer. Understanding this frees one from the egoic sense of ‘I am doing this,’ which is the root of attachment to results and subsequent karmic bondage.

  • Disidentification: The verse encourages us to disidentify with the temporary, ever-changing psycho-physical complex. By observing actions as external processes, we begin to detach from the false notion that ‘I am this body,’ ‘I am this mind,’ or ‘I am the one who experiences pleasure and pain from these actions.’ This disidentification is the path to inner freedom.

  • Holistic Perspective: The extensive list of actions—from seeing and hearing to breathing and even blinking—demonstrates that all activities, whether voluntary or involuntary, gross or subtle, are part of the play of Prakriti. No action is excluded from this principle of non-doership of the Self.

This verse relates directly to Dharma by guiding us to perform our duties (dharma) with an understanding of our true nature. It relates to Karma Yoga by emphasizing action without attachment, as the ‘doer’ is not the Self. It reinforces Sankhya’s discrimination between Purusha and Prakriti, showing how the Purusha remains untainted by the workings of Prakriti.

Practical Application

  1. Mindful Observation: Practice observing your daily activities without immediate identification. When you eat, simply notice the sensation of taste and the act of chewing, rather than thinking ‘I am eating.’ When you walk, feel your feet on the ground and the movement of your legs, recognizing these as functions of the body. This practice cultivates a sense of detachment and enhances mindfulness.
  2. Reducing Emotional Reactivity: By understanding that thoughts and emotions are also phenomena arising in the mind (part of Prakriti), one can observe them without being swept away. Instead of saying ‘I am angry,’ you can shift to ‘Anger is arising within me.’ This creates a crucial space between you (the witness) and the emotion, allowing for a more measured and conscious response.
  3. Cultivating Inner Peace Amidst Activity: In a busy world, we often feel overwhelmed by the sheer volume of tasks. Applying the principle of non-doership means performing actions diligently, but without the burden of egoic attachment to outcomes. Recognizing that ‘the senses are interacting with their objects’ (as stated in 5.8) allows for a deep sense of peace, knowing that the core Self remains untouched by the hustle and bustle.

Reflection

  • How deeply do I identify with my actions and their results? What emotional burdens does this identification create?
  • Can I observe my body and mind performing actions, even subtle ones like breathing or thinking, as if I were a separate, silent observer?
  • What would it mean to truly live with the understanding that ‘I do nothing at all,’ even while fully engaged in life?

Key Concepts

  1. Witness Consciousness (Sakshi Bhava): The Self as the pure, unattached observer of all activities.
  2. Non-Doership (Akartritva): The understanding that the true Self is not the agent of action; actions are performed by the gunas of Prakriti.
  3. Disidentification: The process of separating one’s sense of self from the body, mind, and senses.
  4. Discrimination (Viveka): The ability to distinguish between the eternal Self and the temporary, material nature (Prakriti).
