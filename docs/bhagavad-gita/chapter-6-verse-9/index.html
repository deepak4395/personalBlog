The Unity of the Self: Bhagavad Gita Chapter 6, Verse 9

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 9

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

सर्वभूतस्थमात्मानं सर्वभूतानि चात्मनि। एकं सत्त्वं न पश्यन्ति यस्तु दृष्टिः सदा समः॥

Transliteration (IAST)

sarvabhūtasthamātmānaṃ sarvabhūtāni cātmani | ekaṃ sattvaṃ na paśyanti yastu dṛṣṭiḥ sadā samaḥ ||

Translation

He who sees the self in all beings and all beings in the self is never separated from the self.

Explanation

Context

This verse is part of Chapter 6, Atma Samyama Yoga, which discusses the importance of self-discipline and the path of meditation. In this chapter, Lord Krishna is imparting wisdom to Arjuna about the practice of yoga and the significance of controlling the mind. The Gita emphasizes that true yoga is not merely physical postures but a profound understanding of the self and the universe.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • सर्वभूतस्थम् (sarvabhūtastham): The essence of all beings; indicates the universal consciousness that pervades everything.
  • आत्मानम् (ātmānaṃ): The self; refers to the true nature of an individual beyond the physical form.
  • सर्वभूतानि (sarvabhūtāni): All beings; encompasses every living entity in the universe.
  • च (ca): And; a conjunction that connects two ideas.
  • आत्मनि (ātmani): In the self; signifies the realization of the self within the broader context of existence.
  • एकम् (ekaṃ): One; emphasizes the unity and interconnectedness of all things.
  • सत्त्वम् (sattvaṃ): Existence or essence; reflects the fundamental reality.
  • न (na): Not; indicates negation.
  • पश्यन्ति (paśyanti): See; refers to perception or understanding.
  • यस्तु (yastu): He who; specifies a particular type of person.
  • दृष्टिः (dṛṣṭiḥ): Vision; the perspective or outlook one holds.
  • सदा (sadā): Always; denoting continuity.
  • समः (samaḥ): Equanimous or equal; refers to a balanced state of mind.

Deeper Understanding

In this verse, Krishna is addressing the profound concept of unity and interconnectedness. He teaches Arjuna that a true yogi sees the self in all beings and recognizes that all beings exist within the self. This perspective is crucial for realizing the non-dual nature of reality where individual identities dissolve into a greater, unified consciousness.

The key concepts include:

  • Unity of Existence: This verse emphasizes the idea that all living beings are interconnected and share the same essence. It challenges the perception of separation and highlights a broader understanding of existence.
  • Equanimity: By seeing the self in others, a person can develop a sense of equanimity, treating everyone with respect and compassion, regardless of their circumstances.
  • Self-Realization: The verse encourages self-inquiry and meditation, guiding individuals to look beyond their ego and personal identity to discover their true self.

Practical Application

Modern readers can apply the teachings of this verse in various ways:

  1. Embrace Compassion: In daily interactions, practice seeing others as extensions of yourself. This can lead to more compassionate actions and responses, fostering understanding instead of conflict.
  2. Mindfulness Practices: Incorporate meditation or mindfulness practices that focus on the interconnectedness of life. For example, during meditation, visualize yourself surrounded by all beings and recognize the shared essence.
  3. Service to Others: Engage in community service or volunteer work where you consciously recognize the unity in diversity. This serves as a practical application of seeing the self in others while also contributing positively to society.

  • What practices can you adopt to cultivate a vision of unity in your daily life?
