Beyond the Veil of Gunas: Recognizing the Imperishable Divine

This verse explains how the three modes of material nature (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas) delude beings, preventing them from recognizing the imperishable Supreme Being.

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 13

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

त्रिभिर्गुणमयैर्भावैरेभिः सर्वमिदं जगत्। मोहितं नाभिजानाति मामेभ्यः परमव्ययम्।।

Transliteration (IAST)

tribhir guṇamayair bhāvair ebhiḥ sarvam idaṁ jagat | mohitaṁ nābhijānāti mām ebhyaḥ param avyayam ||

Translation

Deluded by these three modes of material nature (Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas), the entire world does not know Me, who am beyond them and imperishable.

Explanation

Context

This verse, Chapter 7, Verse 13, is a pivotal point in the Bhagavad Gita’s Jnana Vijnana Yoga (Knowledge and Realization) chapter. Krishna has been systematically revealing the nature of the Supreme Reality. He first established Himself as the essence of all existence (verses 8-11) and the origin of both spiritual and material natures (verse 4-7). In verse 12, Krishna declared that all states of being—whether characterized by goodness (Sattva), passion (Rajas), or ignorance (Tamas)—emanate from Him, yet He is not contained within them; rather, they exist within Him. Verse 13 directly follows, elaborating on the consequence of these Gunas: they delude all beings, preventing them from recognizing the ultimate, imperishable truth that transcends them.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • tribhiḥ (त्रिभिः): By three.
  • guṇamayaiḥ (गुणमयैः): Consisting of or pervaded by the Gunas (modes of material nature).
  • bhāvaiḥ (भावैः): By states of being, dispositions, or manifestations. Here, referring to the experiences and conditions arising from the Gunas.
  • ebhiḥ (एभिः): By these.
  • sarvam (सर्वम्): All, entire.
  • idam (इदम्): This.
  • jagat (जगत्): World, universe, moving creation.
  • mohitam (मोहितम्): Deluded, bewildered, infatuated. From moha (delusion or infatuation).
  • na abhijānāti (नाभिजानाति): Does not know, does not recognize thoroughly. na (not) + abhijānāti (knows thoroughly).
  • mām (माम्): Me (Krishna, the Supreme Being).
  • ebhyaḥ (एभ्यः): From these (the Gunas).
  • param (परम्): Beyond, superior.
  • avyayam (अव्ययम्): Imperishable, immutable, inexhaustible.

Deeper Understanding

Krishna, the divine speaker, reveals a profound truth about human perception and spiritual realization. He explains that the entire world – meaning all living entities – is ensnared by the three Gunas (Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas). These Gunas are not just psychological states but are the fundamental constituents of Prakriti, or material nature. They are the threads that weave the fabric of our phenomenal experience, influencing our thoughts, emotions, actions, and perceptions.

  • The Gunas as Deluders: Sattva (goodness, purity, illumination) binds through happiness and knowledge. Rajas (passion, activity, desire) binds through attachment to action and its fruits. Tamas (ignorance, inertia, delusion) binds through negligence, laziness, and sleep. While Sattva seems beneficial, even it, by creating attachment to happiness and knowledge, can prevent the ultimate transcendence. The constant interplay and dominance of these Gunas create a dense veil of illusion (Maya), preventing beings from seeing beyond the temporary, ever-changing material manifestations. Our identification with these Guna-driven experiences creates a false sense of reality, distracting us from the deeper, unchanging truth.

  • The Supreme as Param Avyayam: Krishna emphasizes His transcendental nature. He is param (beyond) these Gunas and avyayam (imperishable). While the Gunas manifest, change, and dissolve, the Supreme Being is eternal, immutable, and transcends all material attributes and their effects. The world, caught in the ebb and flow of Guna-induced experiences, perceives only the fluctuating forms and qualities of existence, failing to recognize the changeless, eternal substratum that supports everything – which is Krishna Himself. This inability to discern the permanent from the impermanent is the moha or delusion.

  • Relation to other Gita themes: This verse is central to understanding the concept of Maya, the Lord’s illusory energy that creates the material world and binds living entities. The Gunas are the very components of this Maya. It implicitly lays the groundwork for Bhakti Yoga (devotion), as overcoming this delusion often requires turning to a higher power. It also underpins Jnana Yoga (knowledge), highlighting the necessity of true wisdom to penetrate the veil of the Gunas and realize the avyayam (imperishable) Self and Supreme Self. The Gunas create a world of duality, and transcending them is key to achieving equanimity and liberation.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivate Self-Awareness: Begin to observe the influence of the Gunas in your daily life. When you feel energetic and driven (Rajas), peaceful and clear (Sattva), or lethargic and confused (Tamas), acknowledge these as temporary states arising from the Gunas. This awareness helps you to detach from extreme reactions and cultivate an inner sense of equanimity. For instance, instead of being overwhelmed by anger or frustration, observe it as a Guna-induced emotion, allowing you to respond more thoughtfully.
  2. Make Mindful Choices: Consciously choose activities, thoughts, and associations that elevate Sattva. Engaging in practices like meditation, selfless service, wholesome eating, truthful communication, and studying uplifting scriptures helps to reduce the dominance of Rajas and Tamas. A Sattvic lifestyle creates a clearer mind, making it easier to perceive deeper truths. For example, choosing to spend an evening in quiet contemplation or helping others, rather than engaging in excessive stimulation or passive consumption.
  3. Seek the Unchanging Amidst Change: In a world of constant flux – successes, failures, joys, sorrows – consciously cultivate a perspective that seeks the underlying, unchanging reality. This can involve daily spiritual practice, contemplation on the impermanence of material phenomena, or remembering that true peace and fulfillment lie beyond external circumstances. When faced with a significant life change or loss, instead of identifying solely with the temporary situation, reflect on the enduring spiritual essence within yourself and the universe.

  • What specific practices can I adopt to reduce the influence of Rajas and Tamas and cultivate Sattva?
  • How can I consciously shift my focus from the temporary and changing aspects of life to the eternal and imperishable truth?

Key Concepts

  1. The Three Gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas): The fundamental modes or qualities of material nature that govern all phenomena, influencing consciousness, actions, and perception.
  2. Delusion (Moha): The state of being bewildered or confused, caused by identification with the temporary manifestations of the Gunas, which obscures the recognition of the Supreme Reality.
  3. Transcendence (Param): The concept that the Supreme Being (Krishna) exists beyond the influence, limitations, and attributes of the Gunas, being their source but not bound by them.
  4. Imperishability (Avyayam): The eternal, unchanging, and inexhaustible nature of the Supreme Reality, standing in stark contrast to the temporary and ever-changing material world governed by the Gunas.
