The Omniscient Lord and the Veiled Truth: Understanding Krishna's Knowledge

Krishna declares His absolute knowledge of all beings across past, present, and future, contrasting it with the inability of mortals to fully comprehend His divine nature.

November 17, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Jnana Vijnana Yoga
7.26

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 26

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

वेदाहं समतीतानि वर्तमानानि चार्जुन । भविष्याणि च भूतानि मां तु वेद न कश्चन ॥२६॥

Transliteration (IAST)

vedāhaṃ samatītāni vartamānāni cārjuna | bhaviṣyāṇi ca bhūtāni māṃ tu veda na kaścana ||26||

Translation

O Arjuna, I know all beings – those of the past, those of the present, and those of the future – but no one knows Me.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 7, “Jnana Vijnana Yoga,” delves into the nature of the Supreme Lord, Krishna, and the path to realizing Him. Having previously described His dual nature (material and spiritual energies) and declared Himself as the origin and sustenance of all existence, Krishna now addresses the challenge of truly knowing Him. In verse 25, He revealed that He is not manifest to everyone, being veiled by His divine potency (yoga-maya), leading the deluded to mistake Him for an ordinary being. This verse (7.26) directly follows and reinforces that idea, but from Krishna’s own perspective, emphasizing His absolute omniscience while lamenting the general human inability to comprehend His true, transcendental form.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • वेदाहं (vedāham): A combination of veda (I know) and aham (I). So, “I know.”
  • समतीतानि (samatītāni): “That which has completely passed,” referring to past beings or events.
  • वर्तमानानि (vartamānāni): “That which is present,” referring to present beings or events.
  • च (ca): “And.”
  • अर्जुन (arjuna): O Arjuna, Krishna’s address to His dear disciple.
  • भविष्याणि (bhaviṣyāṇi): “That which will be,” referring to future beings or events.
  • च (ca): “And.”
  • भूतानि (bhūtāni): “Beings” or “creatures” – encompassing all living entities.
  • मां (māṃ): “Me.”
  • तु (tu): “But” or “however,” indicating a contrast.
  • वेद (veda): “Knows.”
  • न कश्चन (na kaścana): “No one” or “not anyone.”

Combined, the verse states: “I know all beings – past, present, and future, O Arjuna, but no one knows Me.”

Deeper Understanding

This verse is a profound declaration of Krishna’s divine nature as the Supreme Being (Ishvara). It highlights two critical aspects: His absolute omniscience and the general human ignorance regarding His true identity.

1. Krishna’s Omniscience: As the source and sustainer of all existence, Krishna is not bound by the linear progression of time that governs mortal beings. For Him, past, present, and future are not distinct segments but are simultaneously present in His eternal consciousness. He knows every thought, every action, every destiny of every living being across all epochs. This knowledge is not learned or acquired; it is inherent in His very being. This divine attribute underscores His role as the ultimate controller, the witness (Upadrasta), and the sanctioner (Anumanta) of all actions and their karmic consequences. Nothing is hidden from Him; no secret remains unrevealed in His all-encompassing vision.

2. The Veiled Truth (Why no one knows Him): Despite His infinite knowledge and all-pervading presence, Krishna states that “no one knows Me.” This is not a literal statement that absolutely no one can know Him, but rather that the vast majority of conditioned souls, deluded by the material energy (Maya), fail to recognize His supreme, transcendental nature. They may perceive Him as a historical figure, a mythical deity, or even an ordinary human, but they do not grasp His true, eternal, and all-opulent form. This ignorance stems from attachment to dualities, material desires, and the inability to look beyond the temporary and superficial aspects of existence. Only those who transcend the influence of Maya, cultivate pure devotion, and are graced by Him can truly begin to understand His divine mystery, as hinted at in later chapters (e.g., 18.55: bhaktyā mām abhijānāti – “by devotion, one comes to know Me”). This verse sets the stage for understanding the challenges of spiritual realization.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivating Humility and Trust: Recognizing that there is a supreme, omniscient consciousness that knows all past, present, and future events can foster profound humility. We often operate under the illusion that we understand the world and our own destinies. This verse reminds us of our limited perception. This humility can lead to greater trust in the cosmic order and a higher power, reducing anxiety about the unknown and encouraging surrender to divine will. Instead of striving to control every outcome, we can find peace in knowing that a greater intelligence is at play.

  2. Mindfulness and Ethical Living: The understanding that all our actions, thoughts, and intentions are known by the Supreme Lord (and by the impartial law of karma) encourages a heightened sense of responsibility. It promotes ethical behavior not out of fear, but out of an awareness that our deeds have consequences and are part of a larger, observed reality. This can inspire us to live more consciously, aligning our actions with dharma, knowing that there’s an ultimate witness to our conduct.

  3. Seeking Deeper Truths: This verse challenges us to move beyond superficial understanding and seek true spiritual knowledge. If the Supreme is not easily known, it implies that the path to knowing Him requires sincere effort, introspection, and purification of the heart. It encourages us to question our assumptions about reality and to embark on a journey of self-discovery and spiritual inquiry, rather than settling for conventional or materialistic views.

Reflection

  • In what ways do I unknowingly project my limited human understanding onto the divine or the universe?
  • What steps can I take to cultivate a deeper sense of humility and trust in a higher order in my daily life?

Key Concepts

  • Divine Omniscience: Krishna’s absolute, simultaneous knowledge of all past, present, and future events and beings.
  • Human Limitation: The inherent inability of most conditioned souls to comprehend the Supreme Lord’s true, transcendental nature due to material delusion.
  • The Veiled Reality: The idea that Krishna, despite being the source of all, remains largely ‘unknown’ or ‘unseen’ by those obscured by Maya.
  • Transcendental Knowledge: The implication that true knowledge of the divine requires spiritual endeavor beyond ordinary sensory and intellectual perception.
