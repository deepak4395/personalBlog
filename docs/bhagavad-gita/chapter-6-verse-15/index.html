The Yogi's Ultimate Peace: Abiding in the Divine

This verse reveals how continuous yoga practice and a controlled mind lead the yogi to the ultimate peace of Nirvāṇa, residing in Krishna.

September 20, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Atma Samyama Yoga
6.15

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 15

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

युञ्जन्नेवं सदात्मानं योगी नियतमानसः । शान्तिं निर्वाणपरमां मत्संस्थामधिगच्छति ॥ १५ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

yuñjann evaṃ sadātmānaṃ yogī niyatamānasaḥ | śāntiṃ nirvāṇaparāmāṃ matsaṃsthām adhigacchati || 15 ||

Translation

Thus, constantly engaging the self (mind) in yoga, the yogi with a controlled mind attains the supreme peace of Nirvāṇa, which abides in Me.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled ‘Atma Samyama Yoga’ or ‘Dhyana Yoga,’ is dedicated to the profound practice of meditation and self-control. Lord Krishna has been meticulously guiding Arjuna through the intricacies of establishing a conducive environment, posture, and mental discipline for a yogi. Verses 10 through 14 lay down the foundational principles for a meditator – from finding a secluded spot to maintaining a steady, unwavering gaze and purifying the heart. Verse 15 builds directly upon these instructions, revealing the glorious culmination of such dedicated practice: the attainment of ultimate peace and liberation, intrinsically linked to the Divine.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • युञ्जन् (yuñjan): Engaging, joining, practicing yoga. It signifies the continuous act of connecting or yoking the mind.
  • एवम् (evam): Thus, in this manner. It refers back to the disciplined practices outlined in the preceding verses.
  • सदा (sadā): Always, constantly, perpetually. Emphasizing the continuous nature of the practice.
  • आत्मानम् (ātmānam): The self, the mind, the individual consciousness. Here, it specifically refers to engaging the mind or the inner being.
  • योगी (yogī): The practitioner of yoga; one who is striving for union or has achieved it.
  • नियतमानसः (niyatamānasaḥ): With a controlled mind. Niyata means regulated or disciplined, and mānasaḥ refers to the mind. This highlights the essential prerequisite of mental discipline.
  • शान्तिम् (śāntim): Peace, tranquility, serenity.
  • निर्वाणपरमाम् (nirvāṇaparāmām): Supreme Nirvāṇa. Nirvāṇa signifies the extinction of suffering, liberation from the cycle of birth and death. Paramām means supreme, ultimate, or highest. Together, it denotes the highest state of peace, transcending all worldly suffering.
  • मत्संस्थाम् (matsaṃsthām): Abiding in Me, resting in Me. Mat (in Me, referring to Krishna, the Supreme Being) + saṃsthām (abiding, situated, resting). This crucial phrase connects the ultimate peace directly to the Divine.
  • अधिगच्छति (adhigacchati): Attains, reaches, achieves.

Deeper Understanding

Krishna’s teaching in this verse is both a promise and a profound philosophical statement. He assures Arjuna that consistent and disciplined yoga practice, particularly meditation, culminates in the highest state of peace. This peace is not merely the absence of disturbance but nirvāṇaparāmāṃ śānti – the supreme peace of Nirvāṇa.

For the Gita, Nirvāṇa is not an impersonal void but a state of liberation achieved through the realization of one’s true self and its intrinsic connection to the Divine. The most significant aspect of this verse is the phrase matsaṃsthām – abiding in Me. Krishna unequivocally states that this supreme peace, this Nirvāṇa, is not an independent achievement but a state found in Him. This distinguishes the Gita’s understanding of liberation from purely impersonal or nihilistic interpretations. The ultimate reality is personal (Ishvara, Krishna), and true liberation is a state of union or abiding in that Supreme Being. This integrates the path of Dhyana Yoga (meditation) with Bhakti Yoga (devotion), suggesting that the deepest meditative absorption naturally leads one to the Divine source of all existence.

The verse emphasizes that such a state is not accidental but the direct result of sadātmānaṃ yuñjan (constantly engaging the self in yoga) and possessing a niyatamānasaḥ (controlled mind). Without these two pillars – consistent effort and mental discipline – the ultimate spiritual goal remains elusive. The peace attained is not fleeting but paramā, supreme and everlasting.

Practical Application

  1. Consistency in Practice: In our fast-paced modern lives, it’s easy to let spiritual practices slide. This verse reminds us that sadā (always, constantly) engaging the mind is key. Instead of sporadic, intense efforts, commit to shorter, consistent periods of meditation, mindfulness, or prayer daily. For example, dedicate 10-15 minutes every morning to silent contemplation or breath awareness, even on busy days.
  2. Cultivating Mental Discipline: The ‘controlled mind’ (niyatamānasaḥ) is crucial. This doesn’t mean suppressing thoughts, but learning to observe them without attachment and gently redirecting focus. Practice mindfulness in daily activities – eating, walking, working – to bring awareness to the present moment and reduce mental wandering. When faced with stress or distraction, consciously pause, take a few deep breaths, and choose your response rather than reacting impulsively.
  3. Seeking Inner Peace through Connection: Understand that true, lasting peace isn’t contingent on external circumstances but is an internal state connected to a higher spiritual source. When feeling agitated or lost, instead of seeking external fixes, turn inward through meditation, prayer, or quiet reflection, remembering that the supreme peace abides in Me (Krishna). This shifts the focus from external gratification to internal spiritual nourishment.

Reflection

  • How consistently do I engage in practices that calm and focus my mind?
  • What specific steps can I take to cultivate greater mental discipline in my daily life?
  • Do I perceive ultimate peace as an internal state, an external achievement, or a deeper connection to the Divine?

Key Concepts

  • Consistent Yoga Practice: The necessity of continuous and dedicated effort in spiritual disciplines.
  • Mind Control (Niyata Manasa): The foundational prerequisite for attaining higher states of consciousness and inner peace.
  • Supreme Peace (Nirvāṇa Parama): The ultimate spiritual goal, signifying liberation from suffering and the cycle of rebirth.
  • Divine Abode (Matsaṃsthām): The profound teaching that ultimate peace and liberation are found by abiding in or connecting with the Divine (Krishna).
