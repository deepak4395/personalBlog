Arjuna's Plea: The Unsuccessful Yogi's Fate

Arjuna questions if an unsuccessful yogi, failing to achieve spiritual realization, loses both worldly pleasures and divine union, perishing like a scattered cloud.

October 13, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Atma Samyama Yoga
6.38

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 38

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

कच्चिन्नोभयविभ्रष्टश्छिन्नाभ्रमिव नश्यति। अप्रतिष्ठो महाबाहो विमूढो ब्रह्मणः पथि॥

Transliteration (IAST)

kaccinnobhayavibhraṣṭaśchinnābhramiva naśyati। apratiṣṭho mahābāho vimūḍho brahmaṇaḥ pathi॥

Translation

O mighty-armed Krishna, does he not, being deluded on the path to Brahman, perish like a scattered cloud, having lost his footing in both (material enjoyment and spiritual realization)?

Explanation

Context

Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, known as Atma Samyama Yoga or the Yoga of Self-Control, delves deep into the practice of meditation (Dhyana Yoga) and the discipline required to control the mind and senses. Lord Krishna has been elaborating on the ideal state of a yogi, the disciplined lifestyle, and the profound peace attained through steady practice. However, Arjuna, ever the practical and discerning student, recognizes the immense difficulty of this path. In previous verses, he expressed his doubts about controlling the ‘restless mind.’ Here, in verses 37-39, Arjuna raises a crucial and very human concern: what happens to an individual who sincerely embarks on this arduous spiritual journey, dedicating themselves to yoga, but fails to achieve perfection or ‘siddhi’ before death? This specific verse (6.38) is part of Arjuna’s poignant question, where he fears that such an unsuccessful yogi might lose out on both the material enjoyments they renounced and the spiritual liberation they sought, effectively perishing without achieving anything substantial.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • कच्चित् (kaccit): Surely not? Is it not? (A particle indicating a question, often with a sense of apprehension).
  • न (na): Not.
  • उभयविभ्रष्टः (ubhayavibhraṣṭaḥ): ubhaya (both) + vibhraṣṭaḥ (fallen from, lost). Meaning, “lost from both.” This refers to both material prosperity/enjoyment (which one might have given up for spiritual life) and spiritual realization.
  • छिन्नाभ्रमिव (chinnābhamiva): chinna (scattered, torn) + abhram (cloud) + iva (like). “Like a scattered cloud.” This powerful metaphor describes something that loses its form, direction, and dissipates without reaching a destination or fulfilling its purpose.
  • नश्यति (naśyati): Perishes, is destroyed, vanishes.
  • अप्रतिष्ठः (apratiṣṭhaḥ): a (not) + pratiṣṭhaḥ (established, firm footing). “Without firm footing,” “unestablished,” lacking a stable base.
  • महाबाहो (mahābāho): O mighty-armed one. An epithet for Krishna, signifying Arjuna’s respect and his appeal for a strong, definitive answer.
  • विमूढः (vimūḍhaḥ): vi (intensifier) + mūḍhaḥ (deluded, bewildered, foolish). “Greatly deluded,” “bewildered,” or one who has lost their way due to confusion.
  • ब्रह्मणः पथि (brahmaṇaḥ pathi): brahmaṇaḥ (of Brahman, of the Absolute Truth) + pathi (on the path). “On the path to Brahman” or “on the spiritual path.”

Deeper Understanding

Arjuna’s question in this verse is profoundly relatable to anyone contemplating a significant life change or spiritual commitment. It highlights the fear of making a substantial sacrifice (of worldly pleasures and pursuits) only to fail in the intended spiritual goal. The core of his concern lies in the idea of being ubhayavibhraṣṭaḥ – losing both. If one abandons the path of material enjoyment for the spiritual path but doesn’t succeed in spiritual realization, what becomes of them? Have they lost everything?

The metaphor of the ‘scattered cloud’ is particularly poignant. A cloud forms, gathers potential, and journeys through the sky. If it doesn’t condense to rain, doesn’t join a larger system, or simply dissipates due to adverse winds, it vanishes without fulfilling its purpose. It represents a loss of potential, a failure to materialize, and ultimately, a vanishing without leaving a lasting impact. Arjuna fears that the unsuccessful yogi might suffer a similar fate – their life’s spiritual potential scattered and lost.

Furthermore, the term vimūḍhaḥ (deluded or bewildered) suggests that the failure might not just be a lack of effort but perhaps a misunderstanding of the path itself, or succumbing to the mind’s illusions. The ‘path to Brahman’ is the ultimate journey towards self-realization and union with the Absolute Truth, a journey that demands unwavering focus and dedication. Arjuna’s query implicitly touches upon the concepts of karma, reincarnation, and the continuity of spiritual progress across lifetimes, which Krishna will address in His reassuring response.

Practical Application

Arjuna’s concern, though ancient, resonates deeply with modern spiritual seekers and indeed, anyone pursuing a long-term goal that requires sacrifice and sustained effort:

  1. Embrace Imperfect Progress: In today’s instant-gratification world, we often seek immediate results. This verse reminds us that spiritual growth is a journey, not a destination achieved overnight. Don’t be discouraged by temporary setbacks or the fear of not reaching perfection. Every step, every sincere effort, no matter how small or imperfect, contributes to one’s spiritual evolution. The journey itself builds character, discipline, and wisdom.
  2. Value the Process, Not Just the Outcome: While the ultimate goal of yoga is profound, the very act of practicing self-control, meditation, and ethical living brings immediate benefits. These practices cultivate peace of mind, reduce stress, improve focus, and foster compassion. These are tangible gains that enhance daily life, irrespective of whether one achieves ‘enlightenment’ in this lifetime. Don’t let the fear of not reaching the summit prevent you from enjoying the climb and the views along the way.
  3. Persistence Despite Setbacks: The metaphor of the ‘scattered cloud’ is a warning against giving up completely after a fall. Life is full of distractions and challenges that can make us ‘lose our footing.’ The key is not to let these temporary lapses lead to complete abandonment of the path. If you falter, acknowledge it, learn from it, and re-establish your practice. The spiritual journey is about continuous effort and returning to the path after deviations.

Reflection

  • What does ‘success’ on your personal or spiritual path truly mean to you? Is it an ultimate destination, or is the journey itself a form of success?
  • How do you manage the fear of ‘losing both worlds’ when making significant commitments or sacrifices in your life?
  • Can you identify times when you felt like a ‘scattered cloud’? What helped you regain your focus and direction?

Key Concepts

  • Ubhayavibhraṣṭaḥ (Loss from Both): The fear of an unsuccessful yogi losing both worldly enjoyment and spiritual realization.
  • Chinnābhamiva (Like a Scattered Cloud): A powerful metaphor for dissipation, loss of purpose, and vanishing without achieving potential.
  • Vimūḍhaḥ (Bewildered/Deluded Yogi): Highlights the potential for confusion or misdirection on the spiritual path.
  • Apratiṣṭha (Without Firm Footing): Emphasizes the importance of stability and consistent effort in spiritual practice.
