The Ultimate Knowledge: Knowing Which Nothing Remains Unknown

Krishna promises to reveal complete knowledge (jñāna) and its practical realization (vijñāna), assuring that after knowing this, nothing else needs to be known.

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 2

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

ज्ञानं तेऽहं सविज्ञानमिदं वक्ष्याम्यशेषतः। यज्ज्ञात्वा नेह भूयोऽन्यज्ज्ञातव्यमवशिष्यते॥

Transliteration (IAST)

jñānaṁ te'haṁ savijñānam idaṁ vakṣyāmy aśeṣataḥ | yaj jñātvā neha bhūyo 'nyaj jñātavyam avaśiṣyate ||

Translation

I shall declare to you in full this knowledge (jñāna) along with its realization (vijñāna), knowing which nothing else remains to be known in this world.

Explanation

Context

In the first verse of Chapter 7, known as Jñāna Vijñāna Yoga (The Yoga of Knowledge and Realization), Lord Krishna promises Arjuna that He will speak further about Himself, specifically how one can know Him completely, free from doubt. This second verse immediately follows, laying down a profound declaration that sets the stage for the entire chapter and indeed, much of the remaining teachings of the Gita. Krishna is not just offering another piece of information; He is promising the ultimate truth, a comprehensive understanding that, once attained, leaves no room for further inquiry or uncertainty. It’s a statement of absolute completeness and finality in spiritual wisdom, a direct invitation to Arjuna—and all seekers—to pay close attention to what follows. This promise underscores the supreme importance of the knowledge Krishna is about to impart, positioning it as the pinnacle of all understanding.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • ज्ञानं (jñānam): Knowledge. In the context of the Gita, this often refers to theoretical or intellectual understanding of spiritual truths, such as the nature of the Self, the Divine, and the cosmos.
  • तेऽहं (te’ham): To you, I. Krishna is directly addressing Arjuna, signifying a personal and intimate transmission of wisdom.
  • सविज्ञानम् (savijñānam): Along with realization/experiential knowledge. This is a crucial term. Vi-jñāna signifies a deeper, direct, and practical experience of the jñāna. It’s not just knowing about something, but knowing it through direct perception and embodiment. It bridges the gap between theory and practice, between belief and experience.
  • इदं (idam): This. Referring to the knowledge Krishna is about to reveal.
  • वक्ष्यामि (vakṣyāmi): I shall declare/speak. A promise of imparting this wisdom.
  • अशेषतः (aśeṣataḥ): Completely, in full, without any remainder. Emphasizes the comprehensive nature of the knowledge. Nothing will be left out.
  • यज्ज्ञात्वा (yaj jñātvā): Knowing which. Once this knowledge is grasped.
  • नेह (neha): Not here (in this world).
  • भूयोऽन्यत् (bhūyo ‘nyat): Anything else further.
  • ज्ञातव्यम् (jñātavyam): To be known.
  • अवशिष्यते (avaśiṣyate): Remains.
    • Thus, “Knowing which, nothing else remains to be known here in this world.”

Deeper Understanding

This verse presents a profound philosophical distinction between jñāna and vijñāna, which is central to the spiritual journey. Jñāna is the intellectual comprehension of spiritual truths – understanding the scriptures, philosophical concepts, and the nature of reality through study and contemplation. It is essential, providing the map for the journey. However, vijñāna is the direct, intuitive, and experiential realization of those truths. It is walking the path, experiencing the landscape, and ultimately reaching the destination. One might know about the taste of a mango (jñāna), but only by eating it does one have vijñāna.

Krishna’s promise to impart this knowledge savijñānam (along with realization) means He is offering a holistic path to truth. It’s not just about accumulating facts about the Divine, but about integrating that understanding into one’s very being, transforming perception, and directly experiencing the presence of the Divine in all aspects of existence. This integrated knowledge is the ultimate wisdom because it resolves all doubts, answers all fundamental questions about existence, purpose, and suffering, and liberates the individual from the cycle of ignorance and material entanglement.

The statement “knowing which nothing else remains to be known” is a powerful testament to the self-sufficiency and completeness of this divine knowledge. It implies that all other forms of knowledge—material, scientific, philosophical—are ultimately subsets or partial reflections of this supreme truth. Once the root cause of all phenomena, the ultimate reality (Brahman or Ishvara), is understood and realized, all its manifestations become clear. This knowledge leads to moksha (liberation) because it dispels the fundamental ignorance that binds the soul to the material world. It brings an end to the endless seeking and desiring that characterizes human life, leading to a state of profound peace and contentment.

Practical Application

  1. Seek Holistic Understanding: When approaching any subject, especially spiritual teachings, don’t stop at intellectual comprehension. Strive for practical application and direct experience. If you study compassion, practice it. If you learn about meditation, meditate. Bridge the gap between theory and practice in all aspects of your life.
  2. Prioritize Core Knowledge: In an age of information overload, this verse encourages us to discern what truly matters. Instead of endlessly accumulating superficial facts, focus on foundational truths that can transform your perspective and lead to lasting peace. Ask yourself: “Does this knowledge bring me closer to understanding myself and the ultimate reality, or is it merely distracting?”
  3. Integrate Spirituality into Daily Life: Don’t compartmentalize spiritual learning to specific times or places. The vijñāna aspect means living your understanding. If you believe in unity, practice it in your interactions. If you understand impermanence, live with less attachment. Let your spiritual insights inform your decisions, reactions, and relationships every moment.

Reflection

  • Where do I currently distinguish between knowing about something and truly knowing it through experience in my life?
  • What fundamental questions am I still seeking answers to, and could a deeper understanding of the Divine address them?
  • How can I move beyond mere intellectual understanding of spiritual concepts towards their direct realization in my daily existence?

Key Concepts

  • Jñāna (Knowledge): Intellectual or theoretical understanding of spiritual truths.
  • Vijñāna (Realization/Experiential Wisdom): Direct, intuitive, and practical experience or embodiment of spiritual knowledge.
  • Ultimate Truth: The supreme, comprehensive knowledge of the Divine, knowing which resolves all doubts and fulfills all inquiry.
  • Completeness of Divine Knowledge: The idea that understanding the ultimate reality provides a complete framework for comprehending all existence, leaving nothing further to be known for true liberation.
