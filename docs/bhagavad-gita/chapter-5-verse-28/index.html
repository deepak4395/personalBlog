Realizing the Supreme: The Path to Everlasting Peace (Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 5, Verse 28)

Realizing Krishna as the ultimate enjoyer of all efforts, the Lord of all worlds, and the true friend of all beings, one attains supreme peace.

Chapter 5: Karma Sanyasa Yoga

Verse 28

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

भोक्तारं यज्ञतपसां सर्वलोकमहेश्वरम्। सुहृदं सर्वभूतानां ज्ञात्वा मां शान्तिमृच्छति॥

Transliteration (IAST)

bhoktāraṁ yajñatapasāṁ sarvalokamaheśvaram | suhṛdaṁ sarvabhūtānāṁ jñātvā māṁ śāntimṛcchati ||

Translation

Having known Me as the enjoyer of all sacrifices and austerities, the great Lord of all worlds, and the friend of all beings, one attains peace.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 5, “Karma Sanyasa Yoga,” or the Yoga of Renunciation of Action in Knowledge, explores the relationship between sannyasa (renunciation) and karma yoga (action in devotion). Arjuna is confused about which path is superior – renouncing all action or performing action without attachment. Krishna clarifies that true renunciation is not about abandoning action but about renouncing the fruits of action and the sense of doership. He emphasizes that both paths, when understood correctly, lead to the same goal. In the verses leading up to 5.28, Krishna has been describing the state of a liberated soul, one who has transcended dualities, whose mind is steady, and who finds inner joy. He has spoken about controlling the senses, focusing the mind, and the importance of meditation for achieving liberation. Verse 28 culminates this section by revealing the ultimate truth whose realization brings complete and lasting peace. It provides the foundational understanding upon which all spiritual practices rest. It’s a key verse that summarizes the essence of devotional knowledge, leading to a state of inner tranquility and freedom from suffering.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • भोक्तारं (bhoktāraṁ) यज्ञतपसां (yajñatapasāṁ): “The enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities.” Bhoktā means the enjoyer, the one who receives and benefits. Yajña refers to ritualistic sacrifices, devotional offerings, and any action performed for a higher purpose. Tapas refers to austerities, self-discipline, and penance undertaken for spiritual purification. Krishna declares Himself as the ultimate recipient and beneficiary of all such efforts, implying that any spiritual endeavor, when offered to the Divine, finds its true fulfillment in Him. It means that the fruits of all spiritual practices ultimately belong to the Supreme.
  • सर्वलोकमहेश्वरम् (sarvalokamaheśvaram): “The great Lord of all worlds.” Sarva means all, loka means worlds or planes of existence, and Maheśvaram means the great Lord or Supreme Controller. This emphasizes Krishna’s absolute sovereignty and omnipotence. He is not just a deity among many but the supreme ruler and orchestrator of the entire cosmos, encompassing all realms and beings.
  • सुहृदं (suhṛdaṁ) सर्वभूतानां (sarvabhūtānāṁ): “The friend of all beings.” Suhṛt is a special kind of friend – a well-wisher, a benefactor, one who genuinely desires the welfare of another without expectation. Sarvabhūtānāṁ means of all living entities, from the smallest insect to the greatest sage. This reveals the benevolent and compassionate aspect of the Divine. The Supreme Being is not a distant, indifferent ruler but an intimate, loving friend who constantly guides and supports all beings.
  • ज्ञात्वा (jñātvā) मां (māṁ) शान्तिम् (śāntim) ऋच्छति (ṛcchati): “Having known Me, one attains peace.” Jñātvā (having known or realized) is crucial. It’s not just intellectual understanding but a deep, experiential realization. When one truly internalizes these three truths about the Supreme – His proprietorship, His sovereignty, and His benevolence – one achieves śānti, profound and lasting peace, freedom from agitation, fear, and sorrow.

Deeper Understanding

Krishna is teaching Arjuna a profound truth that forms the bedrock of spiritual life: the nature of the Supreme Being and our relationship with Him.

  1. Divine Proprietorship (Bhoktāraṁ Yajñatapasāṁ): This concept challenges the ego-centric view that “I am the doer” and “I am the enjoyer.” By understanding that Krishna is the ultimate enjoyer and recipient of all yajña (sacrifices, offerings, good deeds) and tapas (austerities, self-discipline), a practitioner shifts their focus from personal gain or recognition to selfless offering. This knowledge dissolves the ego’s attachment to results, as all efforts are understood as offerings to the Divine. It’s a call to perform actions with a spirit of dedication, knowing that their true value lies in their offering to the Supreme. This is the essence of Karma Yoga.
  2. Divine Sovereignty (Sarvalokamaheśvaram): Realizing God as the supreme Lord of all worlds instills a sense of humility and trust. It means that there is a cosmic order, a divine plan, and a supreme controller who governs everything. This understanding helps one surrender to the divine will, accepting circumstances with equanimity, knowing that everything is ultimately under His control. It removes the burden of feeling solely responsible for the outcome of events and fosters a sense of security in the face of life’s uncertainties. It connects to the concept of Ishvara (the Supreme Controller) in Yoga philosophy.
  3. Divine Benevolence (Suhṛdaṁ Sarvabhūtānāṁ): This is perhaps the most comforting and transformative aspect. To know that the Supreme Being is not just a powerful ruler but also a loving, unconditional friend to all beings eradicates fear, loneliness, and despair. It means that we are never truly alone; the divine is always with us, wishing us well, guiding us, and supporting us in our journey. This fosters a deep sense of devotion (Bhakti Yoga) and trust. It encourages compassion towards all beings, as they are all equally beloved children of the same divine friend.

These three realizations collectively dismantle the illusion of separation, ego, and fear. When one truly knows Krishna in these three aspects – as the ultimate enjoyer, the supreme controller, and the selfless friend – the mind finds its ultimate repose. This knowledge leads to śānti (peace), which is not merely the absence of disturbance but an abiding state of inner tranquility and fulfillment, born of right understanding and connection with the Divine. It liberates one from the cycle of desire, attachment, and suffering.

Practical Application

  1. Transforming Work into Worship: Approach your daily tasks, big or small, as an offering to the Divine. Whether it’s your professional work, household chores, or community service, perform them with diligence and dedication, not for personal praise or monetary gain alone, but as a service to the Supreme. This shifts the focus from “what I get” to “what I give,” leading to greater satisfaction and reducing stress from outcome expectations. For example, a chef can cook with the intention of nourishing others, seeing it as an offering, rather than just a means to earn money.
  2. Cultivating Trust and Surrender: When faced with challenges or uncertainties, remember that there is a greater cosmic order and a Supreme Controller. Practice surrendering your anxieties and control to this higher power. Do your best, but then trust that the outcome is ultimately in divine hands. This doesn’t mean inaction, but rather acting with faith and letting go of obsessive worry. For instance, if you’re working on a project, give it your full effort, but don’t become overly anxious about the results, trusting that the universe will unfold as it should.
  3. Fostering Universal Compassion: If the Supreme is the friend and well-wisher of all beings, then all beings are interconnected and worthy of our compassion. Extend kindness, empathy, and understanding not just to your loved ones but to strangers, adversaries, and even animals. This realization naturally leads to a more inclusive and harmonious way of living, reducing prejudice and conflict. For example, when encountering someone difficult, try to see them as a fellow being, a part of the same divine family, and respond with patience rather than anger.

Reflection

  • How does viewing your actions as offerings change your approach to daily life?
  • In what areas of your life do you struggle with control, and how might surrendering to a higher power bring peace?
  • How can you cultivate a deeper sense of universal friendship and compassion in your interactions?

Key Concepts

  • Divine Proprietorship: The understanding that the Supreme Being is the ultimate enjoyer and beneficiary of all sacrifices, austerities, and actions.
  • Divine Sovereignty: The realization that the Supreme is the great Lord and controller of all worlds and cosmic phenomena.
  • Divine Benevolence: The profound truth that the Supreme Being is the unconditional friend and well-wisher of all living entities.
  • Attainment of Peace (Shanti): The ultimate result of realizing these three aspects of the Divine, leading to freedom from fear, attachment, and suffering.
