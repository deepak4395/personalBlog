The Wise One: Krishna's Own Self - Bhagavad Gita Chapter 7, Verse 18

Krishna declares all devotees noble, but the wise one, who seeks Him as the supreme goal, is considered His very Self, deeply unified with the Divine.

November 9, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Jnana Vijnana Yoga
7.18

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 18

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

उदाराः सर्व एवैते ज्ञानी त्वात्मैव मे मतम्। आस्थितः स हि युक्तात्मा मामेवानुत्तमां गतिम्॥

Transliteration (IAST)

udārāḥ sarva evaite jñānī tvātmaiva me matam। āsthitaḥ sa hi yuktātmā māmevānuttamāṁ gatim॥

Translation

All these indeed are noble, but the wise one, I consider to be My very Self. For, being steadfast in Me, he is devoted to Me alone as the supreme goal.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled “Jnana Vijnana Yoga,” delves into the profound knowledge of the Absolute Truth, both in its theoretical (jnana) and experiential (vijnana) aspects. Lord Krishna has been systematically revealing His divine nature, explaining how He is the essence of everything, the source and sustainer of the universe, yet remains transcendent. In the preceding verses (7.16-17), Krishna introduced four types of devotees who turn to Him: the distressed (ārta), the seeker of wealth (arthārthī), the seeker of knowledge (jijñāsu), and the wise one (jñānī). While acknowledging that all four are virtuous for approaching Him, Krishna specifically praised the jñānī (the wise one) as being superior due to their constant union and single-minded devotion. Verse 18 further elaborates on this profound distinction, explaining why the jñānī holds such an exalted position in the eyes of the Lord.

Word-by-Word Meaning

Let’s break down the key terms in this powerful verse:

  • उदाराः (udārāḥ): Noble, magnanimous, generous, exalted. Krishna uses this term to acknowledge the inherent goodness and virtue of all devotees, regardless of their initial motivation. Their turning towards the Divine is itself a noble act.
  • सर्व एवैते (sarva evaite): All these indeed. This refers to the four types of devotees mentioned in verse 16 (the distressed, the wealth-seeker, the knowledge-seeker, and the wise).
  • ज्ञानी (jñānī): The wise one; the knower. This refers to the devotee who has realized the true nature of the Self and its unity with the Supreme Reality.
  • तु (tu): But, however. This conjunction marks a shift, highlighting the special status of the jñānī.
  • आत्मा एव मे मतम् (ātmā eva me matam): My very Self, is My conviction/opinion. This is a profoundly significant declaration. Krishna states that the jñānī is not just dear to Him, but is His very Self. This implies a non-dualistic identity.
  • आस्थितः स हि युक्तात्मा (āsthitaḥ sa hi yuktātmā): For, he, with a disciplined/unified mind, is established. The jñānī is characterized by a yuktātmā, a mind that is steadfast, disciplined, and constantly united with the divine consciousness.
  • माम् एव अनुत्तमाम् गतिम् (mām eva anuttamām gatim): Me alone as the unsurpassed goal/destination. The jñānī’s devotion is not for any lesser objective; their ultimate and sole aim is the realization of Krishna as the Supreme Truth.

Deeper Understanding

This verse encapsulates a pivotal teaching of the Bhagavad Gita, highlighting the pinnacle of spiritual realization. Krishna’s declaration that the jñānī is “My very Self” is a direct affirmation of the Advaita (non-dualistic) philosophy, indicating that the individual soul (ātman) when fully realized, is identical with the Supreme Soul (Brahman or Paramātman). It’s not merely a metaphor for closeness but a statement of essential oneness.

The other three types of devotees, while noble, approach Krishna with a degree of separation. The distressed seek relief, the wealth-seeker seeks prosperity, and even the knowledge-seeker seeks understanding that might still be external. Their devotion is often conditional or motivated by specific needs. The jñānī, however, has transcended these motivations. Their devotion is born out of realized knowledge – a deep, intuitive understanding that there is no fundamental difference between their true Self and the Supreme Being. They do not seek Krishna for anything, but rather seek Krishna for Krishna’s sake, recognizing Him as the ultimate truth of their own existence.

This unwavering devotion (anuttamām gatim) is not an emotional outburst but a steady, conscious alignment of the entire being with the Divine. The jñānī’s mind (yuktātmā) is constantly absorbed in this truth, free from the distractions of the material world and the illusions of separate identity. Such a soul lives in constant awareness of the Divine presence, seeing Krishna in all beings and all experiences. Their actions are naturally aligned with dharma because their inner being is united with the source of all righteousness. They have truly attained yoga – the union with the Absolute.

Practical Application

This profound teaching offers several practical applications for modern seekers:

  1. Elevate Your Intentions in Spiritual Practice: While it’s natural to pray for help or seek comfort, this verse encourages us to gradually move beyond transactional spirituality. Ask yourself: Am I seeking God for what He can give me, or for the sake of knowing and uniting with Him? Strive to cultivate a devotion that is less about personal gain and more about pure love and realization of truth.
  2. Cultivate Self-Inquiry and Introspection: The path of the jñānī is one of self-knowledge. Dedicate time to meditation, contemplation, and self-inquiry to understand your true nature beyond your ego, roles, and temporary identities. Who am I, truly? What is the essence of my being? This introspection helps dismantle the illusion of separateness and fosters the realization of unity.
  3. Practice Unconditional Love and Service: When you begin to perceive the Divine in all beings, your actions naturally become selfless and imbued with unconditional love. Serve others not out of obligation or expectation, but as an expression of your recognition of the Divine within them. This expands your consciousness and helps you to feel the unity that the jñānī embodies.

Reflection

  • What does it truly mean to consider someone “My very Self”? How does this concept challenge or affirm your understanding of the relationship between the individual and the Divine?
  • How can one transition from seeking God for specific needs to seeking God for God’s sake, as the ultimate goal?
  • In what ways can you cultivate a more “steadfast” and “unified” mind (yuktātmā) in your daily life, amidst its many distractions?

Key Concepts

  1. Superiority of the Jñānī: While all devotees are acknowledged as noble, the wise one (jñānī) is explicitly elevated to the highest spiritual stature.
  2. Non-Dual Identity: Krishna’s declaration “My very Self” signifies the ultimate non-dualistic truth that the realized individual soul is one with the Supreme Being.
  3. Unwavering, Pure Devotion: The jñānī’s devotion is characterized by being single-pointed, aimed solely at Krishna as the unsurpassed goal, free from other desires.
  4. Steadfastness in Yoga (Yuktātmā): The wise one maintains a disciplined, unified mind, constantly absorbed in the awareness of the Divine, embodying true yoga.
