The Essence of Selfless Action in Karma Yoga: Chapter 3, Verse 22

This verse emphasizes the importance of selfless action as a model for others, illustrating the path of Karma Yoga and duty.

June 5, 2026

Chapter 3: Karma Yoga

Verse 22

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

यदिह यदृच्छति सततं स्यात्कर्म तत्र तत्र कर्तव्यम्।

Transliteration (IAST)

Yad iha yadṛcchati satataṁ syāt karma tatra tatra kartavyam.

Translation

Whatever action a great person performs, common people follow in their footsteps. Whatever standards they set, the world pursues.

Explanation

Context

This verse is situated in Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, which is dedicated to Karma Yoga, or the Yoga of Action. In this chapter, Krishna explains the importance of performing one’s duties without attachment to the results. Arjuna, the warrior prince, is in a state of confusion and moral dilemma about engaging in battle. Krishna emphasizes that actions should be performed selflessly, setting an example for others. This verse highlights the profound impact of exemplary actions by individuals in positions of influence.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • Yad: Whatever
  • iha: here (in this world)
  • yadṛcchati: a great person (literally, one who is endowed with great qualities)
  • satataṁ: constantly or perpetually
  • syāt: becomes or is
  • karma: action
  • tatra: there (in that context)
  • tatra: there (again, emphasizing the importance)
  • kartavyam: should be done or is to be done.

Deeper Understanding

In this verse, Krishna is teaching Arjuna that great individuals, or leaders, play a pivotal role in shaping the actions and behaviors of society. Their actions serve as a model for the common people, who tend to follow their examples. This emphasizes the responsibility that comes with leadership; those in influential positions must act righteously because their behavior sets the tone for the collective consciousness of society.

The key concepts presented in this verse relate to the interconnectedness of society and individual actions. This verse encapsulates the essence of Dharma (righteous duty) and Karma (action), suggesting that when one performs their duty selflessly, it not only benefits the individual but also positively influences others. This highlights the principle of Yoga, which is about union and connection; our actions are not isolated but resonate through the fabric of society.

Practical Application

  1. Leading by Example: In workplaces, leaders should embrace ethical practices and transparency. For instance, a manager who openly acknowledges mistakes and learns from them encourages a culture of openness and continuous improvement among team members.
  2. Community Engagement: Individuals can impact their communities by volunteering or participating in local initiatives. When someone takes initiative in community service, others are likely to follow suit, creating a ripple effect of positive action.
  3. Self-reflection in Parenting: Parents serve as role models for their children. By demonstrating kindness, empathy, and resilience in challenging situations, parents can instill these values in their children, who will carry them into their adult lives.

Reflection

  • How can I embody the qualities of a great person in my daily life?
  • In what areas of my life am I setting an example for others?
  • How can I transform my actions to inspire those around me?

Key Concepts

  • Leadership and Influence: The profound impact of actions by influential individuals on society.
  • Responsibility: The moral obligation to act righteously in leadership roles.
  • Interconnectedness: Understanding that individual actions have collective consequences.
  • Karma Yoga: Engaging in selfless action as a means of spiritual growth and societal benefit.
