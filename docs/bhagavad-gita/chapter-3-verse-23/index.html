The Divine Example: Why Krishna Acts Tirelessly

Krishna explains that if He, the Supreme Being, were to cease tireless action, humanity would follow His path of inaction, leading to societal decay.

June 6, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Karma Yoga #Divine Example #Leadership #Dharma #Action
3.23

Chapter 3: Karma Yoga

Verse 23

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

यदि ह्यहं न वर्तेयं जातु कर्मण्यतन्द्रितः। मम वर्त्मानुवर्तन्ते मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः॥

Transliteration (IAST)

yadi hyahaṃ na varteyaṃ jātu karmaṇyatandritaḥ | mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ ||

Translation

For if I were not to engage in action, tirelessly and without lassitude, O Pārtha, all men would follow My path in every respect.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled “Karma Yoga,” delves into the philosophy of action. Arjuna, initially confused about the necessity of engaging in battle when spiritual wisdom often suggests renunciation, is seeking clarity from Lord Krishna. Krishna has been explaining that true renunciation is not about abstaining from action, but performing action without attachment to its fruits. In the preceding verses (3.21-22), Krishna established that whatever a great man does, others follow, and that He Himself has no duty to perform in the three worlds, nor anything to gain, yet He continues to act. This particular verse (3.23) serves as a crucial explanation for why Krishna, the Supreme Being, tirelessly engages in action, even though He is beyond all needs and desires. It highlights the profound consequences of His potential inaction, setting the stage for the concept of lokasaṅgraha (welfare of the world) which is explicitly discussed in the subsequent shloka. Krishna is not merely stating a theoretical principle; He is demonstrating it through His own divine conduct, providing the ultimate example for humanity.

Word-by-Word Meaning

Let’s break down the key terms in this powerful verse:

  • यदि ह्यहं (yadi hyahaṃ): “If indeed I.” Krishna refers to Himself, emphasizing His supreme position.
  • न वर्तेयं (na varteyaṃ): “Were not to engage.” This signifies a hypothetical scenario of inaction.
  • जातु (jātu): “Ever,” “at any time.” It underscores the absolute nature of the hypothetical non-engagement.
  • कर्मणि (karmaṇi): “In action.” Refers to all forms of duty, work, or engagement in the world.
  • अतन्द्रितः (atandritaḥ): “Untiringly,” “without lassitude,” “diligently,” “without indolence.” This is a critical adjective, highlighting the quality of action – not just action, but action performed with full energy, alertness, and commitment.
  • मम वर्त्मानुवर्तन्ते (mama vartmānuvartante): “My path would be followed.” Vartman means path or way; anuvartante means they would follow. Krishna’s way, even if it were inaction, would become the norm.
  • मनुष्याः (manuṣyāḥ): “Men,” “humanity.” Refers to all human beings.
  • पार्थ (pārtha): “O Pārtha,” a common epithet for Arjuna, meaning “son of Pṛthā” (Kuntī). It signifies an intimate address.
  • सर्वशः (sarvaśaḥ): “In every respect,” “entirely,” “completely.” This emphasizes that humanity would imitate His path in all aspects, leading to universal inaction.

Deeper Understanding

This verse unveils a profound truth about leadership, divine responsibility, and the interconnectedness of existence. Krishna, as the Supreme Lord, is not bound by karma; His actions do not create new karmic reactions because they are performed without desire for results and for the maintenance of the cosmic order. Yet, He reveals that His continued, tireless action is absolutely essential.

What is Krishna teaching Arjuna? Krishna is teaching Arjuna that actions performed by influential figures—be they divine beings, spiritual masters, political leaders, or even parents—set a powerful precedent. If the Supreme Lord Himself, who is the ultimate source of all creation and sustenance, were to stop acting, humanity would misinterpret this as the ideal path. They would conclude that if God Himself is inactive, then there is no need for anyone else to act, leading to a widespread embrace of inertia and irresponsibility. This would disrupt the natural order, cease all productive activity, and ultimately lead to chaos and the destruction of the worlds.

Key concepts presented:

  • Divine Example (Ādarśa): Krishna acts as the supreme exemplar. His actions are not for personal gain but for universal welfare. He demonstrates that even the most enlightened being must engage in action for the benefit of others.
  • Consequence of Inaction: The verse highlights the catastrophic consequences of inaction, especially from those who are looked up to. It underscores the idea that inaction, in certain contexts, can be more detrimental than misguided action.
  • Diligence (Atandritaḥ): The word atandritaḥ is crucial. It’s not just about acting, but acting with full awareness, energy, and freedom from laziness. This quality of action is what Krishna expects from His devotees and what He Himself embodies. It speaks to a state of being fully present and engaged in one’s duties.
  • Relates to Dharma and Lokasaṅgraha: This verse implicitly lays the groundwork for lokasaṅgraha, the welfare and maintenance of the world, which is explicitly mentioned in the next verse (3.24). Krishna’s actions are directed towards upholding dharma (righteousness, cosmic order) and ensuring the continuity of creation. Without divine intervention and continuous action, the fabric of existence would unravel. It emphasizes that everyone has a role to play in maintaining the world, and leaders have a special responsibility to set a positive, active example.

Practical Application

The wisdom of this verse is highly relevant in contemporary life, offering powerful insights for individuals and leaders alike:

  1. Leadership by Example: Whether you are a parent, a teacher, a manager, a community leader, or simply an elder sibling, your actions are observed and often emulated. If you display diligence, integrity, and commitment, those under your influence are likely to follow suit. Conversely, apathy, laziness, or unethical conduct on your part can quickly spread and undermine collective effort. This teaching encourages us to be mindful of the example we set, understanding that our conduct has a ripple effect.

  2. Cultivating Diligence in Duty: Krishna’s emphasis on atandritaḥ (untiringly, diligently) teaches us to approach our duties with full energy and commitment, rather than performing them half-heartedly or with procrastination. In a world often characterized by distraction and short attention spans, cultivating diligence in our work, studies, or personal responsibilities can lead to greater effectiveness, satisfaction, and contribute positively to our environment. It’s about performing one’s karma with a sense of purpose and presence.

  3. Understanding Societal Interdependence: This verse reminds us that society functions through the collective actions of its members. If individuals, especially those in positions of power or influence, shirk their responsibilities, the entire system suffers. It fosters a sense of accountability, prompting us to consider how our personal choices and actions contribute to the larger well-being or decay of our community and the world. Every action, no matter how small, has a consequence and sets an example.

Reflection

  • In what areas of my life am I setting an example for others, and what kind of example is it?
  • Am I performing my daily duties and responsibilities with diligence and full engagement (atandritaḥ), or with a sense of apathy or procrastination?
  • How does my commitment (or lack thereof) to my responsibilities impact those around me and the broader community?

Key Concepts

  • Divine Example (Ādarśa): The Supreme Being setting the ultimate standard for action and conduct for the welfare of the world.
  • Leadership by Action: The principle that those in positions of influence must act diligently and responsibly, as their actions (or inactions) profoundly shape the behavior of others.
  • Diligence (Atandritaḥ): The importance of performing actions tirelessly, without lassitude, and with full commitment and awareness.
  • Consequences of Inaction: The understanding that societal and cosmic order depends on continuous, purposeful action, and that inaction, especially from leaders, can lead to chaos.
