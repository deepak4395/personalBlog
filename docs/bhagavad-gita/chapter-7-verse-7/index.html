Lord Krishna declares Himself as the ultimate reality, the thread upon which all existence is strung, revealing His omnipresence and supreme nature.

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 7

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय। मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव॥

Transliteration (IAST)

mattaḥ parataraṁ nānyat kiñcidasti dhanañjaya | mayi sarvamidaṁ protaṁ sūtre maṇigaṇā iva ||

Translation

There is nothing whatsoever higher than Me, O Dhananjaya. All this is strung on Me, like clusters of gems on a thread.

Explanation

Context

Chapter 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, “Jnana Vijnana Yoga,” focuses on the profound knowledge (Jnana) and realized understanding (Vijnana) of the Divine. Lord Krishna, having previously introduced His two natures—the inferior material energy (apara prakriti) and the superior spiritual energy (para prakriti)—begins to systematically reveal His own supreme and all-encompassing nature. He has already stated that He is the origin and dissolution of the entire cosmos (BG 7.6). This present verse, 7.7, serves as a powerful culmination of these preceding statements, unequivocally declaring His unparalleled supremacy and omnipresence, providing a pivotal insight into the ultimate reality that underpins all existence. It sets the stage for a deeper exploration of how the divine manifests in various aspects of the world.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • मत्तः (mattaḥ): From Me; than Me. This signifies Krishna as the ultimate source and comparison point.
  • परतरम् (parataram): Higher, superior, beyond. It denotes a state of ultimate transcendence.
  • न (na): Not.
  • अन्यत् (anyat): Other, anything else.
  • किञ्चित् (kiñcit): Whatsoever, anything at all.
  • अस्ति (asti): Is, exists.
  • धनञ्जय (dhanañjaya): O Arjuna, conqueror of wealth. A name for Arjuna, often used by Krishna to emphasize his valor and worthiness to receive such profound knowledge.
  • मयि (mayi): In Me. This highlights Krishna as the substratum, the container, the essence.
  • सर्वम् (sarvam): All.
  • इदम् (idam): This. Referring to the entire universe, all phenomena, all beings.
  • प्रोतम् (protam): Strung, threaded, woven, permeated. This is a crucial verb, indicating an intimate connection and underlying support.
  • सूत्रे (sūtre): On a thread. The unseen, unifying element.
  • मणिगणाः (maṇigaṇāḥ): Clusters of gems, a multitude of jewels. Representing the diverse manifest forms of existence.
  • इव (iva): Like, as if. Introducing the powerful analogy.

Deeper Understanding

In this iconic shloka, Krishna asserts His position as the ultimate, non-dual reality. The declaration “There is nothing whatsoever higher than Me” is a profound statement of absolute monotheism and the ultimate nature of Ishvara (the personal God) in Vedanta. It signifies that all other deities, cosmic forces, or philosophical concepts of the absolute are ultimately aspects or manifestations of this one supreme reality, Krishna Himself.

The powerful analogy of “clusters of gems on a thread” is central to understanding Krishna’s omnipresence and immanence. Imagine a necklace: the individual gems are diverse in color, size, and shape, each unique and distinct. Yet, they are all held together by a single, often unseen, thread. Without the thread, the gems would scatter, losing their cohesion and purpose as a necklace. Similarly, the entire universe, with its infinite variety of beings, phenomena, and experiences—the stars, planets, mountains, rivers, plants, animals, and humans—are like these diverse gems. Krishna is the invisible thread (sūtra) that permeates, sustains, and unifies them all.

This teaching resolves the apparent paradox of unity and diversity. The world appears diverse (many gems), but it is fundamentally unified by the divine essence (the single thread). Krishna is not just the creator (the one who makes the necklace) but also the very substance and sustainer (the thread itself) that holds everything together. This means He is both transcendent (beyond all manifestation) and immanent (present within all manifestation).

This concept relates deeply to dharma as the underlying order and sustenance of the cosmos, with Krishna being the ultimate source of that dharma. It also informs yoga, guiding the seeker to perceive this fundamental unity behind all duality, leading to Vijnana – direct realization of this truth, not just intellectual understanding (Jnana). The verse encourages us to look beyond the superficial differences and perceive the divine thread that connects everything, fostering a sense of universal kinship and sacredness.

Practical Application

  1. Cultivating Unity and Compassion: Understanding that the same divine essence permeates all beings can radically shift our perspective. When we see others—friends, family, strangers, even adversaries—as “gems on the same thread” as ourselves, it naturally fosters empathy, compassion, and a sense of shared humanity. This can help overcome prejudice, conflict, and division by recognizing the underlying unity.
  2. Finding Stability Amidst Chaos: In a world often characterized by change, uncertainty, and fragmentation, this teaching offers a profound sense of stability and reassurance. Knowing that there is an ultimate, unchanging reality (Krishna as the thread) that holds everything together can provide inner peace and resilience. When faced with life’s challenges, one can remember that despite superficial disarray, there is an underlying order and support.
  3. Mindfulness and Sacredness in Daily Life: This verse invites us to perceive the sacred in the mundane. Every object, every living being, every experience becomes an expression of the divine thread. Whether it’s the beauty of nature, the complexity of a machine, the warmth of human connection, or the simple act of breathing, recognizing Krishna as the underlying essence elevates ordinary moments to spiritual experiences. This constant awareness can transform daily life into a continuous practice of devotion and spiritual realization.

Key Concepts

  • Divine Omnipresence: Krishna’s declaration that “all this is strung on Me” signifies His presence in every aspect of creation, both manifest and unmanifest.
  • Ultimate Reality (Para-tattvam): The assertion “nothing whatsoever higher than Me” establishes Krishna as the supreme, foundational truth of existence.
  • Unity in Diversity: The analogy of gems on a thread beautifully illustrates how a single divine essence unifies the seemingly disparate and diverse manifestations of the universe.
  • Substratum of Existence: Krishna is presented not just as the creator, but as the invisible, fundamental support and essence that holds all creation together.
