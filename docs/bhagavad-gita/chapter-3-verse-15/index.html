The Divine Essence: Karma, Brahman, and Eternal Sacrifice

This verse reveals how righteous action (karma) stems from the Vedas, which originate from the Imperishable Brahman, eternally present in selfless sacrifice.

May 30, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Karma Yoga
3.15

Chapter 3: Karma Yoga

Verse 15

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

कर्म ब्रह्मोद्भवं विद्धि ब्रह्माक्षरसमुद्भवम् । तस्मात्सर्वगतं ब्रह्म नित्यं यज्ञे प्रतिष्ठितम् ॥ १५ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

karma brahmodbhavaṁ viddhi brahmākṣarasamudbhavam | tasmātsarvagataṁ brahma nityaṁ yajñe pratiṣṭhitam || 15 ||

Translation

Know that action (karma) originates from Brahman (the Veda), and Brahman originates from the Imperishable (Akshara). Therefore, the all-pervading Brahman is eternally established in sacrifice (Yajna).

Explanation

Context

In Chapter 3, Krishna is meticulously building the case for Karma Yoga – the path of selfless action. He has already established that action is inevitable for all beings (3.5) and that inaction is not a viable path to liberation. Verses 3.10-14 introduced the concept of Yajna (sacrifice or selfless action) as the very foundation of creation and sustenance. Krishna explained how the devas (celestial beings or cosmic forces) are pleased by Yajna, leading to rain, food, and life itself. He emphasized the reciprocal relationship between humanity and the cosmos, facilitated by Yajna. Now, in verse 15, Krishna takes this profound concept a step further, revealing the ultimate divine origin of Yajna and, by extension, all righteous action. He connects it directly to Brahman, the ultimate reality, thereby elevating selfless action from a mere duty to a sacred, cosmic principle.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • कर्म (karma): Action, work, duty. Refers to the activities performed by human beings.
  • ब्रह्मोद्भवं (brahmodbhavaṁ): Originating from Brahman. Here, ‘Brahman’ refers specifically to the Vedas, the sacred scriptures or divine knowledge that prescribe righteous action.
  • विद्धि (viddhi): Know, understand, realize.
  • ब्रह्माक्षरसमुद्भवम् (brahmākṣarasamudbhavam): Brahman (the Veda) originating from the Imperishable (Akshara). ‘Akshara’ refers to the Supreme, Immutable Brahman, the ultimate reality, the source of all existence and knowledge.
  • तस्मात् (tasmāt): Therefore, for that reason.
  • सर्वगतं (sarvagataṁ): All-pervading, omnipresent.
  • ब्रह्म (brahma): Brahman, the ultimate reality, the Supreme Being.
  • नित्यं (nityaṁ): Eternally, perpetually, always.
  • यज्ञे (yajñe): In sacrifice, in selfless action, in ritualistic performance for a higher purpose.
  • प्रतिष्ठितम् (pratiṣṭhitam): Established, founded, situated.

Deeper Understanding

Krishna is unveiling a profound chain of cosmic causation and spiritual significance. He teaches Arjuna that:

  1. Action (Karma) originates from Brahman (the Vedas): This implies that righteous actions are not arbitrary human constructs but are guided by divine wisdom as revealed in the Vedas. The Vedas provide the framework for dharma, the cosmic law and righteous living. Thus, our duties and actions, when aligned with universal principles, have a divine sanction.

  2. Brahman (the Vedas) originates from the Imperishable (Akshara): This is a crucial link. It means that the divine knowledge itself (Vedas) is not a human invention but emanates directly from the ultimate, eternal, unchangeable reality – the Supreme Brahman. This elevates the authority of the Vedas and, by extension, the actions prescribed by them, making them expressions of the ultimate truth.

  3. The all-pervading Brahman is eternally established in Yajna: This is the culmination of the verse. Since all righteous action stems from the Vedas, which in turn come from the Supreme Brahman, it logically follows that the Supreme Brahman itself is perpetually present in Yajna. Yajna, therefore, is not merely a ritual; it is a direct manifestation and embodiment of the all-pervading divine principle. When we perform selfless action, we are not just fulfilling a duty; we are participating in the cosmic order, connecting directly with the divine source, and upholding the very fabric of existence. This understanding transforms Yajna into a spiritual act of profound significance, a means to realize the omnipresence of Brahman.

This verse beautifully integrates the practical world of action with the highest spiritual truth. It reinforces Karma Yoga by showing that selfless action (Yajna) is not separate from spiritual realization but is a direct path to it, as the divine itself resides within such action.

Practical Application

  1. Elevating Daily Work: Instead of viewing work as a mundane necessity or a means solely for personal gain, one can transform it into Yajna. Whether you are a software engineer, a teacher, a healthcare worker, or a homemaker, approach your tasks with dedication, integrity, and a sense of service to the larger good. Understanding that your actions contribute to the cosmic flow connects you to the ‘all-pervading Brahman’ and brings purpose beyond mere paycheck or recognition.
  2. Ethical Foundation for Decisions: When faced with choices, especially ethical dilemmas, reflect on the ‘imperishable source’ – the universal principles of truth, compassion, and dharma. This verse encourages us to ground our actions not in fleeting desires or societal pressures, but in eternal wisdom, ensuring that our ‘karma’ aligns with the ‘Brahman’ (Veda) and ultimately the ‘Akshara’ (Supreme Reality).
  3. Community Service and Environmentalism: Acts of selfless service to the community, charity, or efforts to protect the environment can be seen as direct forms of Yajna. By contributing to the well-being of others and the planet, one is participating in the sustenance of life and aligning with the divine principle that is ‘eternally established in sacrifice.’ This perspective fosters a sense of unity and responsibility.

Reflection

  • How can I consciously connect my everyday actions to a higher purpose or universal principle?
  • What ‘imperishable source’ or guiding wisdom do I draw upon when making important decisions?
  • How does viewing my contributions as ‘Yajna’ change my perspective on effort, outcome, and personal satisfaction?

Key Concepts

  • Divine Origin of Karma: Righteous action is not arbitrary but flows from divine wisdom.
  • Brahman as Veda and Ultimate Reality: The verse uses ‘Brahman’ to denote both sacred texts (Vedas) and the Supreme, Imperishable Reality (Akshara).
  • Yajna as Divine Manifestation: Selfless action (Yajna) is where the all-pervading Brahman is eternally present.
  • Cosmic Interconnectedness: All aspects of existence – from ultimate reality to human action – are linked in a sacred, sustaining chain.
