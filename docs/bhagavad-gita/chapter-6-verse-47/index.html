The Pinnacle of Yoga: Devotion as the Supreme Path to Union

Krishna reveals that among all yogis, the one who worships Him with unwavering faith and an inner self abiding in Him is the most perfectly united.

October 22, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Atma Samyama Yoga
6.47

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 47

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

योगिनामपि सर्वेषां मद्गतेनान्तरात्मना । श्रद्धावान्भजते यो मां स मे युक्ततमो मतः ॥ ४७ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

yoginām api sarveṣāṁ mad-gatenāntarātmanā | śraddhāvān bhajate yo māṁ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ || 47 ||

Translation

Among all yogis, he who, with profound faith, constantly abides in Me within his inner self, and worships Me, is considered by Me to be the most perfectly united in Yoga.

Explanation

Context

This forty-seventh verse serves as the concluding and crowning statement of the Sixth Chapter, “Atma Samyama Yoga,” or the “Yoga of Self-Control.” Throughout this chapter, Lord Krishna has meticulously detailed various aspects of Dhyana Yoga (meditation yoga), emphasizing the importance of controlling the mind and senses, practicing detachment, and striving for inner peace and equanimity. He has described the ideal yogi, their lifestyle, and the method of meditation. After elaborating on the arduous path of self-mastery and contemplative absorption, Krishna now reveals the ultimate secret and the supreme form of yoga, indicating where all these practices ultimately lead and what truly constitutes the highest union with the Divine.

Word-by-Word Meaning

Let’s break down the key terms in this powerful verse:

  • योगिनाम् (yoginām) अपि (api) सर्वेषाम् (sarveṣām): “Among all yogis, even.” This phrase establishes a comparative context, setting the stage for Krishna to declare the best among them.
  • मद्गतेन (mad-gatena): “Abiding in Me” or “with Me as the destination.” This refers to the mind or consciousness being completely fixed upon the Divine.
  • अन्तरात्मना (antar-ātmanā): “With the inner self,” “with the mind/heart.” This signifies that the connection is not superficial but from the deepest core of one’s being.
  • श्रद्धावान् (śraddhāvān): “Endowed with faith,” “full of faith.” Śraddhā is not blind belief but a deep, knowing conviction and trust in the spiritual path and the Divine.
  • भजते (bhajate): “Worships,” “serves,” “loves,” “devotes oneself to.” This verb encapsulates the essence of devotional service and loving adoration.
  • यः (yaḥ) माम् (mām): “He who (worships) Me.” Krishna directly refers to Himself as the object of this supreme devotion.
  • सः (saḥ) मे (me) युक्ततमः (yuktatamaḥ) मतः (mataḥ): “He is considered by Me to be the most perfectly united (yogi).” Yuktatamaḥ is the superlative form of yukta (united, connected in yoga), meaning the ‘most’ or ‘supremely’ united.

Deeper Understanding

This verse is a profound culmination of Krishna’s teachings on yoga. After guiding Arjuna through the intricacies of Raja Yoga and Dhyana Yoga, Krishna unveils the ultimate truth: the highest form of spiritual union is achieved through unwavering devotion (Bhakti) directed towards the Divine, coupled with profound faith (śraddhā).

What Krishna is teaching Arjuna here is that while self-control, meditation, and detachment are crucial steps on the spiritual path, they are ultimately means to an end. The ultimate goal is not merely to quiet the mind or achieve a state of void, but to establish a loving, conscious connection with the Supreme Being. The emphasis on mad-gatenāntarātmanā highlights that this connection must be from the very core of one’s being, not just an external ritual or intellectual exercise. The inner self (antarātman) must be absorbed in the Divine.

This verse subtly integrates elements of Karma Yoga (action performed as an offering), Jnana Yoga (understanding the nature of the Divine), and Dhyana Yoga (meditation leading to absorption) into the overarching framework of Bhakti Yoga. A yogi who is śraddhāvān (full of faith) and bhajate (worships) the Lord with their antarātman (inner self) fixed on Him (mad-gatena) is considered the yuktatamaḥ (most perfectly united). This implies that true union (yoga) is not just a state of mind, but a dynamic relationship of love and devotion. It doesn’t negate the value of other yogic paths but rather indicates that their ultimate purpose is to lead one to this state of loving devotion. The highest yogi is not just one who can control their breath or sit in deep meditation, but one whose heart is filled with love and unwavering faith for the Divine.

Practical Application

This verse offers immense practical guidance for modern readers seeking spiritual growth:

  1. Cultivating Conscious Presence: In our fast-paced lives, we can dedicate moments throughout the day to consciously remember the Divine, or our highest ideal. This could be through a brief prayer, a mindful breath, or simply acknowledging the sacredness in everyday tasks. The idea is to make the inner self mad-gatena – always moving towards and abiding in the Divine, even amidst daily activities.
  2. Infusing Actions with Devotion: Transform mundane tasks into acts of service or offerings. Whether it’s cooking a meal, helping a colleague, or caring for family, approaching these actions with a spirit of love, gratitude, and dedication, as if offering them to a higher power, elevates them from mere chores to acts of devotion. This builds śraddhā (faith) by connecting our efforts to a larger purpose.
  3. Practicing Heart-Centered Meditation: While intellectual understanding and physical postures are valuable, this verse encourages a meditation practice rooted in the heart. Instead of solely focusing on emptying the mind, one can meditate on divine qualities, a sacred image, or a mantra with deep feeling and love, allowing the antarātman to connect directly with the Divine.

Reflection

  • What does śraddhā (faith) truly mean to me, and how can I deepen it in my spiritual journey?
  • How can I consciously bring my “inner self” to abide in the Divine throughout my day?
  • In what ways can I transform my actions into expressions of loving devotion?

Key Concepts

  • Bhakti Yoga (Devotion): The supreme path of loving devotion to the Divine.
  • Śraddhā (Faith): A deep, unwavering conviction and trust in the spiritual path and the Supreme Being.
  • Antarātman (Inner Self): The core consciousness or heart from which true spiritual connection emanates.
  • Yuktatama (Supreme Yogi): The highest state of union with the Divine, achieved through loving devotion and faith.
