Arjuna's Ultimate Trust: Seeking Divine Clarity

Arjuna implores Krishna to resolve his profound doubt about the fate of the unsuccessful yogi, recognizing only Krishna's unique wisdom.

October 14, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Atma Samyama Yoga
6.39

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 39

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

एतन्मे संशयं कृष्ण छेत्तुमर्हस्यशेषतः । त्वदन्यः संशयस्यास्य छेत्ता न ह्युपपद्यते ॥ ३९ ॥

Transliteration (IAST)

etanme saṁśayaṁ kṛṣṇa chettumarhasyaśeṣataḥ | tvadanyaḥ saṁśayasyāsya chettā na hyupapadyate || 39 ||

Translation

O Krishna, it is proper for You to completely dispel this doubt of mine; for no one else can be found who can dispel this doubt.

Explanation

Context

This verse marks a pivotal moment in the dialogue between Arjuna and Krishna in the sixth chapter, ‘Atma Samyama Yoga’ (The Yoga of Self-Control). Having previously described the challenges of controlling the restless mind and the arduous path of yoga, Arjuna has just raised a profound and compassionate concern in verses 37 and 38. He questions the fate of an aspirant who begins the spiritual journey with faith but fails to achieve perfection due to a lack of sustained effort or control over the mind, eventually falling away from the path before reaching the goal. Arjuna fears such a person might lose both worldly and spiritual gains, becoming like a ‘rent cloud’ – neither belonging to the earth nor reaching the heavens. This verse (6.39) is Arjuna’s earnest and humble plea to Krishna, acknowledging Krishna as the ultimate authority and the only one capable of dispelling such a deep-seated doubt. It underscores Arjuna’s complete faith and surrender, setting the stage for Krishna’s reassuring and comprehensive answer in the verses that follow.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • एतन्मे (etanme): “this of mine” – referring to the doubt just expressed.
  • संशयम् (saṁśayam): “doubt” – indicating a profound uncertainty or perplexity.
  • कृष्ण (kṛṣṇa): “O Krishna” – a direct address, signifying reverence and intimacy with his divine charioteer and friend.
  • छेत्तुम् (chettum): “to cut, to dispel, to remove” – implying a decisive and complete eradication of the doubt.
  • अर्हसि (arhasi): “You are worthy, it is proper for You” – acknowledging Krishna’s unique qualification and ability.
  • अशेषतः (aśeṣataḥ): “completely, without remainder, entirely” – emphasizing the need for a thorough resolution, leaving no lingering questions.
  • त्वदन्यः (tvadanyaḥ): “other than You” – highlighting Krishna’s singularity as the source of ultimate wisdom.
  • संशयस्यास्य (saṁśayasyāsya): “of this doubt” – reiterating the specific nature of the inquiry.
  • छेत्ता (chettā): “dispeller, remover” – one who can resolve the doubt.
  • न (na): “not”
  • हि (hi): “indeed, certainly” – an emphatic particle.
  • उपपद्यते (upapadyate): “is found, exists, is possible” – implying that no one else possesses the knowledge or authority to provide such a definitive answer.

Deeper Understanding

This verse beautifully illustrates the ideal guru-disciple relationship and the critical role of an enlightened teacher in one’s spiritual journey. Arjuna, despite being a mighty warrior and intellectual, humbly admits his profound doubt (saṁśaya). In Vedic thought, saṁśaya (doubt) is a significant impediment to spiritual progress. It can paralyze action, erode faith, and prevent one from fully committing to the path. Arjuna recognizes that only a fully realized being, like Krishna, possesses the clarity and wisdom to completely eradicate such a fundamental doubt, leaving no room for further confusion.

Krishna, as the Supreme Lord, is not just a friend or charioteer but the ultimate source of all knowledge. Arjuna’s statement, “no one else can be found who can dispel this doubt,” is not hyperbole but an acknowledgment of Krishna’s divine authority (īśvara-tva) and omniscience. It reflects Arjuna’s complete śaraṇāgati (surrender) and śraddhā (faith) in his divine guide. This act of surrender is itself a powerful yoga, opening the disciple to receive the highest wisdom. The request for complete dispelling (aśeṣataḥ) shows Arjuna’s earnestness to resolve the issue definitively, not just superficially.

This verse relates to the broader Gita themes of seeking knowledge (jñāna), the importance of a spiritual preceptor (guru), and the process of spiritual inquiry (jijñāsā). It also reinforces the idea that true spiritual understanding comes from a divine or highly enlightened source, not merely from intellectual speculation or worldly knowledge.

Practical Application

  1. Seek Authentic Guidance for Deep Questions: In modern life, we are bombarded with information, often superficial or conflicting. When facing profound existential, ethical, or spiritual doubts, emulate Arjuna. Seek out genuine experts, spiritual teachers, or time-tested wisdom traditions that have a track record of providing deep, consistent insights, rather than relying on fleeting trends or unqualified opinions. This means discerning reliable sources from unreliable ones.
  2. Cultivate Humility in Learning: Arjuna, despite his status, unhesitatingly admits his doubt and asks for help. In our pursuit of knowledge, especially spiritual knowledge, adopting a humble attitude allows us to truly learn and absorb wisdom. Letting go of ego-driven assumptions and acknowledging our limitations creates space for profound understanding and growth. This openness is vital for any true inquiry.
  3. Address Doubts Systematically and Completely: Don’t let significant doubts fester. Unresolved questions can create anxiety, hinder progress, and erode conviction in any endeavor, particularly spiritual ones. Like Arjuna, make a conscious effort to seek complete clarity (aśeṣataḥ). This might involve diligent study, contemplation, meditation, or direct consultation with a trusted mentor until the doubt is entirely dispelled.

Reflection

  • When faced with significant doubts or uncertainties in life, who or what sources do you typically turn to for guidance? Are these sources truly capable of offering complete resolution?
  • How comfortable are you admitting your lack of understanding or expressing your deepest doubts to others, particularly those you respect?
  • Consider a persistent doubt you hold. What steps could you take to seek a complete and definitive resolution, following Arjuna’s example of earnest inquiry?

Key Concepts

  • Guru-Disciple Relationship: Emphasizes the crucial role of an enlightened teacher in spiritual progress.
  • Elimination of Doubt (Saṁśaya-Chedana): Highlights the necessity of resolving profound doubts for spiritual advancement.
  • Divine Authority: Acknowledges Krishna as the ultimate source of wisdom and truth.
  • Spiritual Inquiry: Illustrates the sincere and humble pursuit of knowledge to overcome obstacles on the path.
