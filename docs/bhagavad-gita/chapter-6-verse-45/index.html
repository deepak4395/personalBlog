The Persistent Yogi's Journey to Supreme Goal

This verse assures that a diligent yogi, even if not perfect in one life, gradually purifies sins over many births to finally achieve the supreme spiritual destination.

October 20, 2026
#Bhagavad Gita #Atma Samyama Yoga

Chapter 6: Atma Samyama Yoga

Verse 45

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

प्रयत्नाद्यतमानस्तु योगी संशुद्धकिल्बिषः। अनेकजन्मसंसिद्धस्ततो याति परां गतिम्॥४५॥

Transliteration (IAST)

prayatnādyatamānastu yogī saṁśuddhakilbiṣaḥ | anekajanmasaṁsiddhastato yāti parāṁ gatim ||45||

Translation

But the yogi, striving assiduously, purified of sins, attains perfection over many births, and then reaches the supreme goal.

Explanation

Context

This verse from the Bhagavad Gita Chapter 6, ‘Atma Samyama Yoga’ (The Yoga of Self-Control), offers profound reassurance to the spiritual seeker. It directly follows Krishna’s comforting words to Arjuna in verses 40-44, where he assures that a ‘fallen’ yogi – one who begins the spiritual path but does not complete it in a single lifetime – does not perish. Instead, such a soul is reborn into favorable circumstances, often into a family of pure and prosperous people, or even into a family of yogis, to continue their spiritual journey. Verse 45 concludes this line of thought by explaining how this accumulated spiritual merit unfolds across multiple lives, emphasizing the cumulative power of persistent effort and the ultimate, inevitable attainment of the supreme goal.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • प्रयत्नाद्यतमानस्तु (prayatnādyatamānastu): This powerful compound signifies consistent and diligent effort. prayatnāt means ‘by effort,’ ‘assiduously,’ or ‘with great endeavor.’ yatamānaḥ means ‘striving,’ ‘endeavoring,’ or ‘exerting oneself.’ tu acts as an emphasis, meaning ‘but’ or ‘indeed.’ Together, it highlights the continuous, conscious, and sincere exertion required on the spiritual path.
  • योगी (yogī): A practitioner of yoga; one who strives for union with the Divine or the ultimate reality.
  • संशुद्धकिल्बिषः (saṁśuddhakilbiṣaḥ): This describes the state of purification. saṁśuddha means ‘completely purified’ or ‘thoroughly cleansed.’ kilbiṣaḥ refers to ‘sins,’ ‘faults,’ ‘impurities,’ or ‘demerits.’ This phrase indicates the gradual removal of karmic impediments, mental defilements, and negative tendencies through spiritual practice.
  • अनेकजन्मसंसिद्धः (anekajanmasaṁsiddhaḥ): This is a crucial concept, referring to spiritual perfection over multiple lifetimes. aneka means ‘many.’ janma means ‘births’ or ‘lifetimes.’ saṁsiddhaḥ means ‘perfected,’ ‘accomplished,’ or ‘attained perfection.’ It underscores that spiritual evolution is often a journey spanning numerous incarnations.
  • ततो (tato): ‘Then,’ or ‘thereafter,’ indicating a subsequent outcome.
  • याति (yāti): ‘Attains,’ ‘goes to,’ or ‘reaches.’
  • परां गतिम् (parāṁ gatim): ‘The supreme goal’ or ‘the highest destination.’ parām means ‘supreme,’ ‘highest,’ or ‘ultimate.’ gatim refers to ‘goal,’ ‘destination,’ ‘state,’ or ‘liberation.’ In the context of the Gita, this means moksha (liberation from the cycle of birth and death), union with Brahman, or dwelling in the highest spiritual abode.

Deeper Understanding

Krishna’s teaching in this verse is a profound testament to the efficacy of spiritual effort and the compassionate nature of the cosmic law. He assures Arjuna, and by extension all seekers, that no genuine spiritual endeavor is ever wasted. Even if one does not achieve complete self-realization in a single lifetime, the accumulated merit, purified consciousness, and cultivated spiritual tendencies (samskaras) are carried forward.

This verse highlights several key concepts:

  1. The Power of Persistence (Prayatna): The emphasis is on ‘striving assiduously.’ Spiritual progress is not about a single dramatic event but a continuous, unwavering dedication to practice and self-improvement. It’s the consistent effort, day in and day out, that gradually transforms the inner being.

  2. Gradual Purification (Saṁśuddhakilbiṣaḥ): Yoga and spiritual practices are processes of purification. Through meditation, selfless action, devotion, and wisdom, the layers of ignorance, ego, and karmic impurities are systematically removed. This cleansing allows the inherent purity of the soul to shine forth. This purification is not a one-time event but a continuous refinement.

  3. Multi-Life Spiritual Progression (Anekajanmasaṁsiddhaḥ): This is a cornerstone of Hindu philosophy – the concept of reincarnation and karmic evolution. The verse explicitly states that perfection is attained over ‘many births.’ This alleviates the pressure of achieving ultimate liberation in a single lifetime, acknowledging the deep-seated nature of conditioning and the time it takes to transcend it. Each life offers a fresh opportunity, building upon the spiritual foundation laid in previous ones.

  4. The Inevitable Supreme Goal (Parāṁ Gatim): Krishna assures that the diligent yogi will certainly reach the supreme goal. This is not a ‘maybe’ but a definitive promise. The path might be long, spanning many lifetimes, but the destination is assured for those who sincerely strive. This ultimate goal is liberation from suffering, the realization of one’s true divine nature, and union with the Absolute.

This verse connects deeply with the themes of karma yoga (performing action without attachment to results, knowing the effort itself is purifying), bhakti yoga (faith in the divine process), and jnana yoga (understanding the ultimate truth). It reinforces the idea that spiritual evolution is a cumulative process, guided by divine grace and the law of karma, ultimately leading to moksha.

Practical Application

  1. Embrace the Long-Term View: Don’t get discouraged by setbacks or slow progress on your spiritual path. Understand that spiritual growth is a marathon, not a sprint, potentially spanning many lifetimes. Every small effort – a moment of mindfulness, an act of kindness, a sincere prayer – contributes to your cumulative spiritual evolution. This perspective fosters patience and resilience.
  2. Focus on Consistent Effort, Not Just Results: Instead of agonizing over immediate perfect outcomes, commit to consistent daily practices. Whether it’s meditation, selfless service, studying scriptures, or conscious breathing, regularity is more important than intensity. The purification and perfection mentioned in the verse happen gradually through sustained engagement.
  3. Cultivate Self-Compassion and Forgiveness: Recognizing that purification takes time, be patient and compassionate with yourself. When you falter or experience spiritual dryness, acknowledge it without harsh self-criticism. Forgive your shortcomings and gently redirect your focus back to your practice. This allows for sustained effort without burnout and fosters a more loving relationship with your own spiritual journey.

  • What specific, consistent ‘prayatna’ (effort) can you commit to in your daily life, knowing its cumulative, long-term effect?
  • How can you cultivate more patience and self-compassion towards your own spiritual journey, understanding its multi-lifespan nature?
