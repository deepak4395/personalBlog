The Essence of Existence: Krishna's All-Pervading Presence

Krishna reveals His immanent nature, identifying Himself as the fundamental essence, light, sacred sound, and power in all creation.

Chapter 7: Jnana Vijnana Yoga

Verse 8

Sanskrit (Devanagari)

रसोऽहमप्सु कौन्तेय प्रभास्मि शशिसूर्ययोः। प्रणवः सर्ववेदेषु शब्दः खे पौरुषं नृषु॥

Transliteration (IAST)

Raso'ham apsu kaunteya prabhāsmi śaśi-sūryayoḥ. Praṇavaḥ sarva-vedeṣu śabdaḥ khe pauruṣaṁ nṛṣu.

Translation

O son of Kunti, I am the taste in water, the light in the sun and moon, the sacred syllable Om in all Vedic mantras, the sound in ether, and the virility in men.

Explanation

Context

This verse continues Krishna’s profound discourse on His supreme nature (Para Prakriti) as the ultimate reality underlying the entire universe. In the preceding verses of Chapter 7, Krishna has already introduced His two natures – the lower (Apara Prakriti) comprising material elements and the higher (Para Prakriti) which is the conscious, life-giving principle. He declared that nothing is superior to Him and that everything rests upon Him like pearls strung on a thread. With verse 7.8, Krishna embarks on a series of vivid examples (extending up to 7.11) to illustrate how His divine presence is not just abstract but tangibly present as the essential quality within various aspects of creation. This approach helps Arjuna, and indeed all seekers, to move beyond intellectual understanding (Jnana) to direct realization (Vijnana) by perceiving the divine in the seemingly mundane.

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • रसः (rasaḥ): Taste, essence, flavor. This refers to the defining quality that makes something what it is.
  • अहम् (aham): I (Krishna). This is a direct identification.
  • अप्सु (apsu): In waters. (Locative plural, indicating ‘in’).
  • कौन्तेय (kaunteya): O son of Kunti (a name for Arjuna). A term of endearment and address.
  • प्रभा (prabhā): Light, splendor, radiance. The illuminating quality.
  • अस्मि (asmi): I am. Another direct identification.
  • शशिसूर्ययोः (śaśi-sūryayoḥ): Of the moon and the sun. (Dual genitive, indicating ‘of both’).
  • प्रणवः (praṇavaḥ): The sacred syllable Om (AUM). This is considered the primordial sound of creation.
  • सर्ववेदेषु (sarva-vedeṣu): In all Vedas. (Locative plural, indicating ‘in all’). The Vedas are the foundational scriptures of Hinduism.
  • शब्दः (śabdaḥ): Sound. The fundamental vibration.
  • खे (khe): In ether, in space. (Locative singular of ‘kha’, meaning space or sky). Ether is often considered the medium for sound.
  • पौरुषम् (pauruṣam): Manliness, virility, inherent capacity, strength, endeavor, courage. It refers to the intrinsic power or potential within human beings.
  • नृषु (nṛṣu): In men. (Locative plural of ‘nṛ’, meaning man or human).

Through these examples, Krishna signifies that He is not merely the creator of these things but the very essence, the life, the defining quality that makes them what they are and enables their function.

Deeper Understanding

This verse is a profound statement on the immanence of God. Krishna is not a distant, transcendent creator, but the very fabric and essence of existence. By identifying with qualities rather than just forms, He teaches us to look beyond the superficial appearance of things and perceive the divine substratum that gives them their meaning and function. He is the ‘rasa’ that makes water refreshing and life-sustaining, the ‘prabha’ that illuminates the world and sustains life on Earth. These are not merely poetic analogies; they are pointers to the ultimate reality that pervades every aspect of our experience.

The inclusion of ‘Praṇavaḥ sarva-vedeṣu’ (the sacred syllable Om in all Vedic mantras) is highly significant. Om is considered the primordial sound, the very vibration of creation, and the sonic representation of Brahman, the Absolute Reality. By declaring Himself as Om, Krishna asserts His identity with the ultimate spiritual truth, the source of all knowledge and spiritual power. Similarly, ‘śabdaḥ khe’ (the sound in ether) reinforces the idea of pervasive vibration and energy, indicating His presence in the subtlest elements of the universe.

Finally, ‘pauruṣaṁ nṛṣu’ (the virility/inherent capacity in men) connects the divine directly to human potential. This ‘paurusham’ is not just physical strength but encompasses inherent courage, drive, resilience, moral fortitude, and the capacity for righteous action (dharma). It is the divine spark that enables human endeavor, creativity, and the pursuit of higher ideals. This reminds us that our true strength and potential are rooted in the divine within us.

This teaching is a form of practical meditation. By consciously recognizing Krishna in these fundamental aspects of reality, one cultivates a continuous awareness of the divine presence. This is a direct path towards Jnana Vijnana – gaining both intellectual knowledge and direct realization of the Supreme Being.

Practical Application

  1. Mindful Appreciation: Practice seeing the divine in your daily life. When you drink water, consciously appreciate its refreshing taste as a manifestation of Krishna. When you feel the warmth of the sun or see the moon’s gentle glow, acknowledge it as His light. This transforms mundane activities into opportunities for spiritual connection and gratitude.
  2. Cultivating Inner Strength: Recognize that the ‘paurusham’ – the inherent capacity, strength, and drive within you – is a divine gift. Use this strength wisely for righteous actions (dharma), to overcome challenges, to serve others, and to pursue your higher purpose. This fosters self-belief and purposeful living, reminding you that your potential is divinely endowed.
  3. Engaging with Sacred Sound: If inclined, engage in chanting or meditating on the sacred syllable Om. Understanding that Om is Krishna’s essence in the Vedas can deepen your practice, helping you connect with the primordial vibration of creation and fostering inner peace and spiritual insight.

Reflection

  • How can I consciously cultivate a deeper awareness of the divine presence in my everyday experiences, using the examples Krishna provides?
  • What aspects of ‘paurusham’ (inherent capacity, strength, courage) resonate most with me, and how can I utilize these qualities for my spiritual growth and the benefit of others?
  • How does perceiving Krishna as the essence of all things change my perspective on the world and my place within it?

Key Concepts

  • Immanence of God: The concept that God is present within creation, not just separate from it.
  • Essence (Rasa): Krishna is the fundamental, defining quality that gives things their unique nature and function.
  • Pranava (Om): The sacred syllable Om, representing the primordial sound of creation and the essence of Vedic wisdom, directly identified with Krishna.
  • Divine Spark in Humanity (Paurusham): The inherent capacity, strength, courage, and drive in human beings are seen as a direct manifestation of the divine.
