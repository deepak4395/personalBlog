## Explanation

### Context
In Chapter 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled Jnana Vijnana Yoga, Krishna delves into the nature of knowledge and wisdom. This chapter is pivotal as it distinguishes between two types of knowledge: the knowledge that leads to liberation and the knowledge that is rooted in ignorance. In this specific verse, Krishna emphasizes the paradoxical nature of wisdom and ignorance, illustrating how both emerge from the divine.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **vidyā**: knowledge, particularly spiritual knowledge
- **avidhyā**: ignorance, or lack of spiritual knowledge
- **asmin**: in this
- **hi**: indeed, emphasizes the statement
- **sarveindriyāṇām**: of all senses (indicating totality)
- **sannyastāḥ**: restrained, controlled, or surrendered
- **avijñāya**: by understanding or knowing
- **yato**: from whom, from which
- **bhūyaḥ**: more than, greater
- **śāntaḥ**: peaceful
- **asmi**: I am
- **tataḥ**: from that, therefore
- **sadā**: always, perpetually

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna articulates a profound truth about the nature of existence and consciousness. He identifies Himself with both knowledge and ignorance, suggesting that both are part of His divine play. The verse teaches Arjuna—and through him, all seekers—that understanding the dualities of life can lead to a deeper realization of the Supreme. 

Krishna's assertion, "I am the knowledge of the wise," indicates that true wisdom is aligned with understanding the self and the universe. Conversely, His identification with ignorance implies that those who are unaware of their true nature are also expressions of His will, albeit in a more limited scope. This duality is essential for the pursuit of dharma, as it highlights the necessity of transcending both knowledge and ignorance through yoga and self-realization.

The themes of peace and surrender are crucial as well. Krishna states that He is the peace of the peaceful, suggesting that true tranquility arises from understanding the transient nature of worldly experiences. Therefore, the verse encapsulates the essence of yoga, which is not merely about physical postures but about achieving inner balance and harmony through knowledge and devotion.

### Practical Application
1. **Cultivating Knowledge**: Modern readers can apply the teachings of this verse by actively seeking knowledge that enhances their understanding of self and the universe. Engaging in practices such as meditation, reading spiritual texts, and attending lectures can help one discern between ignorance and true wisdom.

2. **Embracing Duality**: Understanding that both knowledge and ignorance are aspects of the divine allows individuals to approach life’s challenges with greater compassion. Instead of judging others for their ignorance, one can recognize it as part of a greater journey toward enlightenment.

3. **Finding Peace**: By recognizing Krishna as the source of peace, individuals can practice mindfulness and acceptance. When faced with stress or conflict, they can remind themselves to return to a state of peace by centering their thoughts on divine presence.
