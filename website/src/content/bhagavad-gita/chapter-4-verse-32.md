## Explanation

### Context
This verse serves as a crucial summary and transition point within the fourth chapter, "Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga," which emphasizes the path of knowledge and action. Prior to this verse, Lord Krishna has meticulously enumerated various types of sacrifices (`yajñas`) that people undertake: sacrifices involving material possessions (dravya-yajña), austerities (tapo-yajña), yogic practices (yoga-yajña), self-study and knowledge (svādhyāya-yajña and jñāna-yajña), and control of senses and vital breath (prāṇāyāma-yajña). Having presented this diverse array of spiritual endeavors, Krishna now consolidates their essence, setting the stage for the profound revelation that the sacrifice of knowledge (`jñāna-yajña`) is supreme, which he will elaborate on in the subsequent verses. This verse connects the seemingly disparate forms of spiritual practice, highlighting their common origin and ultimate purpose.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **एवं (evaṃ)**: Thus, in this manner. It refers back to the various types of sacrifices previously described.
*   **बहुविधा (bahuvidhā)**: Many kinds, manifold, diverse. This underscores the vast range of spiritual practices.
*   **यज्ञाः (yajñāḥ)**: Sacrifices, spiritual endeavors, acts of devotion or offering. In the Gita, `yajña` transcends mere ritualistic fire sacrifices and encompasses any dedicated action performed with a higher purpose.
*   **वितताः (vitatāḥ)**: Spread out, extended, performed, widely prevalent.
*   **ब्रह्मणो मुखे (brahmaṇo mukhe)**: This is a key phrase. It can be interpreted in a few ways:
    *   "In the mouth of Brahman": Here, `Brahman` refers to the Vedas, the eternal spiritual scriptures, which are considered the "breath" or "word" of the Supreme Reality. So, these sacrifices are prescribed and taught by the Vedas.
    *   "Originating from Brahman": `Brahman` as the Supreme Reality, indicating that these spiritual paths ultimately emanate from the divine source itself.
    *   "Leading to Brahman": The ultimate goal of these sacrifices is the realization of Brahman.
*   **कर्मजान् (karmajān)**: Born of action, originating from action. This is the central revelation of the verse – all these diverse sacrifices, regardless of their form, are fundamentally rooted in action.
*   **विद्धि तान् सर्वान् (viddhi tān sarvān)**: Know them all. Understand this fundamental truth about them.
*   **एवं ज्ञात्वा (evaṃ jñātvā)**: Knowing this, having understood this truth. The emphasis is on intellectual and spiritual comprehension.
*   **विमोक्ष्यसे (vimokṣyase)**: You shall be liberated, you will be freed. This is the ultimate promise and reward for such understanding.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna's teaching in this verse is profound, unifying the diverse paths of spiritual endeavor under a single principle. By stating that "many kinds of sacrifices are spread out originating from the Veda," he acknowledges the validity and divine sanction of various practices. However, the pivotal insight comes with the declaration, "Know them all to be born of action." This means that whether one is performing elaborate rituals, practicing severe austerities, engaging in deep meditation, or studying scriptures, all these activities are fundamentally `karma`, or action.

This concept is central to Karma Yoga. It’s not about renouncing action, but understanding its true nature and performing it with the right attitude. The `brahmaṇo mukhe` aspect implies that these actions are not random or self-serving, but are guided by divine wisdom (Vedas) and directed towards the Supreme. When actions are undertaken as `yajña` – as an offering, a selfless endeavor, or a means to connect with the divine – they transcend their mundane nature.

The verse bridges the apparent gap between ritualistic action, asceticism, and intellectual pursuit. All these are forms of `karma` designed to purify the mind, refine the senses, and ultimately lead to `jñāna` (knowledge). The ultimate goal, `vimokṣyase` (liberation), is achieved not just by performing these actions, but by understanding their true nature and purpose. It's the `jñātvā` (knowing/understanding) that unlocks liberation. This knowledge is the realization that the external forms of sacrifice are merely vehicles, and the true sacrifice lies in the purification of the self and the realization of one's oneness with Brahman. This understanding frees one from the bondage of `karma` and its fruits, leading to spiritual freedom.

### Practical Application
1.  **Transforming Daily Life into Sacrifice**: This verse encourages us to view our everyday actions, responsibilities, and work not as mere tasks but as potential forms of `yajña`. Whether it's doing our job with dedication, caring for our family, serving the community, or engaging in personal growth, if performed with a sense of offering, duty, and detachment from results, these actions become spiritual practices. For instance, a software developer can view writing code as an act of creation and service, performed with mindfulness and excellence, rather than just a means to a paycheck.
2.  **Unity in Diversity of Spiritual Paths**: In a world often fragmented by religious and spiritual differences, this teaching offers a unifying perspective. It suggests that various spiritual disciplines – be it prayer, meditation, yoga, service, or scriptural study – are all valid "sacrifices born of action," ultimately aiming for the same liberation. This understanding fosters tolerance and respect for different paths, recognizing that the essence is the internal transformation and understanding, not just the external form. A person practicing mindfulness meditation and another performing devotional chanting are both engaging in `karmajān yajña`, seeking the same ultimate truth.
3.  **Mindful Intention Over Ritualistic Adherence**: The emphasis on "knowing this, you shall be liberated" highlights the importance of conscious understanding and intention. It's not enough to mechanically follow rituals or practices; one must comprehend the underlying spiritual principles. This encourages a deeper, more reflective approach to one's spiritual journey, prompting self-inquiry into *why* one performs certain actions and *what* one hopes to achieve, rather than just *how* to do them. It empowers individuals to find spiritual meaning in their unique circumstances, rather than being confined to rigid external forms.

## Key Concepts

*   **Universal Nature of Sacrifice (Yajña)**: All spiritual efforts, regardless of form, are forms of sacrifice.
*   **Action as the Root of Spiritual Practice (Karmajān)**: All sacrifices originate from action, emphasizing the importance of right action.
*   **Liberation Through Understanding (Jñāna)**: True freedom comes from comprehending the underlying principles of action and sacrifice.
*   **Divine Origin of Knowledge (Brahmaṇo Mukhe)**: The spiritual paths are sanctioned by divine wisdom, guiding humanity towards higher truths.
