## Explanation

### Context
This verse, Bhagavad Gita Chapter 2, Verse 57, continues Krishna's profound description of the *Sthitaprajña*, the person of steady wisdom, a central theme introduced by Arjuna's question in verse 54. Having previously elaborated on the Sthitaprajña's ability to abandon all desires, to be free from anxiety and anger, and to control the senses, Krishna now turns to their interaction with the external world. This verse specifically addresses how such an enlightened individual reacts to the dualities of life – the pleasant and the unpleasant, the good and the bad. It builds upon the idea of inner stability, demonstrating that true wisdom is not merely intellectual understanding but a deeply ingrained state of being that remains undisturbed by the fluctuating nature of external circumstances.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **यः (yaḥ)**: He who
*   **सर्वत्र (sarvatra)**: everywhere, in all circumstances, regarding everything
*   **अभिस्रेहः (abhisrehaḥ)**: This word, as provided, literally means "attachment" or "strong affection." However, it is crucial to note that almost all authoritative commentaries and standard editions of the Bhagavad Gita use the reading **अनभिस्रेहः (anabhisnehaḥ)**, meaning "without attachment." The quality of a Sthitaprajña is consistently defined by their freedom from attachment, as it is attachment that fuels our experience of joy and sorrow from external events. For philosophical consistency with the Gita's teachings on the Sthitaprajña, this phrase is understood to mean 'one who is without attachment' or 'one whose attachments no longer bind them and dictate their reactions.'
*   **तत्तत् (tattat)**: that and that, whatever (implying various events or objects)
*   **प्राप्य (prāpya)**: having obtained, having met with, having encountered
*   **शुभ (śubha)**: good, auspicious, pleasant, favorable
*   **अशुभम् (aśubham)**: evil, inauspicious, unpleasant, unfavorable
*   **न अभिनन्दति (na abhinandati)**: neither rejoices, does not delight in, does not exult
*   **न द्वेष्टि (na dveṣṭi)**: nor dislikes, does not hate, does not resent
*   **तस्य (tasya)**: his
*   **प्रज्ञा (prajñā)**: wisdom, intellect, understanding, discernment
*   **प्रतिष्ठिता (pratiṣṭhitā)**: fixed, steady, established, firm, unwavering

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna's teaching here is profoundly liberating. The essence of this verse lies in the Sthitaprajña's unwavering equanimity (*samattva*) in the face of life's inherent dualities (*dvandvas*). For an ordinary individual, good fortune brings elation, and misfortune brings despair. This cycle of joy and sorrow is perpetuated by *attachment*—our identification with objects, outcomes, and experiences. When we are attached to a particular result, its realization brings temporary happiness, and its absence or the opposite outcome leads to suffering.

The Sthitaprajña, however, has transcended this binding attachment. Their inner state is not contingent upon external events. This is not to say they become emotionless or indifferent; rather, their inner peace is so deeply rooted that it cannot be shaken by the transient nature of the world. They understand that both pleasure and pain are fleeting, and neither defines their true self, which is eternal and unchanging. This understanding is the hallmark of *fixed wisdom* (*prajñā pratiṣṭhitā*).

This teaching is deeply connected to *Karma Yoga*, the path of selfless action. A Sthitaprajña performs their duties without attachment to the results, understanding that the outcome is not entirely within their control and does not affect their inherent worth. By letting go of the desire for specific results (good or bad), they free themselves from the emotional rollercoaster of life. This profound detachment is not apathy but a higher form of engagement, where actions are performed with clarity and dedication, yet without the personal identification that leads to suffering.

### Practical Application
1.  **Mindful Observation of Reactions:** In daily life, consciously observe your reactions to events. When you receive good news, notice if you become overly elated. When faced with a setback, notice if you become overly dejected. The goal is not to suppress these natural human emotions but to recognize that they are transient and do not need to define your inner state. By observing without judgment, you create a space between the event and your reaction, allowing wisdom to guide your response.
2.  **Detachment in Professional and Personal Endeavors:** Whether at work, in a creative pursuit, or in relationships, invest your best effort but practice detaching from specific outcomes. If a project succeeds, appreciate it without ego; if it fails, learn from it without despair. Your worth as an individual is not determined by external success or failure, but by your integrity, effort, and inner state.
3.  **Cultivating Inner Stability:** Engage in practices like meditation, mindfulness, or contemplative prayer that help cultivate an inner sanctuary of peace. Regularly remind yourself that true happiness and fulfillment come from within, independent of external praise or criticism, gain or loss. This inner stability allows you to navigate life's inevitable ups and downs with grace and resilience.

*   Can I identify areas in my life where I allow external events to dictate my inner peace?
*   What would it mean to truly experience joy without clinging, and sorrow without despair?

## Key Concepts
*   **Equanimity (Samattva):** Maintaining mental calmness and composure in difficult or fluctuating situations, seeing all experiences with an equal eye.
*   **Non-attachment (Vairagya):** Freedom from desire and craving for objects, outcomes, or experiences, understanding their transient nature.
*   **Steadfast Wisdom (Sthitaprajña):** The state of a person whose intellect and understanding are firmly established in spiritual truth, leading to unshakeable inner peace.
*   **Transcending Dualities (Dvandva-atita):** Moving beyond the pairs of opposites (pleasure/pain, gain/loss, honor/dishonor) that typically bind human beings to suffering.
