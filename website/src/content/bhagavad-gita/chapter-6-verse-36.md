## Explanation

### Context
This profound verse (6.36) comes as Lord Krishna's direct response to Arjuna's heartfelt lament in verses 6.33-34. Arjuna, having understood the goal of Yoga as a state of unwavering tranquility and equanimity, expresses his profound doubt about its feasibility. He compares the mind to a restless, turbulent wind, asserting that controlling it seems more challenging than controlling the wind itself. Krishna, with His infinite wisdom and compassion, acknowledges Arjuna's very human struggle. In this verse, He validates the difficulty but immediately offers hope and a clear path forward, setting the stage for the practical guidance on taming the mind that follows.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **असंयतात्मना (asaṃyatātmanā)**: For one whose self or mind is uncontrolled/undisciplined. `Asaṃyata` means uncontrolled, and `ātmanā` here refers to the mind or inner self.
*   **योगो (yogo)**: Yoga, referring to the state of union with the Divine, inner peace, or the spiritual practice itself.
*   **दुष्प्राप (duṣprāpa)**: Difficult to attain, hard to achieve.
*   **इति (iti)**: Thus, so.
*   **मे (me)**: My.
*   **मतिः (matiḥ)**: Conviction, opinion, belief.
*   **वश्यात्मना (vaśyātmanā)**: By one whose self/mind is controlled, disciplined, or submissive.
*   **तु (tu)**: But, on the other hand.
*   **यतता (yatatā)**: By one striving, making effort, endeavoring.
*   **शक्यः (śakyaḥ)**: Possible, capable of being done.
*   **अवाप्तुम् (avāptum)**: To attain, to achieve.
*   **उपायतः (upāyataḥ)**: By proper means, through suitable methods or practices.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna's response is both empathetic and empowering. He doesn't dismiss Arjuna's concern but confirms that indeed, for a person whose mind is not disciplined, the state of Yoga – that profound inner calm and connection – is exceedingly difficult to achieve. This validation is crucial; it tells us that the struggle with a restless mind is universal and not a sign of personal failing. It sets a realistic expectation for the spiritual journey.

However, the verse doesn't end there. The crucial turning point is `tu` (but). Krishna immediately pivots to the solution: `vaśyātmanā tu yatatā śakyo'vāptumupāyataḥ` – *but by one who is self-controlled and strives by proper means, it is possible to attain it*. This is the essence of His teaching regarding mental mastery.

Here, Krishna highlights three indispensable elements for success in Yoga:
1.  **Self-Control (Vaśyātmanā)**: This refers to the cultivation of inner discipline, where one is not merely a slave to their impulses, desires, and the whims of the mind. It is about actively guiding the mind rather than being driven by it.
2.  **Persistent Effort (Yatatā)**: The path to self-mastery is not for the faint-hearted or the inconsistent. It demands continuous, dedicated effort (`abhyāsa` as mentioned in 6.35). Spiritual growth is an active process, not a passive one.
3.  **Proper Means (Upāyataḥ)**: This is a critical insight. It's not enough to just try hard; one must also employ the right methods. These 'proper means' include practices like meditation (dhyana), detachment (vairagya), ethical conduct (yama and niyama in Ashtanga Yoga), study of scriptures, selfless action, and seeking guidance from experienced teachers. It implies a skillful, intelligent approach to spiritual practice.

This verse beautifully synthesizes the themes of effort and grace (through proper means) in the Gita. It assures us that while the journey is challenging, it is not impossible for those who are willing to commit to discipline and intelligent practice. It relates directly to *dharma* in the sense of fulfilling one's spiritual duty to oneself, and to *karma yoga* by emphasizing diligent, skillful action in the realm of inner transformation.

### Practical Application
1.  **Cultivate Mindful Awareness**: Start by simply observing your mind without judgment. Notice its tendency to wander, its attractions, and aversions. This initial step of awareness is the beginning of self-control. Practices like daily meditation, even for short periods, are the 'proper means' to gently bring the mind under observation and then under control.
2.  **Set Realistic Goals for Self-Discipline**: Instead of trying to conquer all desires at once, choose one area where you want to exert self-control – perhaps your speech (avoiding gossip), your diet (mindful eating), or your reactions to stress. Consistently applying effort in one area builds mental muscle that can then be extended to others. This is applying 'proper means' to tangible areas of life.
3.  **Seek and Apply 'Upāyataḥ'**: Don't just follow popular trends; consciously seek out and apply time-tested spiritual practices and wisdom. This could mean studying the Gita, practicing specific breathing exercises (pranayama), engaging in selfless service (seva), or finding a qualified mentor. Learning the 'how-to' and diligently applying it is key to making progress and not just 'trying hard' without direction.

### Reflection
*   Where in my life do I feel my mind is most uncontrolled? How does this impact my peace and effectiveness?
*   What specific 'proper means' (upāyataḥ) am I currently employing to gain self-control? Are they effective, or do I need to explore new methods?
*   Am I consistent and persistent in my spiritual efforts, even when faced with setbacks?

## Key Concepts
*   **Self-Control (Vaśyātmanā)**: The fundamental requirement for spiritual progress, moving from being driven by the mind to guiding it.
*   **Persistent Effort (Yatatā)**: The necessity of continuous, dedicated practice and endeavor in spiritual disciplines.
*   **Proper Means (Upāyataḥ)**: The importance of employing skillful, effective, and ethical methods in the pursuit of Yoga.
*   **Attainability of Yoga**: The reassuring truth that despite its difficulty, the state of Yoga is achievable for those who commit to the path with discipline and wisdom.
