## Explanation

### Context
This verse, Bhagavad Gita Chapter 5, Verse 15, stands as a profound clarification in Krishna's discourse on Karma Sanyasa Yoga. Chapter 5 aims to reconcile the path of renunciation of action (sannyasa) with the path of action performed without attachment (karma yoga). Krishna has been explaining that a truly enlightened person, though engaged in the world, understands that the Self is not the doer of actions. The previous verses established that the wise see the divine in all beings and actions, and they understand that the Self neither 'does' nor 'causes' actions. This verse further elaborates on the divine's detachment from individual karmic outcomes and points to the fundamental cause of human delusion: ignorance.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **नादत्ते (nādatte):** `न` (na) means 'not,' and `आदत्ते` (ādatte) means 'accepts' or 'takes.' So, 'does not accept' or 'does not take upon Himself.'
*   **कस्यचित् (kasyacit):** Of anyone, of any being.
*   **पापम् (pāpam):** Sin, demerit, evil deed.
*   **न च एव (na ca eva):** Nor indeed, neither.
*   **सुकृतम् (sukṛtam):** Merit, good deed, virtue.
*   **विभुः (vibhuḥ):** The Lord, the all-pervading, the omnipresent, the Supreme Self. This refers to the universal consciousness, Brahman.
*   **अज्ञानेन (ajñānena):** By ignorance. `अज्ञान` (ajñāna) is not just lack of information but spiritual ignorance, a misunderstanding of one's true nature.
*   **आवृतम् (āvṛtam):** Covered, veiled, enveloped.
*   **ज्ञानम् (jñānam):** Knowledge, true wisdom, spiritual insight, the understanding of the Self.
*   **तेन (tena):** By that (ignorance).
*   **मुह्यन्ति (muhyanti):** Are deluded, are bewildered, fall into error, are perplexed.
*   **जन्तवः (jantavaḥ):** Living beings, creatures, mortals.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna delivers two crucial teachings in this concise verse:

1.  **Divine Impartiality and Non-Doership of the Lord:** The first line, "The Lord (Vibhu) does not accept the sin or even the merit of anyone," is a profound statement about the nature of the Supreme Being. It clarifies that the divine is not a personal entity sitting in judgment, actively rewarding or punishing individuals. The `Vibhu` is the universal consciousness, the ultimate reality, which is pure, untainted, and beyond the dualities of good and evil, merit and demerit. The Lord is like the sun, which illuminates all actions, good or bad, but is not affected by their nature. The karmic laws operate automatically, based on the universal principle of cause and effect, much like natural laws. The Self, being the eternal witness, remains untouched by the actions performed by the individual ego (jiva) through the body and mind. This concept liberates us from the idea of a whimsical God and emphasizes the inherent justice of the cosmos.

2.  **The Veil of Ignorance (Ajñāna) as the Root of Delusion (Moha):** The second line, "Knowledge is covered by ignorance, and by this, beings are deluded," identifies `ajñāna` (spiritual ignorance) as the root cause of human suffering and confusion. True `jñāna` (knowledge) here refers to the understanding of one's true nature as the eternal, unchanging Self, distinct from the perishable body, mind, and ego. This knowledge is not absent, but `āvṛtam` (covered) by `ajñāna`. This covering causes beings (`jantavaḥ`) to `muhyanti` (be deluded). This delusion manifests as identifying with the temporary, mistaking the unreal for the real, leading to attachments, aversions, egoism, and the endless cycle of actions and reactions (samsara). It is this fundamental misunderstanding that prevents us from experiencing our innate freedom and bliss.

This verse reinforces the central theme of the Gita: liberation comes not from external rituals or mere physical renunciation, but from the realization of the Self, which dispels the darkness of ignorance.

### Practical Application
1.  **Embrace Personal Responsibility without Guilt:** Understanding that the Lord is impartial and karma operates impersonally empowers us. We are solely responsible for our actions and their consequences, but this understanding should lead to mindful action, not guilt or fear. It encourages us to take ownership of our lives and spiritual journey, knowing that our efforts in the right direction will yield positive results according to universal law, not divine favoritism.
2.  **Actively Seek Self-Knowledge:** Since ignorance (`ajñāna`) is the root of delusion, the most practical application is to dedicate oneself to dispelling it. This means engaging in self-inquiry, studying spiritual texts like the Gita, practicing meditation, and seeking the company of the wise. Regularly questioning our perceptions, challenging our ego-driven desires, and observing our mental patterns can help lift the veil of ignorance.
3.  **Cultivate Detachment from Outcomes:** Recognizing that the divine is detached from outcomes can inspire us to perform our duties (`dharma`) with dedication but without excessive attachment to the fruits. We do our best, knowing that the universe will take care of the results according to the karmic principle. This fosters inner peace, reduces anxiety, and allows for greater effectiveness in action.

## Key Concepts
*   **Divine Impartiality (Vibhu's Detachment):** The Supreme Lord is a witness, unaffected by individual good or bad deeds.
*   **The Veil of Ignorance (Ajñāna):** Spiritual ignorance is the primary cause of suffering and delusion.
*   **Delusion (Moha):** The state of being bewildered or confused due to the covering of true knowledge.
*   **Self-Knowledge (Jñāna):** The ultimate understanding of one's true nature, which dispels ignorance and leads to liberation.
