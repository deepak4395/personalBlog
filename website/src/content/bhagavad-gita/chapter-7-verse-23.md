## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 7, "Jnana Vijnana Yoga," or the Yoga of Knowledge and Realization, is where Lord Krishna begins to unveil His supreme, transcendental nature to Arjuna. Having established Himself as the ultimate reality, the origin of everything, and the substratum of the entire universe, Krishna then addresses the various ways beings approach the Divine. He explains that while He is the singular Supreme Being, many, deluded by Maya (the illusory energy), fail to recognize Him in His entirety. Verses 20-22 discuss how individuals whose intelligence has been swayed by material desires often turn to other devas (demigods or celestial beings) for specific boons. Krishna, being the indweller of all, facilitates their faith in these forms and grants their desires through them. This particular verse, 7.23, serves as a direct continuation and crucial clarification, delineating the ultimate outcome of such worship compared to direct, unalloyed devotion to Krishna Himself. It draws a stark contrast between temporary, material gains and eternal, spiritual liberation.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
Let's break down the Sanskrit to grasp the essence:
*   **अन्तवत् (antavat):** Temporary, perishable, having an end. This is a crucial qualifier, indicating the transient nature of the results.
*   **तु (tu):** But, indeed. It introduces a contrast.
*   **फलम् (phalam):** Fruit, result, outcome.
*   **तेषाम् (teṣām):** Of them; referring to those who worship devas for material benefits.
*   **तत् (tat):** That.
*   **भवति (bhavati):** Becomes, is.
*   **अल्पमेधसाम् (alpa-medhasām):** Of those with small or meager intelligence. `Alpa` means small or little, and `medhas` refers to intellect or wisdom. This doesn't imply a lack of intelligence in worldly matters, but a limited spiritual foresight.
*   **देवान् (devān):** To the demigods, celestial beings.
*   **देवयजः (deva-yajaḥ):** The worshippers of demigods. `Deva` (god) and `yaja` (worshipper).
*   **यान्ति (yānti):** Go, attain, reach.
*   **मद्भक्ताः (mad-bhaktāḥ):** My devotees. `Mat` (My, referring to Krishna) and `bhaktāḥ` (devotees).
*   **यान्ति माम् अपि (yānti mām api):** Also come to Me. `Mām` (Me) and `api` (also).

### Deeper Understanding
This verse delivers a fundamental teaching of the Bhagavad Gita: the distinction between temporary material gains and eternal spiritual liberation. Krishna is not condemning the worship of devas, nor is He denying their existence or power. Rather, He is highlighting the *nature of the results* obtained from such worship and the *level of intelligence* that seeks them.

1.  **The Ephemeral Nature of Material Rewards:** When individuals worship devas for specific boons—wealth, health, progeny, success, or even heavenly planets—the results are `antavat`, temporary. Even dwelling in celestial realms is not eternal; one must return to the earthly plane once the accumulated merits (punya) are exhausted. This reinforces the cyclical nature of `samsara` (the cycle of birth and death). Krishna emphasizes that focusing solely on these transient benefits reflects `alpa-medhasām`, or limited intelligence, because it overlooks the ultimate goal of freedom from this cycle.

2.  **The Eternal Goal of Devotion to Krishna:** In stark contrast, Krishna declares, "My devotees come to Me." This statement is profound. "Coming to Me" signifies attaining union with the Supreme, achieving `moksha` (liberation) from `samsara`, and entering an eternal state of spiritual bliss (`ananda`) and knowledge (`jnana`). This is the ultimate, everlasting fruit that transcends all material limitations. Krishna presents Himself as the Supreme Source, and direct devotion to Him bypasses the need for intermediaries, leading directly to the highest spiritual abode.

3.  **Hierarchy of Attainments:** The Gita establishes a clear hierarchy. While devas are powerful beings governing various aspects of the cosmos, they are still within the realm of `Maya` and are ultimately subservient to the Supreme Lord. Worshipping them yields results within that same finite realm. Worship of Krishna, the source of all devas and the entire creation, leads to transcendence of that realm altogether. This isn't about exclusive sectarianism but about understanding the comprehensive nature of the Divine and the most direct path to ultimate liberation.

This verse connects deeply with themes of `karma` (action and its results), `dharma` (righteous conduct), and `yoga` (union). It teaches that true `bhakti` (devotion) is not a transactional exchange for worldly desires, but an unconditional love and surrender to the Supreme, driven by a desire for liberation and union, not just temporary comfort.

### Practical Application
For a modern reader, this teaching offers profound insights into setting priorities and understanding the true nature of fulfillment:

1.  **Evaluate Your Motivations:** In any endeavor, especially spiritual or self-improvement practices, honestly ask yourself: What am I truly seeking? Am I looking for quick fixes, temporary comfort, or deeper, lasting transformation? If our prayers or efforts are solely focused on material gains (a better job, a new car, a perfect relationship), we might be settling for `antavat` (temporary) results. Shifting our focus to inner growth, peace, and connection to a higher purpose can lead to more profound and enduring satisfaction.

2.  **Invest in the Eternal, Not Just the Ephemeral:** Just as we save for retirement or invest in education for long-term benefits, this verse encourages us to "invest" in our spiritual well-being. Material possessions, fame, and even physical health are all subject to time and decay. While not ignoring these, prioritize practices that cultivate inner peace, wisdom, compassion, and a connection to the eternal self. This could mean dedicating time to meditation, selfless service, studying scriptures, or cultivating virtues, rather than solely chasing fleeting external achievements.

3.  **Broaden Your Perspective Beyond Immediate Gratification:** In a consumer-driven society that often promotes instant gratification, this teaching is a powerful counter-narrative. It urges us to develop `medhas` (intelligence) that looks beyond the immediate, the tangible, and the perishable. When faced with choices, consider the long-term impact on your spiritual journey and overall well-being, not just the short-term pleasure or gain. This helps in making wiser decisions, whether in career, relationships, or personal habits.

*   How can I cultivate a more expansive view of "intelligence" that values eternal wisdom over fleeting worldly success?
*   In what ways can I deepen my devotion or spiritual practice to seek ultimate union rather than just immediate solutions?

## Key Concepts

*   **Ephemeral vs. Eternal Results:** The fundamental contrast between temporary material gains from demigod worship and everlasting liberation from devotion to the Supreme.
*   **Limited vs. Expansive Intelligence (Alpa-medhasām):** The idea that focusing solely on transient worldly desires reflects a narrow perspective, while seeking ultimate truth requires broader spiritual discernment.
*   **The Goal of Bhakti (Devotion):** True devotion to Krishna is not a transactional exchange for material benefits but a path to ultimate liberation and union with the Divine.
*   **Hierarchy of Attainments:** While various paths and deities exist, the Gita indicates that direct devotion to the Supreme Source (Krishna) leads to the highest, most enduring spiritual destination.
