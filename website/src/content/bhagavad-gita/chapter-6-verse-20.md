## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled "Atma Samyama Yoga" or "The Yoga of Self-Control/Meditation," is Krishna's practical guide to the path of Dhyana Yoga (meditation). Having established the theoretical foundations of action, knowledge, and devotion in earlier chapters, Krishna now turns to the inner discipline required for spiritual realization. He has already discussed the ideal qualities of a yogi, the suitable environment for practice, proper posture, and the initial steps of mind control. Verse 18 defined Yoga as the state where the mind is perfectly restrained and fixed on the Self, and Verse 19 used the analogy of a flameless lamp in a windless place to illustrate the steady, unwavering state of a yogi's mind. Building upon this, Verse 20 elaborates on the profound experience that occurs when the mind truly achieves this state of stillness and focus. It describes the direct perception of the Self and the resulting supreme inner contentment, setting the stage for the deeper bliss and liberation detailed in the subsequent verses.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **यत्र (yatra):** When, where.
*   **उपरमते (uparamate):** Becomes still, ceases (from activity), rests, withdraws.
*   **चित्तं (cittaṁ):** The mind, consciousness, mental faculty.
*   **निरुद्धं (niruddhaṁ):** Restrained, controlled, disciplined, made captive.
*   **योगसेवया (yogasevayā):** By the practice or service of Yoga. `Yoga` (union, discipline) + `sevayā` (by service, by practice).
*   **यत्र चैव (yatra caiva):** And when, and indeed where.
*   **आत्मना (ātmanā):** By the Self (specifically, by the purified intellect or subtle mind, acting as an instrument).
*   **आत्मानं (ātmānaṁ):** The Self (the Supreme Self, the true, eternal spirit within).
*   **पश्यन् (paśyan):** Seeing, perceiving, beholding.
*   **आत्मनि (ātmani):** In the Self (within one's own being, in the spirit).
*   **तुष्यति (tuṣyati):** Is content, satisfied, pleased, finds joy.

### Deeper Understanding
This verse beautifully articulates the ultimate goal and experience of Dhyana Yoga. Krishna is teaching Arjuna that the purpose of all yogic discipline is not just to quiet the mind, but to use that tranquility as a gateway to direct perception of one's true nature.

1.  **The Stillness of the Mind (`yatroparamate cittaṁ niruddhaṁ yogasevayā`):** The verse begins by emphasizing the prerequisite: the mind (`cittaṁ`), which is usually a turbulent stream of thoughts, desires, and sensory impressions, must become completely still. This stillness (`uparamate`) is not a forceful suppression but a natural cessation of mental agitation, achieved through sustained and dedicated practice of Yoga (`yogasevayā`). This implies consistent effort, patience, and a gradual refinement of one's inner faculties. It’s a state where the mind is no longer distracted by external objects or internal ruminations, but rests in its pure, undisturbed essence.

2.  **Seeing the Self by the Self (`ātmanātmānaṁ paśyan`):** This is a profound and central teaching. The phrase `ātmanātmānaṁ` uses the word 'Atman' twice, with different grammatical cases, indicating a subtle yet crucial distinction. The instrumental `ātmanā` (by the Self) refers to the purified, concentrated intellect or the subtle mind, which has been refined and made clear through yogic discipline. This purified mental instrument then perceives `ātmānaṁ` (the Self), which denotes the transcendental, eternal, spiritual Self – the true essence of existence, distinct from the temporary body and mind. This signifies that the ultimate truth is not perceived through external senses or even discursive reasoning, but through an inner, intuitive vision. It is a direct, non-dual experience where the instrument of perception (the purified mind) merges with the object of perception (the true Self), revealing their inherent oneness. It's like seeing the reflection of the sun perfectly in a completely still, clear pond.

3.  **Contentment in the Self (`ātmani tuṣyati`):** The culmination of this experience is profound and unshakeable contentment (`tuṣyati`). This is not the fleeting happiness derived from worldly achievements, sensory pleasures, or external validation. Rather, it is an `ānanda` (bliss) that arises intrinsically from the recognition of one's true identity as the eternal, blissful Self. This contentment is self-sufficient, independent, and transcends all dualities of joy and sorrow, gain and loss. Once one has tasted this inner satisfaction, the allure of external gratifications diminishes, as they are recognized as temporary and ultimately unfulfilling. This state is the very essence of liberation and supreme peace.

This verse beautifully connects the practice of Yoga (mind control) with its ultimate fruit (Self-realization and inner contentment). It underscores that true happiness is not found externally but discovered within, through the disciplined journey of self-discovery.

### Practical Application
1.  **Dedicated Stillness Practice:** Integrate a daily practice of meditation or quiet contemplation into your routine. Even 10-15 minutes of sitting silently, focusing on your breath, or observing your thoughts without judgment can begin to cultivate the `yogasevayā` (practice of Yoga) necessary to quiet the mind. The goal is to create a space where the mind can naturally settle, rather than forcing it into submission.
2.  **Mindful Self-Inquiry:** Throughout your day, periodically pause and ask yourself, "Who am I, beyond my thoughts, emotions, roles, and possessions?" This `ātma-vichāra` (self-inquiry) helps to gently shift your awareness from the superficial layers of identity to the deeper, unchanging Self. This practice aligns with "seeing the Self through the Self" by using your refined intellect to inquire into your true nature.
3.  **Cultivating Inner Contentment:** Observe where you typically seek satisfaction. Is it primarily from external sources like achievements, possessions, or others' opinions? Consciously try to find moments of contentment in simple inner experiences – the peace of a quiet moment, the joy of a selfless act, or the feeling of presence. This trains the mind to recognize and appreciate the inherent `ātmani tuṣyati` (contentment in the Self) that is always available, independent of external circumstances.

*   How can I integrate more moments of conscious internal stillness into my busy life?

## Key Concepts
*   **Citta Vritti Nirodha:** The cessation of the fluctuations or modifications of the mind, leading to profound stillness.
*   **Atma-Jnana (Self-Realization):** The direct, intuitive perception and understanding of one's true spiritual Self (Atman).
*   **Atma-Tushti (Inner Contentment):** A deep, self-sufficient satisfaction and bliss that arises from within, independent of external circumstances.
*   **Dhyana Yoga:** The path of meditation and mental discipline aimed at achieving union with the divine within.
