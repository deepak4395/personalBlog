## Explanation

### Context
In Chapter 4, Krishna begins by revealing a profound truth to Arjuna: the path of yoga he is about to impart is not a new invention but an ancient, timeless wisdom. Verse 1 established that Krishna first taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvān (the Sun-god), who then taught it to Manu, and Manu to Ikṣvāku. This verse (4.2) continues that narrative, explaining *who* then received this knowledge and, crucially, *what happened* to it. It sets the stage for Krishna’s declaration in the next verse that he is re-teaching this very same ancient yoga to Arjuna, because Arjuna is his devotee and friend. This re-establishment of lost wisdom is a central theme, highlighting the cyclical nature of knowledge and its occasional need for divine intervention.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **एवमेवं (evamevaṁ)**: Thus, in this manner; in the way previously described.
*   **परम्पराप्राप्तम् (paramparāprāptam)**: Received through succession; handed down in a continuous chain or tradition.
*   **इमम् (imam)**: This (referring to the yoga mentioned in the previous verse).
*   **राजर्षयः (rājarṣayaḥ)**: Royal sages; kings who were also sages or seers. A compound of `rājan` (king) and `ṛṣi` (sage).
*   **विदुः (viduḥ)**: Understood, knew, realized.
*   **सः (saḥ)**: That (referring to the same yoga).
*   **कालेनेह (kāleneha)**: By time, here; `kālena` (by time) + `iha` (here, in this world).
*   **महता (mahatā)**: By great (referring to the passage or influence of time).
*   **योगः (yogaḥ)**: The yoga; the divine science of self-realization or union.
*   **नष्टः (naṣṭaḥ)**: Lost, destroyed, perished, obscured.
*   **परन्तप (parantapa)**: O scorcher of foes; an epithet for Arjuna, signifying his prowess and inner strength, implying he is capable of overcoming internal and external obstacles to receive this knowledge.

### Deeper Understanding
This verse offers several profound insights. Firstly, it emphasizes the importance of `paramparā` or disciplic succession. The wisdom of yoga is not something to be casually discovered or invented anew by each generation; it is a profound truth transmitted through an unbroken chain of qualified teachers. This lineage ensures the purity and authenticity of the teachings, guarding against misinterpretations and dilutions. The `rājarṣis` – royal sages – are presented as the recipients of this knowledge. This is a significant detail, as it demonstrates that spiritual wisdom was not exclusively for hermits or renunciates. Even kings, burdened with the immense responsibilities of governance and warfare (much like Arjuna), were capable of attaining the highest spiritual understanding. This challenges the notion that one must abandon worldly duties to pursue spiritual enlightenment, suggesting that a balanced life of action and contemplation is possible and even ideal.

However, the verse also delivers a sobering truth: `sa kāleneha mahatā yogo naṣṭaḥ`. Even such a venerable and potent spiritual science, transmitted through great souls, can be lost or obscured over a long period. Time, with its corrosive effect, can lead to neglect, misinterpretation, or the complete disappearance of even the most profound truths. This loss is not necessarily a physical destruction but often a fading from collective consciousness, a diminishing of understanding, or a corruption of its original intent. This explains *why* Krishna, the divine teacher, needs to re-establish this yoga. He is not introducing a new philosophy but reviving an ancient, essential wisdom that humanity has forgotten or distorted, thereby reaffirming the eternal nature of `dharma` and the periodic need for its re-establishment.

### Practical Application
1.  **Value of Authentic Mentorship**: In our modern world, information is abundant, but wisdom is scarce. This verse teaches us the importance of seeking knowledge from authentic, experienced teachers or traditions (`paramparā`) rather than relying solely on self-discovery or superficial sources. Whether in a spiritual path, a professional skill, or academic pursuit, a qualified mentor can guide us past pitfalls and accelerate our learning.
2.  **Preserving and Transmitting Knowledge**: Just as the ancient yoga was lost over time, valuable knowledge in various fields—from traditional crafts to ethical principles—can fade if not actively preserved and transmitted. We can apply this by documenting our experiences, mentoring younger generations, or supporting institutions that safeguard cultural and intellectual heritage. This proactive approach ensures that wisdom endures beyond our individual lifetimes.
3.  **Integrating Spirituality with Daily Life**: The example of `rājarṣis` (royal sages) is particularly relevant today. Many people feel they must choose between a demanding career or family life and spiritual growth. This verse reminds us that it is possible, and indeed noble, to engage fully in our worldly responsibilities while simultaneously cultivating inner wisdom and spiritual discipline. It encourages a holistic approach where our actions become a form of yoga.

## Key Concepts
*   **Paramparā (Disciplic Succession)**: The unbroken chain of spiritual teachers and disciples through which authentic knowledge is transmitted.
*   **Rājarṣis (Royal Sages)**: Kings who achieved spiritual enlightenment, demonstrating that worldly duties and spiritual pursuit are not mutually exclusive.
*   **The Impermanence of Knowledge**: Even divine wisdom can be lost or obscured over long periods due to human neglect or the influence of time.
*   **Re-establishment of Dharma**: The cyclical necessity for divine intervention to revive and clarify essential spiritual truths when they become lost to humanity.
