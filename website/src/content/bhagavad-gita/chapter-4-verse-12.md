## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 4 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled "Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga," unfolds profound truths about the nature of action, knowledge, and renunciation. Lord Krishna has just declared in the preceding verse (4.11), "ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham" – "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly." This sets the stage for understanding the diverse motivations behind human actions and worship. Verse 4.12 explains *why* many individuals choose to direct their devotion towards various demigods rather than the Supreme Lord Himself. It offers a practical observation about human nature and the immediate gratification sought by most, contrasting it with the deeper, more profound spiritual journey. This verse subtly introduces the idea that while different paths exist and yield different results, their efficacy is directly tied to the desires and goals of the practitioner. It also serves as a prelude to the explanation of the *varna* system in the subsequent verse, linking human actions and qualities to societal roles and spiritual paths.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
Let's break down the individual components of this insightful shloka:
*   **काङ्क्षन्तः (kāṅkṣantaḥ):** Desiring, aspiring for, longing for. This highlights the motivation – a specific want or ambition.
*   **कर्मणां (karmaṇāṃ):** Of actions, relating to the fruits or results of one's endeavors.
*   **सिद्धिं (siddhiṃ):** Success, accomplishment, fulfillment, perfection. In this context, it refers to worldly achievements or desired outcomes.
*   **यजन्त (yajanta):** They worship, they offer sacrifices to. This denotes an act of devotion or ritualistic offering.
*   **इह (iha):** Here, in this world, referring to the material realm of human existence.
*   **देवताः (devatāḥ):** Demigods, deities. These are powerful celestial beings who govern various aspects of the material universe and can grant specific boons.
*   **क्षिप्रं (kṣipraṃ):** Quickly, swiftly, without delay. This emphasizes the speed with which results are obtained.
*   **हि (hi):** Indeed, certainly, surely. A particle affirming the truth of the statement.
*   **मानुषे (mānuṣe):** In the human.
*   **लोके (loke):** In the world or realm. Thus, "mānuṣe loke" means "in the human world" or "among human beings."
*   **सिद्धिर्भवति (siddhirbhavati):** Success becomes, success is achieved.
*   **कर्मजा (karmajā):** Born of action, resulting from one's deeds or efforts.

Combined, the verse explains that people, motivated by a desire for immediate, tangible success from their actions, offer worship to demigods because in the human world, such action-oriented success is attained rapidly.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna's statement here is a profound observation on human psychology and the spiritual hierarchy. He is not condemning the worship of demigods but explaining the *reason* behind it.
*   **The Lure of Immediate Results:** The primary teaching is that most people in the human world are driven by immediate desires for material benefits – wealth, health, progeny, power, fame, removal of obstacles, etc. Demigods are seen as specific administrators or conduits through whom these particular boons can be obtained relatively quickly through prescribed rituals and prayers. For instance, one might worship Lakshmi for wealth, Saraswati for knowledge, or Indra for rain. The *Bhagavatam* and other Puranas elaborate on these specific powers and their corresponding worship.
*   **The Nature of Human Existence:** The phrase "kṣipraṃ hi mānuṣe loke" (swiftly indeed in the human world) is crucial. It highlights that the human realm is uniquely suited for performing actions and experiencing their immediate results. Unlike other realms, where karma might ripen slowly or be purely भोग-भूमि (bhoga-bhūmi, a realm of enjoyment), the human world is a *karma-bhūmi* (a realm of action) where efforts yield quick, discernible fruits. This makes the human birth incredibly significant, offering a rapid path to either material entanglement or spiritual liberation, depending on one's choice of action and worship.
*   **Distinction between Material and Spiritual Goals:** Implicit in this verse is a distinction between temporary, material success (siddhi) and ultimate spiritual liberation (moksha). While demigods can grant material boons, they cannot grant liberation from the cycle of birth and death. That ultimate spiritual goal is achieved only through devotion to the Supreme Lord, Krishna, who is the source of all demigods and the ultimate dispenser of all results. Krishna is not saying demigod worship is wrong, but rather that it is a path chosen by those with specific, often material, desires.
*   **Divine Impartiality (from 4.11):** This verse further clarifies Krishna's statement in 4.11. He reciprocates with all beings according to their surrender. If one surrenders to demigods for material gains, Krishna, as the ultimate sanctioner, facilitates those results through the demigods. If one surrenders to Him for spiritual liberation, He grants that. This demonstrates His impartiality and the freedom of choice afforded to every soul.

### Practical Application
1.  **Examine Your Motivations:** In modern life, we often "worship" various "demigods" – be it career success, social media validation, material possessions, or even specific health regimens – hoping for quick results. This verse prompts us to pause and honestly assess what truly drives our actions and aspirations. Are we seeking fleeting external achievements or profound inner fulfillment? Understanding our motivations helps us align our efforts with our deepest values.
2.  **Patience vs. Instant Gratification:** The "swiftly attained" aspect of worldly success can lead to a culture of instant gratification. While it's natural to desire quick results, spiritual growth and true self-realization often require sustained effort, patience, and a long-term perspective. This verse encourages us to cultivate patience for the deeper, more lasting rewards of spiritual practice, rather than constantly chasing immediate, ephemeral successes.
3.  **Holistic Well-being:** While material success is a natural part of human life and can contribute to comfort, it's crucial not to make it the sole focus. This teaching encourages a balanced approach where material pursuits are integrated with a higher spiritual purpose. One can strive for worldly success while simultaneously cultivating an inner spiritual life, understanding that true peace and happiness transcend mere external achievements. For example, a successful entrepreneur might dedicate a portion of their earnings to spiritual causes or practice mindful leadership, understanding that their work is also a form of service.

*   How do my immediate desires influence my long-term spiritual aspirations and overall sense of purpose?
*   Am I willing to invest in practices that yield slower, but more profound and lasting, spiritual benefits, or do I consistently prioritize quick, temporary fixes?

## Key Concepts

1.  **Karma-phala (Fruits of Action):** The inherent connection between actions and their results, and the human tendency to desire and pursue these fruits.
2.  **Motivation for Worship:** The verse highlights how specific material desires (kāṅkṣantaḥ karmaṇāṃ siddhiṃ) drive individuals to worship particular deities (devatāḥ) for targeted outcomes.
3.  **Efficacy of the Human Realm (Karma-bhūmi):** The unique characteristic of the *mānuṣe loke* (human world) where actions (karma) quickly yield tangible results (kṣipraṃ siddhirbhavati karmajā), making it a significant arena for both material and spiritual progress.
4.  **Hierarchical Nature of Desires:** An implicit distinction is drawn between desires for immediate, worldly success granted by demigods, and the ultimate spiritual liberation that comes from devotion to the Supreme Lord, setting the stage for understanding higher spiritual pursuits.
