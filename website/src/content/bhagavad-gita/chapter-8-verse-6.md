## Explanation

### Context
This verse is a part of Chapter 8, known as Aksara Brahma Yoga, which translates to the Yoga of the Imperishable Absolute. In this chapter, Lord Krishna explains the significance of remembering the divine at the time of death. The teachings are essential for Arjuna, who is grappling with moral dilemmas on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. Krishna emphasizes that the consciousness at death determines the soul's next journey, aligning with the broader themes of duty (dharma) and the cycle of life and death (samsara).

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **यद् (yad)**: whatever
- **आत्मनं (ātmaṇaṁ)**: the self or soul
- **प्राप्यति (prāpyati)**: attains or reaches
- **तद् (tad)**: that
- **योगी (yogī)**: the practitioner of yoga or a sage
- **तद् इव (tad iva)**: that indeed
- **सदा (sadā)**: always

This verse emphasizes that the state of consciousness at the moment of death is crucial, and one will attain the state they remember or focus on.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna communicates a profound truth about the nature of existence and consciousness. The core teaching is that what we focus on at the time of death shapes our future. This not only applies to the end of life but resonates throughout our entire existence. It highlights the necessity of cultivating a steady, focused mind through consistent spiritual practices (sadhana) to ensure a favorable state of consciousness at the moment of leaving the body.

Krishna is teaching Arjuna about the importance of detachment from worldly affairs and the significance of aligning oneself with the divine. The key concepts presented include the transient nature of the physical body and the eternal nature of the soul. This directly connects to the Gita's overarching themes of dharma and karma, encouraging individuals to live righteously, so they can transcend the cycle of birth and death.
