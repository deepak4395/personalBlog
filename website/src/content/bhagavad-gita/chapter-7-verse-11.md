## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, 'Jnana Vijnana Yoga,' is a profound exploration of the nature of the Divine, moving beyond theoretical knowledge (jnana) to realized wisdom (vijnana). Krishna is systematically revealing His pervasive presence in the universe, demonstrating how He is the essence and underlying reality of all existence. He has already declared Himself as the taste in water, the light in the sun and moon, the sacred sound, the pure fragrance of the earth, the brilliance in fire, and the life in all beings. He is the eternal seed of all existence. In this verse, Krishna continues to elaborate on His manifestations, now focusing on human attributes like strength and desire, but with crucial qualifications that elevate them to a divine principle.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **बलं (balaṁ)**: strength, power, might.
*   **बलवताम् (balavatām)**: of the strong, of the powerful ones.
*   **अस्मि (asmi)**: I am.
*   **कामरागविवर्जितम् (kāma-rāga-vivarjitam)**: devoid of *kama* (selfish desire, lust) and *raga* (attachment, passion). This is a critical qualifier, distinguishing divine strength from ordinary, ego-driven power.
*   **धर्माविरुद्धो (dharmāviruddho)**: not contrary to dharma; in harmony with righteousness, ethical principles, and cosmic order.
*   **भूतेषु (bhūteṣu)**: in beings, among all creatures.
*   **कामोऽस्मि (kāmo ’smi)**: I am desire.
*   **भरतर्षभ (bharatarṣabha)**: O best among the Bharatas; an epithet for Arjuna, signifying his noble lineage and qualities.

### Deeper Understanding
This verse offers a nuanced understanding of how the Divine operates within human experience, particularly concerning two powerful forces: strength and desire.

First, Krishna declares, "*balaṁ balavatām asmi kāma-rāga-vivarjitam*" – I am the strength of the strong, devoid of desire and attachment. This is not a celebration of brute force or power used for selfish ends. Instead, Krishna identifies with a higher form of strength: the strength of character, resilience, determination, and spiritual fortitude that is untainted by personal desires (kama) or attachments (raga) to outcomes. It is the strength of a leader who acts for the welfare of their people without personal ambition, the strength of a yogi who maintains equanimity amidst challenges, or the strength of a warrior who fights for righteousness without hatred or craving for victory. This divine strength is pure, selfless, and directed towards upholding dharma.

Second, and perhaps even more revealing, Krishna states, "*dharmāviruddho bhūteṣu kāmo ’smi bharatarṣabha*" – O best among the Bharatas, I am also the desire in beings that is not contrary to dharma. Desire (*kama*) is often seen as a primary cause of suffering and bondage in spiritual traditions. However, Krishna here makes a crucial distinction. He is *not* all desire, but only that desire which is *dharmāviruddha* – not in opposition to dharma. What constitutes such desire?

*   **Dharma** encompasses righteousness, moral duty, ethical conduct, and the natural order that sustains the universe. Therefore, a dharmic desire is one that supports life, promotes well-being, fulfills one's responsibilities, and contributes to the harmony of the individual and society.
*   Examples of dharmic desires include the desire for knowledge, the desire to serve others, the desire to protect one's family, the desire for procreation within the bounds of marriage, the desire to maintain one's health, or the desire for spiritual growth. These desires are foundational to human existence and societal flourishing when pursued ethically. They are not driven by greed, lust, or ego, but by a higher purpose.

By qualifying both strength and desire, Krishna teaches that the Divine is present not in the gross manifestation of these qualities, but in their purest, most righteous, and selfless forms. This verse encourages us to discriminate, to recognize the divine principle within these powerful human urges, and to align our own strength and desires with dharma. It's a call to elevate our human experience by purifying our intentions and actions.

### Practical Application
1.  **Examine Your Strength:** Reflect on how you utilize your personal strengths—physical, intellectual, emotional, or financial. Are you employing them for selfish gain, ego gratification, or to dominate others? Or are you channeling them towards noble causes, helping those in need, pursuing truth, or fulfilling your duties with integrity and detachment from outcomes? Strive to cultivate strength that is "kāma-rāga-vivarjitam" – pure, purposeful, and free from binding desires.
2.  **Discern Your Desires:** Take an inventory of your desires. Which ones lead to inner peace and growth, and which ones create agitation and bondage? Do your aspirations align with your highest values and contribute positively to your life and the lives of others? Actively cultivate desires that are "dharmāviruddha" – those that are ethical, constructive, and lead to well-being, both personal and collective. For example, desiring to excel in your profession to provide for your family and contribute meaningfully is dharmic; desiring wealth through unethical means is not.
3.  **See the Divine in Pure Manifestations:** When you witness acts of genuine strength (e.g., a leader making a difficult but ethical decision, an athlete demonstrating disciplined excellence, a parent tirelessly caring for their child) or observe desires leading to positive creation (e.g., an artist creating beauty, a scientist discovering a cure, a community working together for a common good), recognize that the divine essence of Krishna is at play in these pure expressions. This practice helps to elevate your perception of the world.

### Reflection
*   What forms of strength do I possess, and how can I purify them to be free from selfish desire and attachment?
*   Which of my current desires are truly dharmic, and which might be leading me away from my higher self? How can I reorient them?
*   How can I better perceive the divine presence in the righteous strength and constructive desires I encounter daily?

## Key Concepts
*   **Divine Immanence in Qualities:** Krishna's presence is not just in the elements but in the very essence of human attributes like strength and desire when they are pure.
*   **Strength without Attachment:** True, divine strength is characterized by freedom from selfish desires and attachment to results, emphasizing selfless action and purpose.
*   **Dharmic Desire:** Not all desire is binding. Desire that aligns with righteousness, ethics, and cosmic order (dharma) is an expression of the Divine and essential for purposeful living.
*   **Discernment in Action:** The verse calls for discrimination, urging individuals to identify and cultivate those aspects of strength and desire that are pure and righteous, aligning with the divine principle within.
