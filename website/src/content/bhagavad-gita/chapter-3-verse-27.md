## Explanation

### Context
This verse appears in Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled Karma Yoga, which centers around the concept of selfless action. In the preceding verses, Lord Krishna emphasizes the importance of performing one's duty without attachment to the results. Arjuna, confused about his responsibilities, is guided by Krishna to understand the nature of action, its relationship to the self, and the importance of performing one's dharma, or duty.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **Buddhi**: intelligence or higher understanding.
- **Yukta**: one who is established or connected.
- **Jahāti**: renounces or lets go.
- **Iha**: in this world.
- **Ubhay**: both (good and bad).
- **Sukṛta**: good actions or virtuous deeds.
- **Duṣkṛta**: bad actions or sinful deeds.
- **Tasmāt**: therefore.
- **Yogāya**: for yoga.
- **Yujyasva**: engage.
- **Yogaḥ**: yoga or union.
- **Karmasu**: in actions.
- **Kauśalam**: skill or expertise.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna addresses the complexity of actions and their motivations. He conveys that true renunciation comes through wisdom; a person with clear understanding recognizes that actions are merely expressions of the gunas (qualities of nature) and the ego. By realizing that both good and bad actions arise from the same source—the misidentification with the ego—one can transcend dualities. Krishna encourages Arjuna to engage in yoga, which here signifies a disciplined approach to action that is rooted in self-awareness and detached from the outcomes.

Krishna teaches Arjuna that it is not enough to just abandon actions; true renunciation is about performing one's duty with skill and dedication, while remaining unattached to the fruits of those actions. This teaching connects deeply with the overarching themes of the Gita, such as dharma (duty), karma (action), and yoga (the path to realization). By cultivating skill in action, one can maintain equanimity amidst success and failure, which is essential for spiritual growth.

### Practical Application
For modern readers, applying this teaching means developing a mindset of selfless service and excellence in one's work. Here are some concrete examples:
1. **Workplace Approach**: In a professional setting, focus on delivering quality work without being overly attached to promotions or recognition. By valuing the process over the outcome, you can find greater satisfaction in your efforts, leading to a more fulfilling career.
2. **Personal Relationships**: In relationships, engage with others without the expectation of reciprocation. By giving love and support freely, you practice selflessness. This can strengthen bonds and promote harmony.
3. **Community Service**: Engage in volunteer work or community service where the focus is on helping others rather than seeking personal accolades. This reinforces the idea of performing actions for the greater good and cultivates a sense of unity and purpose.

## Key Concepts
- **Selflessness**: The importance of performing actions without attachment to results.
- **Skill in Action**: The idea that true yoga involves mastering the art of action through wisdom and understanding.
- **Renunciation**: Understanding that true renunciation is not the abandonment of action, but rather acting with clarity and detachment.
- **The Nature of Action**: Recognizing that all actions are influenced by the gunas and the ego, and that we are not the sole doers of our actions.
