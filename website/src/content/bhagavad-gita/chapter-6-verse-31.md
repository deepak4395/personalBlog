## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled 'Atma Samyama Yoga' or the 'Yoga of Self-Control', is dedicated to the practice of Dhyana Yoga, or the yoga of meditation. Lord Krishna instructs Arjuna on controlling the mind, withdrawing the senses, and focusing on the Supreme Self. Verses 6.29 and 6.30 introduce the profound vision of an advanced yogi: one who sees the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self, and who perceives Krishna (the Divine) everywhere and everything in Krishna. Verse 6.31 builds directly upon this foundation, elaborating on the state of such a yogi, whose mind is perfectly disciplined and whose vision is one of complete equality and oneness.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **Sarva-bhūta-stham** (सर्वभूतस्थम्): Dwelling in all beings. `Sarva` means 'all', `bhūta` means 'beings' or 'creatures', and `stham` means 'abiding' or 'situated'.
*   **Ātmānam** (आत्मानम्): The Self.
*   **Sarva-bhūtāni** (सर्वभूतानि): All beings.
*   **Ca** (च): And.
*   **Ātmani** (आत्मनि): In the Self.
*   **Īkṣate** (ईक्षते): Sees, perceives, beholds.
*   **Yoga-yukta-ātmā** (योगयुक्तात्मा): One whose mind or self is disciplined, united, or yoked by yoga. `Yoga` implies union and discipline, `yukta` means 'united' or 'joined', and `ātmā` here refers to the mind or individual consciousness.
*   **Sarvatra** (सर्वत्र): Everywhere.
*   **Sama-darśanaḥ** (समदर्शनः): One who sees with equal vision; possessing equanimity of vision. `Sama` means 'equal' or 'same', and `darśana` means 'vision' or 'seeing'.

### Deeper Understanding
This verse describes the ultimate realization of a yogi who has perfected the practice of Dhyana Yoga. Krishna reveals the profound state of consciousness where the boundaries between the individual self, the Universal Self, and all forms of existence dissolve. The yogi achieves a non-dual perception, understanding that the same divine essence (Ātman) that resides within them also pervades every single being and object in the universe.

**What is Krishna teaching Arjuna?**
Krishna is teaching Arjuna that the highest state of yoga is not just about physical postures or breath control, but about a complete transformation of perception. It's the experiential understanding of the unity of all existence. The `yogayuktātmā`—one whose mind is perfectly aligned through yogic discipline—no longer sees multiplicity but an underlying oneness. This vision transcends all superficial differences of form, status, species, or circumstance.

**Key concepts presented:**
1.  **Unity of Existence:** The fundamental teaching is that there is one universal consciousness, one Self, that manifests in countless forms. The yogi 'sees' this truth, not as an intellectual concept, but as a living reality.
2.  **Interconnectedness:** If the same Self is in all, then all beings are intrinsically connected. There is no true separation. This understanding naturally fosters empathy, compassion, and a sense of shared destiny.
3.  **Samadarśana (Equal Vision):** This is the hallmark of the realized yogi. With `samadarśana`, one views all beings—from a sage to an outcaste, an elephant to a dog, a rich person to a poor person—with an equal eye, recognizing the same divine spark within. This doesn't mean ignoring practical differences but seeing beyond them to the spiritual essence.

**How does this relate to other Gita themes?**
This verse beautifully synthesizes elements from various Yogas. It is the culmination of **Dhyana Yoga**, leading to the direct experience of the **Jñāna Yoga** (Yoga of Knowledge) truth of the identity of Ātman and Brahman. It also informs **Karma Yoga**, as actions performed with this universal vision become selfless service, free from attachment and discrimination. Furthermore, seeing the divine in all beings is a profound expression of **Bhakti Yoga** (Yoga of Devotion), where devotion extends beyond a personal deity to the entire creation.

### Practical Application
1.  **Cultivating Empathy and Compassion:** In daily interactions, consciously try to see the inherent worth and shared humanity (or 'being-ness') in everyone, especially those with whom you disagree or find challenging. Imagine the divine spark of consciousness residing within them, just as it resides within you. This practice can dissolve prejudice and foster genuine connection.
2.  **Overcoming Discrimination:** Actively challenge internal biases and societal prejudices by reminding yourself that the same Universal Self resides in all beings, regardless of their external appearance, background, or beliefs. This fosters a truly inclusive mindset and promotes harmonious coexistence.
3.  **Mindful Interaction with Nature:** Extend this equal vision to animals and the natural world. Recognizing the divine presence in all forms of life encourages a respectful, sustainable, and non-harming approach to our environment and fellow creatures.

*   How would my daily interactions and reactions change if I genuinely perceived the divine Self in every person, animal, and plant I encounter?
*   What specific biases or judgments do I hold that prevent me from cultivating `samadarśana`?
*   How can I consciously practice seeing unity amidst the apparent diversity and challenges of contemporary life?

## Key Concepts

1.  **Universal Self (Ātman):** The inherent, unchanging divine essence that pervades all individual beings and the entire cosmos.
2.  **Interconnectedness of Existence:** The profound understanding that all forms of life and creation are not separate but are expressions of a single, unified consciousness.
3.  **Samadarśana (Equal Vision):** The spiritual ability to perceive the same divine Self in all beings, thereby transcending external differences and fostering equanimity.
4.  **Yogic Discipline:** The consistent practice of meditation, self-control, and ethical living necessary to achieve and sustain this elevated state of consciousness and perception.
