## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled "Atma Samyama Yoga" (The Yoga of Self-Control), delves deep into the practice of meditation and the discipline required to achieve union with the Divine. Krishna has meticulously described the ideal state of a yogi, the disciplined mind, the serene environment, and the unwavering focus needed to achieve spiritual perfection. He has outlined the path of dhyana yoga, emphasizing self-control, detachment, and equanimity. 

At this point, Arjuna, ever the practical student and representative of the common human condition, interjects with a crucial and very relatable question. He acknowledges the difficulty of such a demanding path. He is not questioning the validity of yoga itself but rather the fate of those who embark on it with sincerity and faith (`śraddhā`) but, due to the inherent wavering nature of the human mind, cannot sustain the rigorous discipline to reach the ultimate goal in a single lifetime. This verse marks a pivotal moment, as it leads Krishna to address the profound question of spiritual continuity and the enduring value of sincere effort, even if incomplete.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **अयतिः (ayatiḥ):** One who is not striving, lacking self-control, unsuccessful in effort. It implies a lack of sustained, disciplined effort despite initial intent.
*   **श्रद्धयोपेतः (śraddhayopetaḥ):** Endowed with faith (`śraddhā`). This is a critical qualifier, indicating that the individual is not a skeptic but a genuine seeker who believes in the path and its teachings.
*   **योगाच्चलितमानसः (yogāccalitamānasaḥ):** Whose mind has deviated or wavered from yoga. `Yoga` here refers to the spiritual discipline or the path of meditation and self-realization. `Calita` means deviated or unsteady, and `mānasaḥ` refers to the mind.
*   **अप्राप्य (aprāpya):** Without attaining or achieving.
*   **योगसंसिद्धिम् (yogasaṁsiddhim):** Perfection or complete success in yoga. `Saṁsiddhi` denotes perfect accomplishment or the ultimate goal.
*   **काम् (kām):** What.
*   **गतिम् (gatim):** Destination, state, or ultimate end.
*   **कृष्ण (kṛṣṇa):** O Krishna (Arjuna addressing his charioteer and guru).
*   **गच्छति (gacchati):** Attains, goes to.

Combined, Arjuna is asking: What happens to a person who has faith, starts the spiritual journey, but whose mind wavers, preventing them from achieving the full perfection of yoga?

### Deeper Understanding
This verse beautifully captures a universal human concern on the spiritual path: the fear of failure or wasted effort. Krishna has just described the ideal yogi, a state that seems almost superhuman. Arjuna, with his practical wisdom, immediately thinks of the vast majority who, despite good intentions and genuine faith, might fall short. 

Krishna's teaching here is profound. He is not just answering a question; he is laying the groundwork for a compassionate understanding of spiritual progress. The key concepts presented are:

1.  **The Value of `Śraddhā` (Faith):** Arjuna explicitly states that the person in question is "endowed with faith." This is crucial. It distinguishes a sincere seeker from a casual experimenter. Krishna will later confirm that sincere faith, even if imperfectly applied, is never in vain.
2.  **The Challenge of the Mind:** The phrase "whose mind deviates from yoga" (`yogāccalitamānasaḥ`) acknowledges the inherent restlessness and fickleness of the mind, a theme Krishna himself addressed earlier in this chapter. It highlights that even with faith, mental discipline is exceedingly difficult.
3.  **The Fear of Incompleteness:** "Failing to attain perfection in yoga" points to the concern that partial efforts might lead to no meaningful outcome. Does spiritual progress reset with each life, or is there a continuity?
4.  **Continuity of Spiritual Effort:** Arjuna's question implicitly asks about the karmic implications of spiritual endeavor. Does spiritual karma differ from other forms of karma? Krishna's answer in subsequent verses will reassure us that spiritual effort, unlike material pursuits, creates an indelible imprint on the soul, ensuring that no genuine spiritual striving is ever lost.

This verse relates deeply to the themes of *karma yoga* (action in devotion) and *jnana yoga* (path of knowledge) by emphasizing that the *intention* and *faith* behind an action are as significant as its outward success. It assures us that the spiritual journey is not a pass/fail test but a continuous evolution of the soul, spanning lifetimes.

### Practical Application
Arjuna's question and Krishna's subsequent answer offer immense solace and practical guidance for modern seekers:

1.  **Embrace Imperfection and Persistence:** Many feel discouraged when their meditation practice isn't deep, their spiritual resolutions falter, or their minds wander. This verse reminds us that even if we are "ayatiḥ" (lacking perfect control) and our minds "calita" (wavering), as long as we have "śraddhā" (faith) and continue to strive, our efforts are meaningful. Don't seek instant perfection; seek consistent effort and gentle return to the path.
2.  **Cultivate Unwavering Faith (`Śraddhā`):** In the face of setbacks, the most important anchor is faith in the process, in the teachings, and in our own potential. This faith isn't blind belief but a deep conviction born from understanding and experience. When the mind deviates, faith provides the impetus to gently bring it back.
3.  **Redefine "Success" on the Spiritual Path:** The Gita teaches that true spiritual success isn't always about achieving a dramatic, final state in this lifetime. It's about the journey itself, the purification of the heart, the deepening of understanding, and the cultivation of virtues. Every sincere step taken contributes to our spiritual evolution, building a foundation for future progress, whether in this life or the next.

*   **Challenges of the Mind:** Acknowledgment of the mind's inherent restlessness and its tendency to deviate from focused practice.
*   **Continuity of Spiritual Effort:** The reassurance that genuine spiritual striving is never wasted and contributes to the soul's ongoing evolution.
*   **The Sincere Seeker:** Arjuna's question focuses on those who are truly committed but struggle, highlighting compassion for the human condition on the spiritual path.
