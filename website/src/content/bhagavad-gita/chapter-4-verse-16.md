## Explanation

### Context
This verse is situated in Chapter 4 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled 'Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga,' which translates to 'The Yoga of Knowledge and the Renunciation of Action.' In this chapter, Lord Krishna explains the nature of knowledge and its relationship with action, especially in the context of performing one's duties with the right understanding. Here, Krishna addresses Arjuna's doubts about the nature of action and inaction, encouraging him to seek clarity through wisdom.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **किमर्थं (kimarthaṁ)**: 'What is the purpose?' - This term expresses a quest for understanding the rationale behind action.
- **किमस्ति (kimasti)**: 'What is?' - Invites contemplation on the nature of existence and actions.
- **कर्मणः (karmaṇaḥ)**: 'Of action' - Refers to the actions one undertakes in life.
- **कर्तव्यं (kartavyaṁ)**: 'That which ought to be done' - Indicates duties and responsibilities.
- **न (na)**: 'Not' - Denotes negation or absence.
- **ज्ञायते (jñāyate)**: 'Is known' - Pertains to knowledge or awareness.
- **अज्ञानं (ajñānaṁ)**: 'Ignorance' - Refers to the lack of understanding, especially about duty.
- **कर्मणां (karmaṇāṁ)**: 'Of actions' - Points to the actions one engages in.
- **दुष्टं (duṣṭaṁ)**: 'Obstructive' - Highlights the negative aspect of ignorance.
- **ज्ञानं (jñānaṁ)**: 'Knowledge' - The understanding that leads to clarity and purpose.
- **कर्मसिद्धये (karmasiddhaye)**: 'For the perfection of action' - Indicates that knowledge is essential for the successful execution of duties.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna is guiding Arjuna to understand the profound relationship between knowledge and action. He underscores that ignorance of the nature of action leads to confusion and missteps in fulfilling one’s duties. Krishna challenges Arjuna to reflect on what it truly means to act and what constitutes inaction. 

Krishna emphasizes that without knowledge, one cannot discern the right path, leading to ineffective actions or inaction altogether. This aligns with the broader themes of the Gita, where understanding one’s dharma (duty) and the nature of one’s actions is crucial for spiritual and practical success. 

The verse also accentuates the importance of self-awareness and reflection. By recognizing the barriers posed by ignorance, individuals can strive for knowledge, which in turn facilitates effective action aligned with their true purpose in life. 

### Practical Application
1. **Self-Reflection**: Modern readers can apply this teaching by taking time to reflect on their daily actions. Before making decisions, consider the purpose behind those actions. Are they aligned with your values and responsibilities?
2. **Education and Learning**: Invest in acquiring knowledge relevant to your field or personal interests. Understanding the 'why' and 'how' of your work can lead to greater effectiveness and satisfaction in your actions.
3. **Mindful Decision-Making**: Employ mindfulness in your daily activities. Before acting, pause to gain clarity on the nature of that action—whether it serves a greater purpose or merely reacts to external pressures.
