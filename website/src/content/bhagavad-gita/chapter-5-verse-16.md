## Explanation

### Context
In Chapter 5, 'Karma Sanyasa Yoga' (The Yoga of Renunciation of Action), Lord Krishna continues to elaborate on the superiority of action performed without attachment over mere physical renunciation. He clarifies that true renunciation is not inaction but rather the internal detachment from the fruits of action, performed with wisdom. Prior verses explained that the embodied soul, deluded by ignorance, attributes actions and their results to itself, even though it is *Prakriti* (nature) that operates. This verse builds upon that foundation by presenting the solution to this fundamental delusion: the light of knowledge. It sets the stage for understanding how one transcends the cycle of karma and attains liberation by dispelling the deep-seated ignorance that causes identification with the material world and its transient phenomena.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **ज्ञानेन (jñānena)**: By knowledge. This refers to profound spiritual wisdom, particularly the knowledge of the Self (Atman).
*   **तु (tu)**: But, indeed. It introduces a contrasting or emphatic point.
*   **तत् (tat)**: That. Refers back to the ignorance previously mentioned or implied as the cause of delusion.
*   **अज्ञानम् (ajñānam)**: Ignorance; nescience. This is the root cause of suffering and bondage, the mistaken identity of the Self with the non-Self (body, mind, ego).
*   **येषां (yeṣāṁ)**: Whose, for whom.
*   **नाशितम् (nāśitam)**: Destroyed, annihilated.
*   **आत्मनः (ātmanaḥ)**: Of the Self, belonging to the Self, or by the Self. This implies that the knowledge is of one's true spiritual identity.
*   **तेषाम् (teṣām)**: For them.
*   **आदित्यवत् (ādityavat)**: Like the sun. This is a powerful simile, emphasizing the illuminating and all-encompassing nature of this knowledge.
*   **ज्ञानम् (jñānam)**: Knowledge; specifically, the same Self-knowledge mentioned earlier.
*   **प्रकाशयति (prakāśayati)**: Illuminates, reveals, makes clear.
*   **तत्परम् (tatparam)**: The Supreme, the Highest Truth, the Ultimate Reality (Brahman).

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna here delivers a profound truth about the nature of spiritual liberation. The central teaching is that ignorance (`ajñāna`) is not merely a lack of information but a fundamental misapprehension of reality, a veil that obscures our true nature and the ultimate truth. This ignorance is the cause of all suffering, attachment, and the cycle of rebirth (samsara).

The antidote is not external action or ritual but internal `jñāna` – the knowledge of the Self (`ātman`). This is not intellectual understanding alone, but an experiential realization, a profound awakening to one's true identity as distinct from the perishable body and mind. The analogy of the sun (`ādityavat`) is crucial: the sun does not *create* objects but merely *reveals* them by dispelling darkness. Similarly, self-knowledge does not create the Supreme Truth (`tatparam`); rather, it removes the veil of ignorance, allowing the inherent, ever-present reality to be perceived. The Supreme Truth is not something to be acquired but something to be realized as eternally existing.

This verse connects directly to the core themes of the Gita: the nature of the Self, the distinction between *Purusha* (consciousness) and *Prakriti* (matter), and the path to liberation (*moksha*). It emphasizes that once ignorance is destroyed by the radiant light of self-knowledge, the individual effortlessly perceives the ultimate reality, transcending all dualities and limitations imposed by the deluded mind. It is a state of being where the individual soul recognizes its non-dual nature with the Supreme Brahman.

### Practical Application
1.  **Cultivating Self-Inquiry:** Regularly engage in introspection and self-inquiry. Ask yourself: "Who am I, really? Am I this body, these thoughts, these emotions, or something deeper?" This consistent questioning helps to chip away at the layers of false identification and ignorance.
2.  **Mindfulness and Discerning Awareness:** Practice mindfulness to observe your thoughts, emotions, and bodily sensations without identifying with them. Recognize them as transient phenomena, distinct from the unchanging witness consciousness that is your true Self. This creates a psychological distance from the *ajñāna* that binds us.
3.  **Study and Contemplation of Wisdom Texts:** Engage with spiritual texts like the Bhagavad Gita, Upanishads, or other wisdom traditions. Don't just read, but contemplate their teachings deeply. Discuss them, meditate on them, and strive to integrate their insights into your understanding of yourself and the world. This helps to reinforce the knowledge that dispels ignorance.

## Key Concepts

1.  **Knowledge as Destroyer of Ignorance (Jñāna-Nāśita-Ajñāna):** The central idea that spiritual knowledge is the sole means to annihilate the fundamental ignorance that causes suffering.
2.  **The Self (Ātman):** The verse emphasizes that this knowledge is specifically of one's true, eternal, spiritual Self, distinct from the temporary body and mind.
3.  **The Supreme Truth (Tatparam):** The ultimate reality, Brahman, which is revealed once the veil of ignorance is lifted.
4.  **Analogy of the Sun (Ādityavat):** A powerful metaphor illustrating that knowledge doesn't create truth but merely illuminates what is already present, by dispelling darkness.
