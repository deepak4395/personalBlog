## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled "Jnana Vijnana Yoga," or the "Yoga of Knowledge and Realization," is a pivotal section where Lord Krishna systematically reveals His supreme position as the ultimate reality, the source and sustainer of all existence. Having previously described His material and spiritual energies and how He pervades everything, Krishna now addresses the various approaches people take in their spiritual pursuits. In verses 20 and 21, He discusses those whose intelligence has been carried away by material desires, leading them to worship other deities. He clarifies that He Himself, as the indwelling Supersoul, steadies the faith of such a devotee in their chosen form of worship. Verse 22 builds directly upon this, explaining the ultimate source of the results obtained from such worship, further cementing Krishna's omnipotence and omnipresence.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
Let's break down the key terms in this powerful verse:
*   **स (sa):** He (referring to the devotee mentioned in the previous verses)
*   **तया (tayā):** With that
*   **श्रद्धया (śraddhayā):** faith (the faith that Krishna Himself steadies, as per verse 21)
*   **युक्तः (yuktaḥ):** endowed, connected, joined
*   **तस्याः (tasyāḥ):** of that (referring to the specific deity)
*   **आराधनम् (ārādhanam):** worship, adoration
*   **ईहते (īhate):** endeavors, performs, seeks
*   **लभते (labhate):** obtains, achieves
*   **च (ca):** and
*   **ततः (tataḥ):** from that (worship)
*   **कामान् (kāmān):** desires, objects of desire, material benefits
*   **मया (mayā):** by Me (Krishna)
*   **एव (eva):** alone, certainly
*   **विहितान् (vihitān):** ordained, arranged, prescribed, granted
*   **हि (hi):** indeed, certainly
*   **तान् (tān):** those (desires)

Thus, the verse states that the individual, imbued with the faith Krishna Himself provides, engages in the worship of their chosen deity, and the desires they seek are ultimately granted by Krishna alone.

### Deeper Understanding
This verse offers a profound insight into the nature of divine power and the interconnectedness of all spiritual paths. Krishna is teaching Arjuna, and by extension, all humanity, that while individuals may direct their devotion towards various forms of deities (devatas) for specific material boons – be it wealth, health, progeny, or success – the ultimate orchestrator and bestower of these results is none other than Krishna Himself. 

This doesn't invalidate the worship of other deities; rather, it places it within a larger, more comprehensive divine framework. The devatas (like Indra, Vayu, Surya, etc.) are powerful celestial beings who administer various aspects of the cosmic creation, but their power is not independent. Their authority and ability to grant boons are delegated by and derived from the Supreme Lord, Krishna. He is the `Ishvara`, the supreme controller, the ultimate source of all energies and all manifestations.

Krishna's statement, "mayāiva vihitān hi tān" (which are indeed ordained by Me alone), emphasizes His omnipresence and omnipotence. It means that no matter what form of the divine one approaches, the power that responds and the intelligence that orchestrates the outcome ultimately emanate from Him. He is the underlying reality that empowers all. This teaching helps to resolve the apparent multiplicity of gods in the Vedic tradition by revealing a singular, supreme consciousness that governs all.

This verse also subtly highlights the difference between temporary material gains and ultimate spiritual liberation. While Krishna facilitates the fulfillment of material desires for those who worship other deities, these results are by nature transient. True liberation and eternal happiness come from understanding Krishna's supreme position and directing one's devotion directly towards Him, transcending the need for temporary boons. It connects to the theme of `karma-phala` (fruits of action) and `karma-yoga` (action in devotion), indicating that even the fruits of `sakama-karma` (action with desire) are divinely administered.

### Practical Application
This ancient wisdom holds immense relevance for modern life:
1.  **Cultivate a Universal Perspective on Source:** In our daily lives, we often attribute success or failure to specific people, institutions, or our own efforts. This verse encourages us to look beyond immediate causes and recognize a deeper, universal divine hand orchestrating outcomes. Whether we achieve a promotion, recover from an illness, or find solutions to problems, understanding that the ultimate power facilitating these events is divine can foster humility and a profound sense of gratitude. It shifts our focus from solely external factors to the underlying spiritual reality.
2.  **Reduce Anxiety and Attachment to Outcomes:** When we understand that all results, good or bad, are ultimately ordained by a supreme intelligence, it can help us detach from the intense anxiety surrounding specific outcomes. We still put in our best effort, but we surrender the results to a higher power, trusting in divine providence. This can lead to greater inner peace and resilience in the face of life's unpredictable nature.
3.  **Deepen Gratitude and Spiritual Connection:** Every blessing, every moment of grace, every fulfilled desire, can be seen as a direct gift from the ultimate divine source. This perspective transforms ordinary experiences into opportunities for spiritual connection and gratitude. It encourages us to offer thanks not just for the 'what' but for the 'who' behind all existence, fostering a more continuous and profound spiritual awareness in daily life.

## Key Concepts
*   **Divine Omnipotence:** Krishna's supreme power as the ultimate bestower of all results, regardless of the object of worship.
*   **Ultimate Controller (Ishvara):** The concept that Krishna is the singular, supreme ruler and orchestrator of all cosmic affairs.
*   **Derived Power:** The understanding that other deities' abilities to grant boons are not independent but are delegated and empowered by the Supreme Lord.
*   **Conditional Fulfillment:** While material desires are granted, they are temporary, contrasting with the eternal liberation offered by direct devotion to Krishna.
