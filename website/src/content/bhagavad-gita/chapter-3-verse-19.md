## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled 'Karma Yoga,' addresses Arjuna's confusion regarding the superiority of action (karma) versus renunciation of action (sannyasa). Having just explained that even the wise act for the welfare of the world (lokasaṅgraha) and that inaction is impossible for any embodied being, Krishna now provides a direct and powerful instruction. Verses 17 and 18 established that a self-realized person, free from desires and with no personal gain or loss from action, still participates in the world. This verse (3.19) builds upon that foundation, offering a practical directive for all individuals, regardless of their spiritual advancement, to engage in action in a specific manner to achieve the highest spiritual goal.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **तस्मात् (Tasmāt):** Therefore. This word links the instruction to the preceding arguments, indicating that what follows is a logical conclusion derived from Krishna's earlier explanations about the nature of action and the wise.
*   **असक्तः (Asaktaḥ):** Without attachment, unattached. This is the pivotal term, signifying freedom from clinging to the results of one's actions, the sense of doership, or the desire for personal gain.
*   **सततं (Satataṃ):** Always, constantly. Emphasizes that this mode of action is not occasional but a continuous state of being.
*   **कार्यं (Kāryaṃ):** To be done, obligatory, necessary. Refers to one's duties, responsibilities, or actions essential for sustenance and societal harmony.
*   **कर्म (Karma):** Action, work, deed.
*   **समाचर (Samācara):** Perform well, execute properly, practice. An imperative verb, a direct command to Arjuna to engage in action diligently.
*   **असक्तो (Asakto):** (Again) Without attachment. The repetition reinforces the supreme importance of detachment.
*   **हि (Hi):** Indeed, for, because. Provides the reason for the instruction.
*   **आचरन् (Ācaran):** Performing, while acting.
*   **कर्म (Karma):** Action.
*   **परमाप्नोति (Paramāpnoti):** Attains the Supreme. (Parama + āpnoti = Supreme + attains). This signifies the highest spiritual realization, liberation, or union with the Divine.
*   **पूरुषः (Pūruṣaḥ):** A person, the individual soul.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna's teaching in this verse is the very essence of Karma Yoga. He is not asking Arjuna to abandon his duties but to transform the *way* he performs them. The instruction is twofold: first, identify the 'work that must be done' (kāryaṃ karma), which refers to one's prescribed duties (svadharma), ethical responsibilities, and actions necessary for the maintenance of life and society. Second, and crucially, perform these actions 'without attachment' (asaktaḥ).

Detachment here means freedom from: 
1.  **Attachment to the fruits of action (karma-phala-tyaga):** Not being driven by the desire for specific outcomes, rewards, or recognition.
2.  **Attachment to the doership (ahankara):** Not identifying oneself as the sole agent of action, but rather as an instrument of a higher purpose or the cosmic will.
3.  **Attachment to the process itself in a self-serving way:** Performing the action for its inherent value or as an offering, rather than for personal gratification derived from the act.

By acting without attachment, the mind is purified. Actions cease to create new karmic bonds that tie the individual to the cycle of birth and death (samsara). Instead, they become a means of spiritual evolution. The promise is profound: 'one attains the Supreme' (paramāpnoti pūruṣaḥ). This 'Supreme' can be understood as self-realization, moksha (liberation), or the ultimate union with Brahman. This verse beautifully integrates action with spiritual liberation, demonstrating that the path to the highest truth does not necessarily require withdrawal from the world but a reorientation of one's engagement with it. It connects Karma Yoga directly to the ultimate goals of Jnana Yoga (knowledge) and Bhakti Yoga (devotion), as a purified mind is receptive to higher knowledge and capable of unadulterated devotion.

### Practical Application
1.  **Professional Life:** In today's competitive world, we often tie our self-worth to career success, promotions, or project outcomes. Applying this teaching means focusing diligently on the quality of your work, putting in your best effort, and fulfilling your responsibilities, but without excessive anxiety or elation over the results. If a project succeeds, acknowledge it, but don't let it inflate your ego; if it fails, learn from it without crushing your spirit. Your peace of mind becomes independent of external outcomes.
2.  **Relationships and Family Duties:** Many conflicts arise from expectations and attachments in relationships. When performing duties for family members – cooking, caring for children, supporting a spouse – do so with love and dedication, but without expecting a specific return, appreciation, or reciprocation. This shifts the focus from transactional giving to selfless service, fostering healthier, more harmonious relationships and reducing resentment.
3.  **Daily Chores and Responsibilities:** Even mundane tasks like cleaning your home, doing laundry, or running errands can be transformed. Instead of viewing them as burdensome chores, approach them as 'work that must be done' for the upkeep of your environment and well-being. Perform them mindfully and efficiently, without complaining or expecting praise. This cultivates a sense of peace and presence, turning routine into a form of meditation.

### Reflection
*   What are the 'kāryaṃ karma' (duties to be done) in my life today, and how can I approach them with greater detachment?
*   How much of my current stress or anxiety stems from attachment to the outcomes of my actions?
*   Can I identify one area where I can consciously practice performing an action without expecting a specific result or recognition?
*   How might my experience of life change if I consistently applied this principle of 'action without attachment'?

## Key Concepts
*   **Karma Yoga:** The spiritual path emphasizing selfless action as a means to liberation.
*   **Detachment (Asakti):** Performing actions without personal desire for their fruits or identification with doership.
*   **Duty (Kāryaṃ Karma/Svadharma):** The performance of one's obligatory and necessary actions.
*   **Attainment of the Supreme (Paramāpti):** The ultimate spiritual goal of self-realization, liberation, or union with the Divine, achievable through detached action.
