## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 4, "Jnana Karma Sanyasa Yoga," delves into the nature of divine action, the lineage of spiritual knowledge, and various forms of sacrifices (yajnas). Krishna has been elucidating how actions performed without attachment to results, imbued with knowledge, do not bind an individual. He has distinguished between action, inaction, and forbidden action, emphasizing that true action is that which leads to liberation. This verse continues to elaborate on the characteristics of such a liberated individual, building upon the principles of selfless action (karma yoga) and enlightened knowledge (jnana yoga). It describes the mental state and attitude of one who performs actions yet remains untouched by their karmic consequences, a state achieved through wisdom and detachment. It is a profound description of a *jivanmukta*, one who is liberated while still living.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **यदृच्छालाभसन्तुष्टो (yadṛcchālābhasantuṣṭaḥ):** Content with whatever gains come spontaneously or unsought. This highlights a mind free from craving specific results, accepting what life offers.
*   **द्वन्द्वातीतो (dvandvātītaḥ):** One who has transcended the pairs of opposites (e.g., joy/sorrow, heat/cold, honor/dishonor). This denotes emotional and mental stability, unswayed by life's dualities.
*   **विमत्सरः (vimatsaraḥ):** Free from envy or jealousy. This signifies a pure heart, unburdened by comparisons or resentment towards others' successes.
*   **समः सिद्धावसिद्धौ च (samaḥ siddhāvasiddhau ca):** Equipoised in success and failure. Maintaining mental balance, neither elated by victory nor dejected by defeat.
*   **कृत्वापि (kṛtvāpi):** Though performing actions, even while engaged in activity.
*   **न निबध्यते (na nibadhyate):** He is not bound. The actions of such a person do not create karmic chains.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna outlines the characteristics of a liberated Karma Yogi, who integrates spiritual knowledge (Jnana) with action to achieve freedom from karmic bondage.

-   **Contentment (yadṛcchālābhasantuṣṭaḥ):** This signifies inner satisfaction independent of specific external gains. It means working diligently without attachment to particular results, accepting what comes as a natural outcome. This freedom from craving (tṛṣṇā) reflects deep trust in the cosmic order or a realistic acceptance of life's unpredictability, fostering a state of inner sufficiency rather than passivity.

-   **Transcending Dualities (dvandvātītaḥ):** The world presents pairs of opposites—pleasure/pain, heat/cold, success/failure. While most people's happiness fluctuates with these external conditions, the liberated individual maintains inner peace regardless of circumstances. This equanimity is not indifference but a profound understanding that these dualities are transient and do not define one's true Self. It's a state of inner stability, akin to the 'sthitaprajña' (steady intellect) described earlier.

-   **Freedom from Envy (vimatsaraḥ):** Envy (matsara) is a destructive emotion born from comparisons and resentment of others' success. A person free from envy possesses a pure heart, genuinely celebrates others, and focuses on their own spiritual journey without debilitating comparisons. This purity is vital for spiritual progress, as envy binds one to ego and worldly desires.

-   **Equanimity in Success and Failure (samaḥ siddhāvasiddhau ca):** This emphasizes maintaining mental equilibrium regardless of the outcomes of actions. Neither elation from success nor dejection from failure disturbs the inner peace. This is central to Karma Yoga, where the focus is on the action itself (karma) rather than its fruit (phala). Such an attitude allows one to perform duties with full dedication, free from anxiety about results.

-   **Unbound by Action (kṛtvāpi na nibadhyate):** This is the ultimate promise. Despite performing actions, such an individual is not bound by them. This freedom arises because their actions are performed without attachment, egoic desire, or agitation over results. Their actions transform into 'akarma' (non-binding action), becoming an offering or a natural expression of their being, rather than a means to personal gain. This aligns with the concept of performing actions as a yajna (sacrifice) to a higher principle, making them purifying rather than binding.

This verse beautifully encapsulates how an individual can live fully in the world, performing all duties, yet remain inwardly free and untouched by the karmic consequences, achieving liberation while still embodied.

### Practical Application
1.  **Cultivate Contentment with 'Serendipitous Gains':** Instead of constantly striving for more or specific outcomes, practice gratitude for what you already have and what comes your way unexpectedly. For example, if you're looking for a new job, apply diligently, but be content with the opportunities that arise, even if they aren't exactly what you envisioned. This reduces anxiety and fosters a sense of peace.
2.  **Practice Equanimity in Daily Life:** Observe your reactions to small successes and failures. Did you get a promotion? Be happy, but don't let it inflate your ego. Did a project fail? Learn from it, but don't let it define your worth. Try to maintain a balanced emotional state, recognizing that both success and failure are temporary aspects of life's flow.
3.  **Address Envy Consciously:** When you feel a pang of jealousy towards someone else's achievement or possession, pause. Recognize the emotion, understand its root (often a feeling of lack in oneself), and then consciously shift your focus to your own path and blessings. Celebrate others' successes as if they were your own, fostering a sense of interconnectedness rather than competition.

## Key Concepts

1.  **Yadṛcchālābhasantuṣṭa (Contentment with Unsolicited Gains):** A state of inner satisfaction independent of specific desires or planned outcomes, accepting what comes spontaneously.
2.  **Dvandvātīta (Transcendence of Dualities):** Maintaining mental and emotional equilibrium beyond the pairs of opposites like pleasure/pain, success/failure, honor/dishonor.
3.  **Vimatsara (Freedom from Envy):** Purity of heart, free from jealousy or malice towards others' achievements or possessions.
4.  **Akarmic Action (Action without Binding):** The principle that actions performed with detachment, equanimity, and without egoic desire for results do not create karmic bondage, leading to liberation.
