## Explanation

### Context
Chapter 5 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled "Karma Sanyasa Yoga," aims to reconcile the paths of *sannyasa* (renunciation of actions) and *karma yoga* (action performed without attachment to results). Krishna clarifies that both, when rightly understood and practiced, lead to the same ultimate goal of liberation. He emphasizes that true renunciation is not inaction, but action performed with a detached mind, seeing the divine in all. Building upon earlier verses that describe the steady-minded individual (verse 20) and the joy of inner realization (verse 24), Verse 25 continues to delineate the characteristics and ultimate destiny of such liberated souls, the *ṛṣayaḥ* or sages, highlighting both their internal purification and their external benevolent actions.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
*   **लभन्ते (labhante)**: Attain, obtain, achieve.
*   **ब्रह्मनिर्वाणम् (brahma-nirvāṇam)**: The liberation of Brahman; the ultimate peace, freedom, and bliss of identifying with the Absolute Reality. *Nirvana* signifies the cessation of suffering and the individual ego, leading to union with Brahman.
*   **ऋषयः (ṛṣayaḥ)**: Sages, seers, wise ones. Those who possess profound spiritual insight and wisdom.
*   **क्षीणकल्मषाः (kṣīṇa-kalmaṣāḥ)**: Whose sins (kalmaṣāḥ) are destroyed or diminished (kṣīṇa). This refers to the eradication of negative karmic impressions, impurities, and the root causes of unrighteous actions.
*   **छिन्नद्वैधाः (chinna-dvaidhāḥ)**: Whose doubts or dualities (dvaidhāḥ) are cut asunder or dispelled (chinna). Freedom from mental conflicts, uncertainties, and the perception of separation.
*   **यतात्मानः (yatātmānaḥ)**: Whose minds or selves (ātmānaḥ) are disciplined or controlled (yata). Those who have mastery over their senses and mental faculties.
*   **सर्वभूतहिते (sarva-bhūta-hite)**: In the welfare or good (hite) of all beings (sarva-bhūta). This denotes universal benevolence.
*   **रताः (ratāḥ)**: Delighting in, devoted to, engaged in.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna, as the divine teacher, is presenting a comprehensive picture of what it means to be truly liberated, an *ṛṣi* who has attained *brahma-nirvāṇa*. This liberation is not a passive state but an active, dynamic engagement with life, characterized by profound inner purity and expansive compassion.

1.  **Freedom from Sin (क्षीणकल्मषाः - kṣīṇa-kalmaṣāḥ):** This goes beyond merely avoiding wrong actions. It signifies the complete eradication of the inner tendencies that lead to unrighteousness – ignorance, ego, attachment, and aversion. When the mind is purified at its root, negative karmic impressions cease to accumulate, and one's actions naturally align with universal dharma.
2.  **Freedom from Doubt (छिन्नद्वैधाः - chinna-dvaidhāḥ):** Doubts are major impediments to spiritual progress. They arise from a fragmented understanding of reality and the mind's tendency to perceive dualities (self/other, good/bad, sacred/profane). A sage who has realized the non-dual nature of Brahman transcends these mental constructs. All uncertainties about one's true nature, the purpose of existence, and the path to liberation dissolve, replaced by unshakeable conviction and clarity. This is the hallmark of true spiritual wisdom (*jnana*).
3.  **Self-Mastery (यतात्मानः - yatātmānaḥ):** Discipline and control over one's mind and senses are foundational. A liberated being is not swayed by external temptations or internal impulses. They are masters of their inner world, able to direct their energies towards higher ideals without distraction. This control is not a forceful suppression but a harmonious alignment of the individual self with the universal Self, achieved through consistent practice and introspection.
4.  **Universal Benevolence (सर्वभूतहिते रताः - sarva-bhūta-hite ratāḥ):** This is a profound and crucial aspect of liberation highlighted in this verse. True spiritual realization is never solely an individualistic pursuit. One who perceives the unity of all existence (Brahman) naturally extends boundless compassion and actively works for the welfare of all beings. Seeing the divine essence in everyone and everything, their actions become selfless service, an inherent expression of their enlightened state. This quality beautifully integrates *karma yoga* into the state of *brahma-nirvana*, demonstrating that inner peace naturally manifests as outward universal love and action for the good of the world.

This verse beautifully synthesizes the paths of *jnana* (wisdom leading to freedom from doubt), *tapas* (self-discipline leading to self-mastery), and *karma* (selfless action leading to universal welfare) as intertwined characteristics and ultimate achievements of one who attains *brahma-nirvana*. It emphasizes that inner purity and outward compassion are inseparable facets of genuine spiritual attainment.

### Practical Application
1.  **Cultivate Universal Compassion (Sarva-Bhuta-Hite Ratāḥ):** Actively seek opportunities to contribute to the welfare of others, not just humans, but all living beings. This can be through volunteering, supporting ethical causes, practicing kindness and empathy in daily interactions, or being mindful of your environmental impact. The key is to perform these actions without expectation of personal reward, seeing them as an offering for the greater good, thereby dissolving the ego's hold.
2.  **Address Inner Doubts (Chinna-Dvaidhāḥ):** Engage in consistent self-inquiry, study spiritual texts, meditate, and seek guidance from wise teachers to systematically understand and resolve your persistent doubts about life, purpose, and spiritual truths. A clear, doubt-free mind is a powerful instrument for spiritual progress. When a doubt arises, instead of avoiding it, face it with an open mind and seek understanding from various perspectives.
3.  **Practice Self-Discipline (Yatātmānaḥ):** Work on gaining mastery over your mind and senses. This doesn't necessarily mean extreme asceticism, but rather conscious choices in your diet, speech, habits, and thought patterns. Regular meditation, mindfulness practices, setting healthy boundaries, and mindful consumption can help you become the master of your inner world, rather than being a slave to external impulses and desires.

### Reflection
*   How do my daily actions reflect a concern for the welfare of all beings, or are they predominantly self-serving?
*   What are the persistent doubts or inner conflicts that hinder my spiritual journey, and what concrete steps can I take to resolve them?
*   In what areas of my life can I cultivate greater self-discipline to align my actions with my higher values and spiritual aspirations?
