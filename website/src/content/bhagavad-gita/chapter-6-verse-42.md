## Explanation

### Context
In Chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, titled 'Atma Samyama Yoga', Lord Krishna elaborates on the practice of yoga and the importance of self-discipline. This chapter is pivotal as it addresses the nature of the mind, meditation, and the paths to spiritual realization. Verse 42 specifically reassures practitioners who may feel disheartened by their perceived failures in spiritual pursuits. It emphasizes that the journey of the soul is continuous and that even setbacks can lead to favorable circumstances in subsequent lives.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **अथवा (athavā)**: Or
- **योगिनाम् (yoginām)**: of the yogis
- **एव (eva)**: certainly
- **कुले (kule)**: in a family
- **जन्म (janma)**: birth
- **आदिमतां (ādimatāṁ)**: of those who are wise or intelligent
- **गतिः (gatiḥ)**: destination or path
- **तत्र (tatra)**: there
- **तं (taṁ)**: that
- **बुद्धि-संयोगं (buddhi-saṁyogaṁ)**: union of intellect or wisdom
- **लभते (labhate)**: attains
- **पौर्व-देहिकम् (paurva-dehikam)**: from previous bodies.

This breakdown reveals the verse's assurance that even if a yogi falls short, their past efforts will not be wasted but will guide them in their next birth.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna addresses a common fear among spiritual seekers: the fear of failure. He reassures Arjuna that the spiritual journey is not linear and that every effort made towards self-realization is valuable. The idea that one can be reborn into a family of wise individuals reflects the law of karma—the belief that our actions, whether successful or unsuccessful, have consequences that shape our future.

Krishna teaches that a failed yogi will not be abandoned but instead will find themselves in a nurturing environment that fosters further spiritual growth. This highlights the concept of grace and divine support in the journey of self-discovery. The key concepts presented in this verse bridge the gap between personal effort and the cosmic order, enhancing our understanding of dharma, karma, and the interconnectedness of all beings.

### Practical Application
In modern life, this teaching can serve as a profound source of encouragement. Here are a few ways to apply it:
1. **Embrace Setbacks**: Recognize that failures in your personal or spiritual life are not the end. Reflect on what you have learned from those experiences and understand that they contribute to your growth. For instance, if you struggle with meditation, instead of giving up, consider the insights gained about your mind and its patterns.
2. **Seek Supportive Communities**: Surround yourself with people who inspire and uplift you. Just as Krishna mentions being reborn into a wise family, being part of a community that values wisdom can help you continue your journey effectively. Engage with spiritual groups, attend workshops, or find a mentor who can guide you.
3. **Cultivate Patience**: Understand that spiritual evolution takes time. Recognize and appreciate the small steps you take every day toward your goals. Celebrate your progress, no matter how minor it may seem.

### Reflection
Consider these questions:  
- How do you react to failure in your life?  
- What lessons have you learned from past setbacks?  
- How can you create an environment that supports your spiritual growth?

1. **Rebirth and Continuity**: The assurance that our efforts are never wasted and can lead to future growth.  
2. **The Role of Community**: The importance of being in a supportive environment that encourages wisdom and spiritual practice.  
3. **Grace and Divine Support**: The idea that higher forces aid our journey, even in times of struggle.  
4. **Interconnectedness of Actions**: Understanding that our past actions shape our present and future circumstances.
