## Explanation

### Context
This verse is part of the second chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, which is known as Sankhya Yoga, or the Yoga of Knowledge. In this chapter, Lord Krishna imparts crucial teachings to Arjuna, who is confused and morally troubled about fighting in the battle of Kurukshetra. Krishna emphasizes the importance of understanding one's duty (dharma) and the nature of the self (atman). Verse 41 serves as a pivotal point in Krishna's discourse where he contrasts resolute and scattered intellects, guiding Arjuna towards clarity of thought and purpose.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **व्यवसायात्मिका (vyavasāyātmikā)**: Resolute or determined. This term implies a focused and directed approach in one's actions and thoughts.
- **बुद्धिः (buddhiḥ)**: Intellect or understanding. Represents the cognitive faculties that guide decision-making.
- **एकेह (eke)**: One-pointed or singular. This indicates a focus on a single goal or purpose.
- **कुरुनन्दन (kurunandana)**: O son of the Kurus. A term of endearment used by Krishna to address Arjuna, linking him to his royal heritage.
- **बहुशाखा (bahuśākhā)**: Many branches or multifaceted. Suggests a lack of focus, with many distractions.
- **अनन्ताश्च (anantāśca)**: Endless or infinite. Implies that a scattered intellect leads to infinite possibilities but no concrete outcome.
- **बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम् (buddhiyo'vyavasāyinām)**: The intellect of the undecided. Refers to those who lack resolution and focus in their thoughts and actions.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna is teaching Arjuna about the importance of having a resolute intellect. The term "vyavasāyātmikā buddhi" indicates that a focused mind leads to clarity and effective action. In contrast, a mind that is scattered and undecided leads to confusion and inaction. This dichotomy reflects a fundamental teaching in the Gita about the nature of the mind and the importance of mental discipline.

The verse resonates with the broader themes of dharma and karma. When an individual is clear about their duty and purpose, their actions align with their higher self, leading to righteousness and fulfillment. Conversely, being indecisive can result in missed opportunities and a lack of direction in life.

Krishna's teachings here are not merely about intellectual focus; they also encompass emotional and spiritual clarity. An individual who is clear in their intentions is more likely to engage in fulfilling activities that contribute to their spiritual growth, whereas distractions can hinder this journey.

### Practical Application
1. **Goal Setting**: In modern life, one can apply the teachings of this verse by setting clear, achievable goals. By focusing on specific objectives, individuals can channel their energy effectively and avoid the paralysis that comes from indecision.
2. **Mindfulness Practices**: Incorporating mindfulness or meditation into daily routines can help cultivate a one-pointed focus. By training the mind to be present, individuals can reduce distractions and enhance their ability to make decisive actions aligned with their values.
3. **Prioritization**: In a world filled with choices and distractions, prioritizing tasks based on their alignment with personal and professional goals can lead to greater productivity and satisfaction. This practice echoes the essence of having a resolute intellect as emphasized by Krishna.
