## Explanation

### Context
This verse belongs to Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, which is focused on Karma Yoga, the path of selfless action. In this chapter, Lord Krishna emphasizes the importance of performing one's prescribed duties without attachment to the results. The dialogue takes place on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, where Arjuna, a warrior prince, is confused about his duty in the face of moral dilemmas. Krishna guides him toward understanding the nature of action and the importance of selfless service.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **yastu**: He who; refers to a person embodying certain qualities.
- **karma**: Action; the deeds or duties one is expected to perform.
- **phala**: Fruit; the results or outcomes of actions.
- **tyāgī**: Renouncer; someone who gives up attachment to outcomes.
- **san-tuṣṭo**: Always satisfied; denotes contentment independent of external factors.
- **ananuśāsanaḥ**: Not dependent on external rules; refers to someone who is not swayed by the dictates of circumstance.
- **uttiṣṭhati**: Stands up; signifies taking action.

- **yaḥ**: He who; referring back to the subject.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna teaches Arjuna about the essence of selfless action. The key concepts presented here include:
- **Non-Attachment**: The verse stresses that one should perform their duties without being attached to the results. This detachment is crucial for spiritual growth; when we are overly focused on outcomes, we often experience anxiety and disappointment.
- **Contentment**: Being satisfied regardless of external circumstances is a powerful spiritual practice. It reflects an inner state of peace that is not contingent upon success or failure.
- **Karma and Dharma**: The concepts of karma (action) and dharma (duty) are intertwined in this teaching. By performing one's dharma without desire for personal gain, one transcends the binding nature of karma, leading to liberation.

### Practical Application
1. **Work Ethic**: In a modern context, this teaching encourages individuals to focus on their work and responsibilities without being overly concerned about promotions or accolades. For instance, a teacher should focus on the quality of education they provide rather than whether they receive recognition for it.
2. **Stress Reduction**: By practicing non-attachment, individuals can alleviate stress. Instead of fixating on the outcome of a project or goal, one can take satisfaction in the effort and learning process itself. For example, an athlete can focus on training and improvement rather than solely on winning a competition.
3. **Mindfulness**: Incorporating mindfulness into daily tasks, such as enjoying the process of cooking rather than just the final meal, allows for a deeper engagement with the present moment, fostering contentment.

## Key Concepts
- **Non-Attachment**: The practice of letting go of desires tied to the results of actions.
- **Contentment**: A state of satisfaction that arises from within, independent of external success.
- **Karma Yoga**: The path of selfless action that leads to spiritual liberation.
- **Dharma**: The principle of duty and righteousness that guides one’s actions.
