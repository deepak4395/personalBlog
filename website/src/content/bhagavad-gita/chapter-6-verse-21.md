## Explanation

### Context
This verse, Bhagavad Gita Chapter 6, Verse 21, continues Krishna's profound discourse on *Dhyana Yoga*, the yoga of meditation and self-control, also known as *Atma Samyama Yoga*. Having previously described the ideal posture, mental discipline, and the process of withdrawing the mind from external distractions, Krishna now elaborates on the *nature* of the supreme happiness and stability attained by a yogi who successfully practices these techniques. It follows the description of the mind becoming steady and senses being controlled, leading to a profound inner experience. This verse paints a vivid picture of the ultimate reward for diligent spiritual practice: a state of unwavering bliss and truth.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
Let's break down the key terms in this powerful verse:
*   **सुखम् (sukham)**: Happiness, joy, bliss. This is not just ordinary pleasure but a deeper, more profound state.
*   **आत्यन्तिकम् (ātyantikam)**: Ultimate, absolute, supreme, final, extreme. It signifies a happiness that is not relative or temporary, but complete and enduring.
*   **यत् तत् (yat tad)**: That which. Referring to the ultimate happiness.
*   **बुद्धिग्राह्यम् (buddhi-grāhyam)**: Grasped or apprehended by the intellect (buddhi). This is a crucial distinction, as it indicates this happiness is not perceived by the senses but by a higher faculty of understanding.
*   **अतीन्द्रियम् (atīndriyam)**: Beyond the senses; transcendental. It reinforces that this bliss is not a product of sensory input or external objects.
*   **वेत्ति यत्र (vetti yatra)**: Where one knows, where one experiences.
*   **न च एव अयम् (na ca eva ayam)**: And never, indeed, this (person).
*   **स्थितः (sthitaḥ)**: Established, situated, firm.
*   **चलति (calati)**: Moves, wavers, deviates, shakes.
*   **तत्त्वतः (tattvataḥ)**: From the truth, from reality, essentially, truly.

### Deeper Understanding
Krishna here describes the pinnacle of yogic achievement: a state of supreme, unqualified happiness that is entirely internal and independent of external factors. This is not the fleeting pleasure derived from sensory objects, which inevitably leads to attachment, craving, and eventual suffering. Instead, it is a `sukham ātyantikam`, an absolute bliss.

Crucially, this bliss is `buddhi-grāhyam atīndriyam` – grasped by the intellect and beyond the senses. This means it is a realization, an understanding, a profound inner experience that transcends the limitations of the physical body and mind. Our ordinary experiences of happiness are mediated by the senses (taste, sight, touch, etc.) and the lower mind, making them transient and subject to change. The bliss described here is perceived by the *buddhi*, the higher discriminating intellect, which is capable of discerning truth (sat) from untruth (asat) and connecting with the eternal Self (Atman).

Once a yogi experiences this `atīndriya` (transcendental) state, they become `sthitaś calati tattvataḥ` – established in truth and never deviates. This signifies an unshakeable inner foundation. The individual's perception of reality shifts fundamentally. They are no longer swayed by the dualities of the world—pleasure and pain, gain and loss, honor and dishonor—because their happiness is no longer dependent on these external conditions. This state is akin to that of a `Sthitaprajna` (person of steady wisdom) described in Chapter 2, but here it emphasizes the *inner experience* that underpins such steadfastness. It is the realization of one's true, eternal nature, which is inherently blissful and unchanging. This realization liberates one from the cycle of desire and aversion, establishing them firmly in the ultimate reality, Brahman.

### Practical Application
1.  **Cultivating Inner Joy**: Instead of constantly chasing external pleasures, which are temporary, dedicate time to practices that foster inner peace and contentment. This could include meditation, mindfulness, deep contemplation, or engaging in activities that bring a sense of flow and purpose, where the 'self' dissolves, and you connect with something larger. Such practices train the `buddhi` to perceive a deeper, more lasting form of joy.
2.  **Developing Discernment (Viveka)**: Train your intellect to differentiate between fleeting sensory gratification and true, profound satisfaction. Before acting on a desire, pause and ask: "Will this truly bring lasting happiness, or is it just a temporary distraction?" This conscious discernment helps in making choices that align with your higher self and long-term well-being, rather than being driven by impulsive sensory demands.
3.  **Building Emotional Resilience**: Understanding that ultimate happiness is internal makes one less vulnerable to external ups and downs. When faced with challenges, losses, or difficult circumstances, instead of collapsing, one can draw upon an inner reservoir of peace and stability. This fosters resilience, allowing you to remain grounded and connected to your inner truth, even amidst life's inevitable storms.

## Key Concepts
1.  **Atīndriya Sukha**: Transcendental, supra-sensory happiness that is beyond the reach of the five senses.
2.  **Buddhi-grāhya Bliss**: Happiness that is apprehended and understood by the higher intellect (buddhi), indicating a state of profound realization rather than mere emotional feeling.
3.  **Steadfastness in Truth (Tattvataḥ Sthitaḥ)**: The unshakeable and unwavering state of one who has realized ultimate reality, making them immune to external fluctuations.
4.  **Inner Resilience**: The ability to remain stable, grounded, and connected to one's true nature and inner peace, irrespective of external conditions or sensory experiences.
