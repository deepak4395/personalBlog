## Explanation

### Context
This verse is situated within the broader context of Chapter 7, titled Jnana Vijnana Yoga, where Lord Krishna elucidates the nature of knowledge and the relationship between the individual soul and the Supreme. In this chapter, Krishna discusses the various types of knowledge and how they relate to the material and spiritual worlds. Here, he emphasizes the importance of faith and devotion as a means to attain ultimate truth and liberation. This verse closely ties into the teachings of the Gita which emphasize the importance of personal devotion (bhakti) alongside knowledge (jnana).

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna emphasizes the integral role of faith (śraddhā) in the spiritual journey. He states that a person who is devoted and engages in worship with faith will attain the ultimate truth. This reflects a profound teaching of the Gita: that knowledge alone is not sufficient for liberation; it must be accompanied by a heartfelt devotion.

Here, Krishna teaches Arjuna that true understanding comes from a combination of knowledge and devotion. The ultimate truth is not just intellectual knowledge but experiential wisdom that transforms one's life. This aligns with the broader themes of dharma (righteous duty) and karma (action) found throughout the Gita, where the interplay of one's actions, intentions, and faith shapes their spiritual path.

2. **Devotional Practices**: Engaging in acts of devotion, such as volunteering or helping others selflessly, can be seen as a form of worship. This not only contributes to personal growth but also to the well-being of the community.

3. **Balancing Knowledge and Faith**: In an age of information, it is essential to balance intellectual knowledge with spiritual practices. One can pursue knowledge while ensuring that it is grounded in a sense of faith and purpose, enhancing both personal and collective understanding.
