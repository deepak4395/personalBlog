## Explanation

### Context
This verse is situated within Chapter 3 of the Bhagavad Gita, which is dedicated to Karma Yoga, or the yoga of action. Here, Lord Krishna explains the importance of performing one's duty without attachment to the results. This chapter addresses Arjuna's confusion about his duty as a warrior and the moral dilemmas of fighting in the battle of Kurukshetra. Krishna emphasizes that action is necessary and that one should act according to their dharma while maintaining a balanced mindset.

### Word-by-Word Meaning
- **यस्तु (yastu)**: He who
- **कर्मफलत्यागी (karma-phala-tyāgī)**: one who renounces the fruits of action
- **सन्तुष्टः (san-tuṣṭaḥ)**: satisfied
- **स्याद्य (syād ya)**: he is
- **एव (eva)**: certainly
- **सः (saḥ)**: he
- **सर्वकर्माण्य (sarva-karmāṇi)**: all actions
- **अविस्मृत्य (avismṛtya)**: without forgetting
- **सदा (sadā)**: always
- **स्याद्युरुत्तमः (syād yur uttamaḥ)**: is indeed the best among men.

### Deeper Understanding
In this verse, Krishna conveys a profound teaching about the nature of action and its outcomes. The essence of Karma Yoga is to perform one's duties without being impacted by the results. This is a significant spiritual teaching that encourages detachment from outcomes while remaining committed to selfless action. 

Krishna is teaching Arjuna that true fulfillment comes from within, and it is not dependent on external achievements or failures. The ability to remain balanced and composed in the face of success or failure is a hallmark of a spiritual practitioner. The emphasis on 'satisfaction with the self' points to the importance of inner peace and self-realization. 

This verse encapsulates key concepts of the Gita, including dharma (duty), karma (action), and yoga (union with the divine). By engaging in action without attachment to the results, one can navigate through life’s challenges while maintaining equanimity and clarity of purpose.

### Practical Application
Modern readers can apply this teaching in various ways:
1. **Workplace Dynamics**: In a professional environment, focusing on the process rather than just the outcomes can lead to greater satisfaction and creativity. For example, an employee who focuses on doing their best work, regardless of whether they receive a promotion or recognition, experiences less stress and more fulfillment.
2. **Personal Relationships**: In personal relationships, one can practice unconditional love and support without being attached to how that love is reciprocated. This can lead to healthier, more stable relationships, where individuals feel free to express themselves without fear of judgment or failure.
3. **Personal Growth**: In personal development, setting goals should be about the journey of growth rather than a fixed destination. For instance, someone exercising for health should focus on enjoying the process of getting fit rather than fixating solely on weight loss or a specific body image.

- In what areas of your life can you practice non-attachment to outcomes?
- How might your relationships change if you approached them with a mindset of selflessness and detachment?

## Key Concepts
- **Detachment**: The importance of renouncing the fruits of action to attain peace.
- **Self-Satisfaction**: Finding contentment within oneself as opposed to external validation.
- **Equanimity**: Maintaining balance amidst the dualities of success and failure.
- **Dharma**: Understanding and fulfilling one’s duty while adhering to spiritual principles.
